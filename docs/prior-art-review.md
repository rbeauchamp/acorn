# Prior-art admission and research qualification

This is a reference for researchers reviewing the implemented algorithms.
For an introduction, read [the design](design.md#the-learning-loop) first.
**PAR** means prior-art review; each numbered entry connects a mechanism to its
source, Acorn's adaptation, implementation/proof owners and remaining limits.
Some entries describe local constructions inspired by prior art, rather than
an unchanged published algorithm. Equation-level citations live with the code
and proofs.

## Admission standard

The objective is a complete, practical integration of general learning
mechanisms for this repository's continuing setting. A paper's use of a narrow
benchmark does not make its algorithm environment-specific. Reject on an actual
incompatible dependency or assumption, with evidence of where it enters the
mechanism. Reproducing published benchmark scores is an optional diagnostic,
not an admission requirement; reproducing the mechanism and accounting for its
assumptions are required.

**The admission bar.** Five criteria must all pass: **sound** (the derivation
reproduces), **in-setting** (one unbroken stream, batch size one, no replay, no
target network, no resets, bounded work per step), **semantically compatible
and non-degenerate in composition**, **affordable** (`O(active)` work, `O(d)`
memory), and **buildable** (specified precisely enough to implement without
inventing the missing mechanism). A nonzero update is insufficient if it learns
the wrong quantity. A known incompatibility or degeneracy needs a correction
with a formal characterization and closure at the strongest applicable layer
in `AGENTS.md`; disclosure or hoped-for tuning does not clear it.

**Material adaptation contract.** Each entry identifies the source mechanism,
its state, update equations, interfaces and load-bearing assumptions. For each
material change, record:

- **Kind:** equivalent specialization, published variant, proved source
  correction, or local approximation. Name any missing mechanism explicitly;
  a new algorithm is separate scope, not an undocumented integration detail.
- **Reason:** the integration need, why this choice meets it, and why a closer
  published construction is unsuitable or more costly. A change being bounded,
  published somewhere, or already implemented is not a sufficient rationale.
- **Consequences:** the meaning of the implemented quantities and which source
  identities, assumptions and guarantees survive, change or are lost. Identify
  the current indexed type, derivation, Lean theorem or admission gate that owns each
  claimed property; distinguish real-arithmetic identities from float behavior.
- **Composition:** reconcile units, time basis, state ownership, information
  available at the update, learned targets, normalization/reference levels and
  changing policies or models across interfaces. Numerical projections need an
  algorithmic interpretation as well as a state-safety argument. A clipped
  estimate does not inherit the unconstrained algorithm's guarantees.
- **Residual uncertainty:** name what is unknown and why it is empirical or
  outside the supported theorem assumptions. Uncertain usefulness may remain;
  a known contradiction in the claimed mechanism may not. Any measurement
  follows the proof-first and prospective study-plan rules in `AGENTS.md` and the
  [scientific evidence guidance](../CONTRIBUTING.md#scientific-evidence).

Apply this contract proportionately to material choices, in the existing entry;
do not require a new dossier for ordinary integration work. An exact published
variant need not acquire a new whole-system convergence proof to be usable.
An approximation is admissible only when the five criteria still pass, its
target and rationale are coherent, and claims are limited to what its owners
establish. A label alone is not a justification.

**Every verdict carries a recorded refutation attempt** by a fresh-context
reviewer whose only instruction was to break it — what was attacked, what it
found, what survived, and what was changed as a result. An unattacked verdict is
unfinished work.

Each entry has the shape the protocol prescribes: **Claim** · **Derivation**
· **Setting** · **Cost** · **Adaptations** · **Composition** · **Verdict** · **Proof of any
correction** · **Refutation attempt**.

## Default promotion and demotion

Admission permits an explicitly selectable research integration. It does not
promote that integration into an implicit ordinary-runtime selection. An
admission verdict of **adopt** below means the mechanism satisfies its stated
technical contract; current default qualification is recorded separately.
Default selection requires the qualification decisions below.

Before promotion, record all five decisions in the relevant PAR entry:

| Requirement | Evidence required before default use |
|---|---|
| Correct and compatible | Pass admission, close known semantic defects, and name the compiler-backed owner and scope of every claimed invariant. |
| Stated benefit | Define the improvement in goal achievement, adaptation, a necessary capability, or resource efficiency, with stream conditions and horizon. |
| Convincing evidence | Prove derivable claims. Name each irreducibly empirical UNKNOWN and register its estimand, comparator, meaningful margin, uncertainty method and assumptions, population, selection history, and stopping rule before outcomes. |
| Acceptable tradeoffs | Meet declared lifetime memory, latency, stability and performance-loss limits. A benefit on one measure does not excuse a failed constraint on another. |
| Actual composition | Bind the complete source/configuration, initialization, learned state, interfaces and comparison arms. Confirm empirical promotion claims on fresh data after development and selection; identify a reproducible fallback. |

For empirical performance promotion, the appropriate uncertainty bound must
clear a predefined practically meaningful improvement, on the stated estimand.
A positive mean or a small p-value alone is insufficient. Cost or capability
promotion must establish that benefit while satisfying a predefined acceptable
performance-loss margin. Fix the numerical margins for the objective before
observing outcomes; do not invent universal percentages or retrofit margins to
an existing result. Report negative and inconclusive results and account for
multiple comparisons, selection, dependence, missing/failed runs and exclusions.
The [scientific evidence guidance](../CONTRIBUTING.md#scientific-evidence) owns full protocol, registration,
retention and reproduction requirements. Creating a protocol does not authorize
execution; the smallest informative empirical setting remains the last resort.

Uncertainty-aware evaluation is motivated by Agarwal, Schwarzer, Castro,
Courville & Bellemare, *Deep Reinforcement Learning at the Edge of the Statistical
Precipice*, NeurIPS 2021, §4.1, PDF p. 5: “reporting interval estimates”
([paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2021/file/f514cec81cb148559cf475e7426eed5e-Paper.pdf),
opened 2026-09-05). That paper studies multi-task deep-RL evaluation; it does not
validate a particular uncertainty method or sampling model for Acorn. The
promotion margins, fresh confirmation and demotion rules here are repository
policy, not attributed paper results.

| Finding | Default decision |
|---|---|
| Correctness or composition defect | Repair or disable the affected integration regardless of its measured score. |
| Credible underperformance without a previously accepted compensating benefit | Demote it and use the qualified fallback for the affected composition. |
| Missing or inconclusive promotion evidence | Keep it experimental; absence of a demonstrated loss is not promotion evidence. |
| Declared benefit and all tradeoff requirements met | Promote with an explicit decision and evidence record. |

A foundational mechanism needed for the learning discipline may carry an
explicitly accepted cost. The owner must accept the necessity, bounded cost,
evidence obligations and reconsideration condition before outcome access.
“It might help eventually” is not an exception, and an after-the-fact mission
rationale cannot convert a negative result into promotion evidence.

Qualification attaches to an integration in a composition, not to an algorithm
for all settings. Material changes to learning equations, interfaces, features,
reward/control criteria or selection rules require reconsideration. Prove
equivalence when available; otherwise disclose what evidence no longer
transfers and keep the changed mechanism experimental pending qualification.
Review the decision with a fresh-context refutation attempt. A deterministic
audit pins the chosen behavior.

### PAR-1 · SwiftTD

[Source](https://rlj.cs.umass.edu/2024/papers/RLJ_RLC_2024_111.pdf). Execution owner: `Acorn.SwiftTd`.

Adaptive sparse TD with per-feature step sizes. Projection, trace pruning and meta-register re-anchoring are declared adaptations. The contracts below describe their finite-word state and update semantics.

**Adaptation and rationale.** Binary unique ActiveSet inputs specialize the paper’s feature factors to one on the active set; sparse eligible-set iteration avoids full sweeps. The scaled trace increment is bounded by the overshoot budget, not the sum of raw step sizes. Projection admits stored weights; pruning and re-anchoring control finite-state storage but alter the unconstrained recurrence.

**Contract and composition.** AcornVerif.MetaGradient states the hand-transcribed recurrence and discrepancy identities; external transcription fidelity remains assumed. AcornVerif.CurrentLearner and AcornVerif.CurrentLearnerArithmetic link state/update contracts to the executed owner.

**Refutation attempt.** The recorded independent review challenged overshoot, sparse cost, register timing, transcription and bounds. It established that the budget concerns scaled trace increments, that the paper also uses sparse loops, and that re-anchoring withdraws the weight increment. It rejected attributing gamma-derived value bounds to log step sizes. These distinctions limit the claim here.

### PAR-2 · Swift-Sarsa

[Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.19539). Execution owner: `Acorn.Sarsa`.

On-policy per-action learners with semi-Markov credit. The declared Sarsa substitution does not implement every control variant; corrected meta-gradient and duration credit must retain their executed owners.

**Adaptation and rationale.** One persistent value-difference register and per-action learners specialize the step-size machinery to on-policy control. Semi-Markov duration catch-up is needed at meta decisions; primitive credit uses executed actions each learning step. The eligibility set, not only the active feature set, owns update cost.

**Contract and composition.** AcornVerif.CurrentControl and AcornVerif.CurrentTemporal connect to execution. AcornVerif.CurrentPower.pow_one_word supplies the one-step power reduction. Tie handling treats values within the admitted numerical band as tied. The corrected meta-register recurrence is a stated departure from the printed listing.

**Refutation attempt.** Independent review checked the chosen-action update, shared register and one-step reductions, and found the undeclared register deviation, eligible-set cost unit and approximate tie band. The corrected register discipline is the recorded source adaptation.

### PAR-3 · Horde

[Source](http://www.incompleteideas.net/papers/horde-aamas-11.pdf). Execution owner: `Acorn.Demon`.

Parallel GVF prediction enters representation. Cumulants and horizons are declared D5; recursive changing features and policies limit transfer of fixed-policy analysis. The same questions about each option's policy learn off-policy (PAR-18).

**Adaptation and rationale.** Parallel bounded GVFs specialize the broader Horde architecture; predictions feed other features to support an online representation. Fixed horizons/targets are D5. The questions about the behaviour learn on-policy; the same questions about each option's policy learn off-policy with GTD2-MP (PAR-18), in place of GQ(λ) with traces and action values. Changing predictions, policies and adaptive steps violate fixed-feature/stationary assumptions.

**Contract and composition.** AcornVerif.CurrentPrediction and Acorn.Handcrafted.PredictionControl own bounded predictions and their use. Bucket admission constrains the output range; reachability is a separate property. The policy, representation and step sizes evolve during the run.

**Refutation attempt.** Independent review challenged attribution, convergence hypotheses, target specializations and reachability. It rejected applying fixed-policy analysis with adapted steps and rejected inferring reachability from a range proof. The terms predictions as knowledge describe this implementation rather than a quoted paper theorem.

### PAR-4 · Generate and test

[Source](http://www.incompleteideas.net/papers/MS-AAAIws-2013.pdf). Execution owner: `Acorn.Features`.

Random projection generation over a hand-authored channel layout. Generation and the tester (PAR-11) are distinct mechanisms; only imprint units are replaced.

**Adaptation and rationale.** The fixed-size bank generates random projections to keep storage bounded and construction reproducible. Each unit samples 32 inputs with replacement from the 11 × 11 tile-kind patch followed by the ten task-word positions (D1), hash-binarizes them and thresholds the signed sum at zero, so one unit can conjoin task and layout, which the linear learners cannot otherwise express. Context positions carry the task words in channel order, so a position's meaning depends on the task type in the first positions; a unit separates task types when it also samples those. A replacement draws the generator's next 32 samples (Mahmood & Sutton, p. 3). This is the generator half; PAR-11 owns the tester. Candidate features still have learned output weights, so their fixed input projections do not make utility estimation impossible.

**Contract and composition.** Acorn.Features, Acorn.FeatureHistory and Acorn.FeatureLifecycle admit dimensions, unique active indices and replacement identity. Every unit's projection is determined by its recorded generator origin (`Bank.build_replace`), so the bank is rebuilt from `O(units)` stored words for any number of replacements. `taskWords_fit` proves every task relation fits the generator's context, so no task word is dropped.

**Refutation attempt.** Independent review checked generator/reset mechanics and rejected the argument that fixed random units cannot be tested because they have no learned parameters: their output weights are learned. The admitted mechanism is bounded generation; representation quality is the subject of F3.

### PAR-5 · Reward-respecting subtasks

[Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.03466). Execution owner: `Acorn.Options`.

Skills receive host reward with held stopping bonuses. Ranked hashed slots select candidate subtasks from positive learned Demon-0 weights.

**Adaptation and rationale.** Host reward remains in each skill’s cumulant. Where ranking finds a candidate, an attained feature adds its held bonus to the stopping value: the unit’s positive Demon-0 weight, held from selection and raised at each refresh to the current weight when larger, stands in for the source’s bonus weight, “one of its largest values” (section 2, equation (4), p. 8). The source replaces the feature’s current weight inside the main value estimate; Acorn adds the held weight to the meta-controller estimate. This supports reward-respecting subtasks without a manually selected subgoal list. The hashed-slot weight is a proxy, and the neutral fallback differs from the paper’s nonzero prescribed stopping value.

**Contract and composition.** Acorn.Options and Acorn.FeatureRanking own the stopping/selection definitions; AcornVerif.Options states the return identities. AcornVerif.CurrentTemporal proves that the executed attained stopping value is at least its estimate for every finite estimate within the prediction cap. Bonus and host-value coordinates must match the policy/sample owner.

**Refutation attempt.** Source review checked strict comparison, stopping equations and neutral fallback. It classified the stopping function as an adaptation rather than an exact instance of the paper at zero bonus; subsequent composition review traced the policy/sample ownership instead of inferring learned-value accuracy from return algebra.

### PAR-6 · Potential-based shaping

[Source](https://ai.stanford.edu/~ang/papers/shaping-icml99.pdf). Execution owner: `Acorn.Options`.

Finite-return coordinate identities preserve the stated terminal and bootstrap terms. Shared-stopping identities do not establish host-policy invariance with changing critics or an arbitrary stopping objective.

**Adaptation and rationale.** Potential shaping uses the source’s discount-coordinate relation. Finite activations must include actual terminal/bootstrap terms; subtracting an initial potential is insufficient to establish policy invariance when the stopping objective changes. Inverse coordinates are needed before comparing option and host estimates.

**Contract and composition.** AcornVerif.Options and the executed Acorn.Options definitions own the terminal telescoping and shared-stopping identities. These compare the stated returns under their hypotheses, not arbitrary objectives or moving critics.

**Refutation attempt.** Independent review rejected a finite-telescoping argument used as a policy-invariance proof, corrected the activation-total interval and identified the missing inverse shaping coordinate at interruption. The retained identities concern the actual terminal and common-coordinate quantities.

### PAR-7 · Options and interruption

[Source](http://www.incompleteideas.net/papers/SPS-aij.pdf). Execution owner: `Acorn.Temporal`.

Typed option lifecycle and semi-Markov updates retain actual accumulated reward and duration. Shared units and strict advantage govern interruption by value; a persistent run that an option's own draw begins also interrupts it and closes its meta span there (PAR-8).

**Adaptation and rationale.** Option-selecting updates accumulate actual reward and duration at decision boundaries. Trace catch-up is a local extension that decays registers without intermediate increments; it is not attributed wholesale to the options theorem. Primitive control uses PAR-9 credit rather than a second catch-up update.

**Contract and composition.** Acorn.Temporal, Acorn.Handcrafted.AgentEpisodes and AcornVerif.CurrentTemporal own lifecycle/time-base contracts. Acorn.Features.CreditGap supplies register decay identities. Strict interruption compares compatible quantities; inaccurate critics do not gain a policy-improvement guarantee.

**Refutation attempt.** Independent review attacked k-step trace decay and found that the stated no-increment decay composes mathematically. It identified the Dutch-register time-base mismatch and interruption quantity substitution, and separated the local trace extension from the source’s semi-Markov backup.

### PAR-8 · Temporally extended exploration

[Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.01782v1): Dabney, Ostrovski & Barreto, *Temporally-Extended ε-Greedy Exploration*, ICLR 2021, arXiv:2006.01782v1, Algorithm 1 (p. 14), Appendix A (pp. 13–14), Appendix B.3 (p. 15) and Table 2 (p. 16). Execution owner: `Acorn.Exploration`.

Persistent exploration has a bounded duration. The local capped duration law is D3 and the rate ε is D6's declared 0.01. That is the source's CartPole setting (Appendix A, p. 14); its Rainbow-based Atari agents reach 0.01 only at the end of "a linear decay schedule 1.0 to 0.01 over the course of the first 4M frames" (Appendix B.3, p. 15; Table 2, p. 16). Support and useful goal discovery are separate obligations from probability admission.

**Adaptation and rationale.** Persistent action runs follow a capped floor-inverse-uniform duration law. It is a tail-equivalent surrogate for the published zeta law, chosen as the explicit local D3 construction; it is not exact source sampling. Serving a run is deterministic, so a universal positive support floor is unavailable at those steps. One further adaptation is declared in D6. The rate is the constant alone, without the Atari agents' decay: counting one step as one frame, that schedule still gives ε ≈ 0.99 after the 34 000 steps a first pass of the standard curriculum can take, so a whole first pass lies inside the removed phase.

The persistent draw applies to the behaviour, as the source's Algorithm 1 (p. 14) applies it to the agent's one behaviour policy: at every step with no run in progress it starts a run with probability ε, and otherwise takes the greedy action. Primitive control (`TemporalControl.choosePrimitive`) and an executing option (`TemporalControl.stepOption`) both draw with `PolicySnapshot.drawPersistent`, so every decision that serves no run makes exactly one persistent draw, by the layer that selects the primitive action. The meta-controller's draw is over meta actions, selects that layer and stays a single step (`PolicySnapshot.draw`). The source has one value function and no hierarchy, so what a run does to an executing option is Acorn's choice, declared in D3: the run interrupts the option. §4.2 (p. 5) defines the run as "the option which takes action a for n steps and then terminates", and Acorn's dispatch executes one occupancy at a time. The option is the executing invocation of the frame it drew, where the run's first action is a sample of the option's own nominal distribution and is credited on-policy. The run's first served step ends that execution, recorded as an interruption, and the meta-controller decides again when the run is spent. A drawn run with no step left to serve is one exploratory step of the option, which continues, so at a unit cap the behaviour is the single-step one. The run gives the interrupted option no terminal credit, because the run is not one of its stopping conditions; the option is linked to the trajectory it was executing and learns from the served steps off-policy, under its own stopping decision ([PAR-17](#par-17--off-policy-option-learning)). The run takes control before the first served step's goal and duration checks, so the interruption is the recorded reason even where one of them would have fired.

**Limit.** A run cuts short the option whose draw began it. Under an assumed uniform, independent draw that the deterministic generator does not supply, a draw begins a run with a step left to serve with probability ε/2 (`ez_duration_tail` at n = 2), so an option that would otherwise return k actions is not interrupted with probability (1 − ε/2)^k: about 0.53 at the 128-action cap and D6's rate. Under the same assumption the expected exploratory share of the behaviour's cycles is between 4.6% and 6% (`declared_share_gt`, `declared_share_lt`), against ε for a single-step draw (`explorationShare_single`). The share an executed stream shows, and the effect of either quantity on achievement, are UNKNOWN: neither is derivable from the definitions, and no achievement claim is made. An interrupted option is credited by one contract in both learners of its meta value: the option run to its own stop. At the first served step its model takes the transition into that step: the executing option's continuing credit where the option's own stopping decision continues (`handoff_model`), and the close toward its terminal targets where that decision ends there (`handoff_stop`). Where it continues, the trajectory then lapses without a terminal target, because a served frame reports a point mass, which is not consistent with the masses of an option that gives another action a nonzero mass (`follow_live`, `served_unarmed`), and the model credits no served step (`follow_idle_model`). The run therefore gives the model no target of its own: its learned target is still the option run to its own stop, and planning ([PAR-14](#par-14--background-planning)) backs the meta value toward that prediction. The meta-controller's sampled span therefore closes at the first served step and covers the option's own actions only: it is credited toward the option's own meta value at that frame where the option's stopping decision continues, and toward the frame's nominal meta value where it ends, by the same stopping decision the option's learners take there (`takeover_value`); every meta trace is then released, so the rewards of the served steps are credited to no meta action (`close_step_idle`). With an exact continuing value, a span closed there has the return of the uninterrupted span (`interrupted_span_return`, over exact reals); the executed credit bootstraps from the learned meta value, which for an option partway through its duration is its value at a fresh start, the same age approximation PAR-17 declares. The span of primitive control still includes a run primitive control began; no model or planning target exists for that action. The deferred span cannot saturate its byte: an option returns at most 128 actions and a run serves at most 127 steps (`stored_clocks`), and 127 + 127 is below 255 (`meta_span_fits`); the bound on the stored span itself is argued from those two facts, not machine-checked.

**Contract and composition.** Acorn.Exploration and AcornVerif.Exploration own the actual duration/range definitions and stated mathematical distribution scope. The duration and normalization theorems state their action-set and distribution assumptions. Every executed branch compares the first source word's 53-bit fraction with the rate as exact rational order; at D6's rate exactly its binary32 value's share of first words explores (AcornVerif.TemporalSupport). `CurrentTemporal.select_persistent` proves the reach over the executed selection, for every state and generator word: each decision is a served step of the committed run the given state holds, or its action, branch, values, masses, committed run and outgoing generator state are those of one persistent draw by the layer its source names, primitive control or an option, from that layer's own frozen values and rate. The draw's generator state is bound to the state selection was given: it is that state's own when no meta decision is drawn, and the one a single plain meta draw from it leaves otherwise. The learners the draw reads are those of the state selection holds when it draws; how they follow from the given state's is not part of the statement. `TemporalSupport.select_declared` proves that under the declared policy the draw explores exactly on D6's first-word numerators of that generator state, and `TemporalSupport.declared_direct` that a decision which draws no meta decision explores exactly on those numerators of the incoming generator state, so a uniform first-word hypothesis on the incoming state gives the branch D6's exact mass there. For a decision that draws a meta decision the same holds of the first word after that draw, which needs the model's independence assumption. `CurrentTemporal.persistent_draw` gives what an exploring draw commits, `durationRemaining_eq_spec` its capped reciprocal form, and `CurrentTemporal.serve_run` that each served step stores the run with its clock one lower. `CurrentTemporal.serve_interrupts`, `handoff_model`, `handoff_policy`, `handoff_age` and `handoff_stop` state what an interrupted option's learners write at the first served step. Where its own stopping decision continues: the model credit the executing option would have taken, tree-backup credit for the policy, and an age that keeps counting. Where it ends: the stop every followed option takes. `CurrentTemporal.takeover_value`, `closeSpan_controller` and `CurrentControl.close_step_idle` state the meta-controller's closing credit: its continuation, its error and that nothing stays eligible after it. That the next meta credit reaches no earlier action also relies on planning leaving every trace as it found it (`CurrentLearner.plan_transient`); the composition over the intervening operations is argued, not one theorem. Under a uniform duration draw the mean run length is H₁₂₈ and the expected exploratory share of the behaviour's cycles is between 4.6% and 6% (AcornVerif.Exploration). The duration and share statements assume uniform, independent draws that the deterministic generator does not supply; the duration model omits the executed reciprocal's binary64 rounding.

**Refutation attempt.** Independent review checked the inverse-uniform off-by-one and normalization argument, and rejected exact-zeta, implicit-cap and overbroad action-domain wording. Independent review of the behaviour-wide draw attacked the reach statement, the claim that an interrupted option takes no terminal credit and the composition with planning. It found the reach statement's rate clause vacuous for a learner-derived rate, which the statement now closes by naming the drawing layer's own frozen policy; that the option's own stopping decision still applies at the first served step, which `handoff_stop` states; and that planning and sampled meta credit aimed at different returns on the interrupted fraction. A second review found the reach statement unbound from the incoming generator state, so that no hypothesis on the incoming draw transferred to it, and rejected declaring the divergence of the two meta targets as a limit. The statement now binds the draw's generator state to the given state, and the meta span closes at the interruption under the option's own contract.

### PAR-9 · Intra-option value learning

[Source](http://www.incompleteideas.net/papers/SPS-aij.pdf). Execution owner: `Acorn.Handcrafted.TemporalControl`.

Primitive action learners receive the executed stream, including option steps. This is an on-policy Sarsa specialization with actual terminal/span semantics; it is not the full off-policy intra-option algorithm.

**Adaptation and rationale.** On-policy Sarsa credit updates the primitive learner from each executed learning-step action, including option execution. This uses otherwise discarded experience without replay; it is a local specialization rather than the source’s full off-policy option-augmented Q-learning algorithm. Frozen control excludes updates explicitly.

**Contract and composition.** Acorn.Handcrafted.TemporalControl and AcornVerif.CurrentControl bind credit to the actual action/terminal semantics. AcornVerif.CurrentPower.pow_one_word closes the one-step duration reduction. Greedification with respect to an estimate is not policy improvement with respect to the true value function.

**Refutation attempt.** Independent review traced all credit branches for dropped rewards, wrong actions and duplicate updates. It rejected a source dangling equation reference, a theorem-shaped improvement claim and omission of the option-augmented maximization domain. The one-step power reduction has a universal implementation-linked owner.

### PAR-10 · Derived exploration rate

[Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.01782). Execution owner: `Acorn.Policy`.

The rate is derived from active optimizer state; duration remains D3. No research profile uses it: they use D6's declared rate or the annealed comparison. The native `own` and `shared` rate words still select it. Empty eligibility, optimizer coupling, support and resource limits are explicit.

**Limit.** The rate is the eligible mean of ν(β) = (β − ln η_min)/(ln η − ln η_min). At the initial step sizes it is about 0.63 for primitive and meta learners and 0.64 for options, and every reinstalled option returns to it. The cited paper gives no rate formula; it declares the rate for each experiment, as a constant or, for its Rainbow-based Atari agents, a decay schedule (D6).

**Adaptation and rationale.** This is a local construction, not a rate formula taken from the cited duration paper. It derives a receiving controller’s probability from active optimizer state to remove a clock schedule without adding a separate intrinsic-reward channel or replay memory. It remains coupled to optimizer parameters; no universal exploration floor or independent tuning claim follows.

**Contract and composition.** Acorn.Policy's `Controller.exploreRate` aggregates the eligible mean of ν over each learner's configuration-indexed rails, and `SwiftTd.ExploreRate.project` admits the rounded probability; Acorn.Exploration only consumes the admitted rate. Empty eligible sets use the declared fallback after transient clearing. Meta rate reads do not occur on every world step, so their cost cannot be amortized as if they did; under off-policy option learning (PAR-17) each option that is not executing reads its rate on every step, and the start that follows one of its stops reads the empty-eligibility fallback. Duration remains D3.

**Refutation attempt.** Independent review rejected comparison against a nonexistent universal support guarantee: deterministic served exploration already lacks it, and removing an annealed floor broadens the limitation. It located the range obligation in the receiving configuration and final rounded mean, not merely individual log step sizes.

### PAR-11 · Generate-and-test tester

[Source](http://www.incompleteideas.net/papers/MS-AAAIws-2013.pdf); [continual backpropagation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13812v3). Execution owner: `Acorn.FeatureLifecycle`.

Every imprint unit carries a contribution utility. Each step one credit accrues per eligible unit, and every 1/ρ credits replace the least useful eligible unit, so turnover follows from the rate by construction. A replaced unit enters with zero outgoing weight in every reader. The rate, maturity threshold and decay are D7.

**Adaptation and rationale.** Mahmood & Sutton's tester "replaces a small fraction ρ of the features that are least useful" and selects only among features whose age has reached the maturity threshold (p. 3). The utility is the contribution utility of Dohare, Hernandez-Garcia, Rahman, Mahmood & Sutton, *Maintaining Plasticity in Deep Continual Learning*, arXiv:2306.13812v3 (2024), eq. (2), §6, p. 19 (published in revised form as *Loss of plasticity in deep continual learning*, Nature 632 (2024)): a running average, with decay η, of |h|·Σₖ|wₖ|, where h ∈ {0, 1} is the unit's own output on the frame and the sum runs over every physically stored learner's weight at the unit's slot (57 learners under discounted control, 60 under differential control; the discounted models' aliased duration position adds no connection). A unit is eligible when its age exceeds m (Algorithm 1, p. 23); Mahmood & Sutton's `ai ≥ µ` admits a unit one step earlier. Ties go to the lower bank index. A unit held as an option objective is eligible only at a free boundary, so no live option loses its objective. The new unit's projection is the generator's next draw, its log step size returns to the initial rail and its per-slot traces clear through SwiftTD's retirement entry. As in the source, which resets only the replaced unit (Algorithm 1, p. 23), every learner keeps its shared previous prediction and weight-change aggregate. Material adaptations:

- *Published variant, rate accrual.* Algorithms 1 and 3 (pp. 23, 44) reinitialize n_l·ρ eligible units per step, with n_l the layer size, and Mahmood & Sutton select ρn of all n features. Acorn follows the authors' released implementation (github.com/shibhansh/loss-of-plasticity, `lop/algos/gnt.py`, function test_features, commit a6b7958), which accrues ρ per *eligible* unit and spends whole accrued units. No credit accrues while nothing is eligible, so the first maturity period ends without a burst of replacements. Turnover by construction therefore holds over eligible units: `run_turnover` gives a replacement within ⌈period/k⌉ tests whenever at least k units are eligible at each. Issue 9's wording over mature units does not hold: away from a free boundary a held unit is mature but not eligible and accrues no credit, so from zero credit, 500 mature units of which 3 are held accrue 9 940 < 10⁴ credits over the ⌈10⁴/500⌉ = 20 tests that wording allows during a live option. Each of the three slots holds at most one unit (`heldCount_le`), so `run_mature_turnover` gives a replacement within ⌈period/(k − 3)⌉ tests whenever at least k > 3 units are mature at each. The two rules agree when every unit is eligible; at the declared settings, once m + 1 steps have passed, at most 52 of 512 units are immature (`run_immature`), so the accrued rate is at least about 0.9 of Algorithm 1's. The credit is an exact integer with period 1/ρ = 10⁴.
- *Equivalent specialization, adaptation utility.* Eqs. (6)–(7) divide by the sum of input-weight magnitudes. Each sample's hash includes its sample index, so every unit has exactly 32 inputs of weight ±1; the divisor is the constant 32 and the utility order is unchanged, so it is omitted. Summing rather than averaging over consumers is also order-preserving.
- *Declared variant, utility form.* Acorn uses eq. (2) rather than the mean-corrected eqs. (3)–(5). The mean correction assumes a consumer bias that absorbs the unit's mean contribution, which the source also transfers into that bias on removal (p. 20); Acorn's linear readers have no bias input, and adding one would change learner semantics. The source's Appendix D reports plain contribution utility as a weaker ablation than the full measure, and the D7 settings come from Algorithm 3, which uses eq. (5). The sum over learners mixes value scales (horizons 10 to 100).
- *Bounded omission, bias correction.* The source divides its running utility by 1 − η^age (eq. (8), p. 21). For every eligible unit that factor lies within 5·10⁻⁵ of one (`maturity_bias`), so the omission can change only which of two utilities within that relative distance is smaller.
- *Composition, hashed slots.* A unit's outgoing weights are the readers' weights at its hashed slot, which a tile-word feature or another unit may share. Zeroing the slot also erases their contribution, and the utility credits the unit with the whole slot weight even when an alias is active in the same frame.
- *Composition, transition rows.* The rows of an option's expectation model (PAR-13) hold weights at ranked positions, not at feature slots. They are not counted in the utility. Replacing a ranked unit vacates its position: its row starts fresh and every other row forgets its weight from that position, and the slot is erased from planning's stored recent feature vectors (PAR-14).

Storage is every unit's generator origin, birth clock and utility, so it is `O(units)` for any number of replacements.

**Contract and composition.** Acorn.FeatureLifecycle owns the tester. `Lifecycle.test_accrual`/`test_balance` prove each test replaces exactly ⌊(c + n)/period⌋ units, where c is the stored credit and n the eligible count, and keeps the remainder; `candidate_least` proves each replaced unit has the least stored utility among eligible units; `test_unheld` proves no held unit is replaced away from a free boundary. AcornVerif.Retirement composes `Lifecycle.advance`, any learner update and `Lifecycle.test` as the agent does: `run_replaced` gives exactly ⌊(c₀ + Σₜ nₜ)/period⌋ replacements over any run, `run_turnover` a replacement within ⌈period/k⌉ tests whenever k units are eligible, `run_mature_turnover` one within ⌈period/(k − 3)⌉ tests whenever k > 3 units are mature, and `run_young`/`run_immature` at most ⌊(period − 1 + L·N)/period⌋ units younger than L steps. AcornVerif.CurrentRetirement proves the local safety of `Lifecycle.replace`: every reader's weight at the replaced slot is positive zero (`replace_zero`), every reader's prediction on an input without the slot is unchanged bit for bit (`replace_predictions`), and adding the new unit's term preserves the numerical value of every finite partial sum under the pinned float model (`replace_neutral`). Every reader keeps its shared previous prediction and weight-change aggregate (`replace_aggregates`, `controller_retire_values`), so each learner's next TD error after a replacement is its unreplaced error formula with only the retired slot's weight replaced by positive zero (`retire_step_error`), and is unchanged bit for bit on inputs without the slot (`retire_step_error_absent`); the controllers' shared error `r + γ·q(a) − vOld` differs only through the same weight. These establish nothing about inputs that contain the slot, where the old unit's and any alias's contribution is removed, nor about cached temporal predictions, which are retained without recomputation. The linkage to `Agent.act` is structural: learners write only the consumer ensemble. Per-step work is `O(N)` utility decay, `O(active units × stored learners)` outgoing sums and an `O(N)` scan when a replacement is due; the encoder already evaluates every unit each step.

**Residual uncertainty.** Whether turnover at the declared rate improves the agent is UNKNOWN: it depends on the stream and on how quickly useful conjunctions are found, which no derivation from the tester's own state settles (F3).

**Refutation attempt.** A fresh-context review attacked citation fidelity, the adaptation kinds, the rate law, utility aggregation, local safety, the held-unit rule and the generator input. Page numbers, quotes and parameter values, the constant adaptation divisor, the bias-correction arithmetic, the learner count and the held-unit rule survived. It found the arXiv locators attributed to the Nature title, the eligible-count accrual attributed to the paper rather than the released code, and undeclared utility-form, aliasing and maturity-boundary differences; each is now cited or declared above. It also found that SwiftTD's retirement entry cleared every reader's previous prediction and weight-change aggregate, which would have perturbed every learner's next TD error at each replacement; the entry now keeps both, and `retire_step_error` characterizes the next error.

### PAR-12 · Ranked learned subtasks

[Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.03466). Execution owner: `Acorn.FeatureRanking`.

Streaming ranking over GVF weight slots drives the assignment refresh at every free decision boundary. Unit identity decides which knowledge is invalidated; a unit that stays ranked keeps its slot and learned state, and its held bonus never decreases. Hash collisions and proxy meaning limit interpretation; spatial comparison remains D2.

**Adaptation and rationale.** Only units whose signed Demon-0 weight is positive are candidates; the positive weight word is both the ranking score and the attainment bonus, so a feature predicting less reward is never pursued as an attainment target. Ranking chooses distinct tied blocks. The bonus is held from selection, as the source sets a bonus weight “so that an option can be learned in preparation for the times at which it is high” (section 2, p. 8). While the unit stays ranked, each refresh raises the held bonus to the current weight when that is larger and never lowers it; this running maximum of the weights seen at free boundaries is the streaming form of the source’s “one of its largest values” (p. 8). Objective identity is the unit alone. A refresh keeps the policy, model, cached prediction and meta-controller row of the first slot holding each still-ranked unit; every other slot takes the next entrant in slot order or the neutral objective and is reinstalled when its unit changes, so a repeated unit keeps one slot. A held unit retires only at a free boundary, where every slot holding it is released to the neutral objective with fresh policy, model and meta-controller row, and the next free boundary installs an entrant; a live option keeps its objective, and its terminal credit is never written into a replacement. No two slots hold the same unit; checkpoint admission refuses an image whose slots repeat one. The refresh runs at every free dispatch, which is each decision that draws a meta decision: in a hierarchical profile, every decision that serves no committed exploration run and continues no option. It reads no attempt or curriculum event: the Alberta Plan’s meta-algorithms for constructing subtasks “operate on every time step” (Sutton, Bowling and Pilarski, 2023, p. 2), and a refresh requested only by an achieved attempt or a new curriculum cycle is consumed before the first reward is learned, when every Demon-0 weight is still zero. Selection runs before the reward delivered with a decision is learned, so the refresh at a decision reads the Demon-0 weights as the previous step left them, and a candidate that a reward creates is installed at the first free dispatch after the decision that delivered it. After each refresh the slots hold exactly the units of the ranked candidates: one slot per candidate, so as many slots hold a unit as the ranking has candidates, up to three. A slot is reinstalled at the first free dispatch at which its unit has left the three ranked score blocks; how often a unit near the third place leaves and re-enters as the weights move is UNKNOWN, since it depends on the experience stream. A primitive-only profile has no option to pursue a subtask, draws no meta decision and assigns none. Installing only at a free dispatch preserves an ending activation’s objective. The rationale is bounded streaming discovery without an authored subgoal list. Feature variability is not tested; the source constructs subtasks only for features whose weight is sometimes high and sometimes low. The same ranking, continued from three score blocks to one fewer than the ranked width, decides which feature slots each option’s expectation model reads and predicts (PAR-13); every free boundary installs it in every model.

**Contract and composition.** Acorn.FeatureRanking and Acorn.FeatureRefresh own selection/refresh and identity; Acorn.FeatureReferences owns release at replacement. `FreeDispatch.refresh_retains` proves, for every state and Demon-0 weight array, that the assignment refresh leaves the first slot holding a still-ranked unit its policy, model, cached prediction and meta-controller row unchanged and that its held bonus never decreases; `FreeDispatch.refreshModels_retains` and `FreeDispatch.refreshModels_model` prove the same of the complete refresh, whose only write to that slot's model is the current feature ranking, which keeps the reward and continuation learners and the weights of every slot that stays ranked; `FreeDispatch.refresh_distinct` proves that after a refresh no two slots hold the same unit, for every held input; `FreeDispatch.refresh_covers` and `FreeDispatch.refresh_ranked` prove that after a refresh the units the slots hold are exactly those of the ranked candidates, `FreeDispatch.refresh_occupancy` that as many slots hold a unit as the ranking has candidates, and `FreeDispatch.refresh_full` that every slot holds a unit once the ranking has a candidate for each; over the executed selection, `TemporalControl.select_assigns` proves that every decision that draws a meta decision returns a state whose objectives are the ranking’s assignment for the Demon-0 weights and objectives the decision started from, with `TemporalControl.select_covers`, `TemporalControl.select_occupancy` and `TemporalControl.select_full` as its consequences, `TemporalControl.select_drawn` proves that a hierarchical agent draws a meta decision whenever it serves no exploration run and no option is active, and `TemporalControl.primitive_undrawn` that a primitive-only profile draws none; `Agent.attempt_continuity` proves that an attempt’s outcome and cycle change no learner state; the full agent’s `TemporalControl.Aligned` carries distinct held units through initialization, every step, retirement and restoration, so `FeatureImage` stores that witness and every typed image round-trips; `FeatureRuntime.retire_releases` proves that after retirement no held assignment names the replaced unit, and `FeatureRuntime.retire_occupied` that away from a free boundary retirement releases nothing; `Bonus` makes every selected bonus positive and finite at every construction and admission boundary. The checkpoint stores the held bonus word and no refresh request; format 18 refuses earlier generations. Nonpositive scores yield neutral skills rather than arbitrary noise.

**Refutation attempt.** Independent review challenged tied candidates, bonus-only changes, early-stream zeros and stopping invariance. It retained block exclusivity and neutral fallback and limited shaping invariance to trajectories with the same stopping value. Invalidating on a bonus-only change would reset a retained unit’s option policy, model and meta-controller row at nearly every refresh, because the Demon-0 weight moves whenever the unit’s trace is active; identity is therefore the unit, and the bonus is held. A bonus held only at its selection-time weight would stay at an early, typically small value; raising it at refresh changes no learned state. Generate-and-test replaces a retired unit’s projection at the same index, so retirement releases its slots rather than letting the replacement inherit learned state. A diagnostic trace refuted an earlier cadence, in which a refresh ran only when an achieved attempt or a new curriculum cycle had requested one: the first request was consumed at the decision before the first reward was learned, and the three slots held no unit from step 1 to step 31 000 of seed 16265277883658242538 of study first-pass-vs-chance r1, and until step 423 of seed 8789851314873071931, as observed and recorded in [#57](https://github.com/rbeauchamp/acorn/issues/57). The refresh therefore runs at every free dispatch.

### PAR-13 · Option expectation models

[Reward-respecting subtasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.03466); [planning with expectation models](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.01191); [planning with expectation models for control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08543); [average-reward options](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.13855). Execution owners: `Acorn.Models`, `Acorn.RankedFeatures`.

Each option has an expectation model. Its reward part predicts the reward accumulated while the option runs. Its transition part predicts, for each feature slot of a small ranked subset, the discounted probability that the slot is active when the option stops. For the share of the outcome's value that the ranked slots do not carry, a shared residual part and one small deviation part per meta action predict each action's share. Differential control keeps a duration part.

**Adaptation and rationale.** Sutton, Machado, Holland, Szepesvari, Timbers, Tanner & White define the reward part r̂(x, o) and the transition part n̂(x, o) ≈ E[γᴷ x(S_K)] of an approximate option model, take both linear, and learn each row of the transition part by TD with cumulant zero and the terminal frame's feature x_j as stopping value (arXiv:2202.03466v4, §4, eqs. (14)–(17), pp. 13–14). Wan, Abbas, White, White & Sutton call such a transition part an expectation model (*Planning with Expectation Models*, IJCAI 2019, arXiv:1904.01191v4).

- *Published rule with a corrected terminal target, rows.* Every row is a SwiftTD prediction learner with cumulant zero whose terminal target is γ times its own slot's indicator at the terminal frame (`Transition.step`, `Transition.terminal`). The source's eq. (17) passes the unscaled x_j to the TD error of eq. (5), which makes a K-step prediction γᴷ⁻¹x_j; eq. (15) defines the transition part with γᴷ. Acorn targets eq. (15), and `option_model_terminal_discount_correction` gives the difference from eq. (17) for every input: (γ − 1)·β·x_j, zero when γ = 1 or the transition is not terminal.
- *Local approximation, ranked subset.* The source's transition part is a d × d matrix; with 16 384 slots that is about 2.7 × 10⁸ weights per option. The Alberta Plan asks for "a ranking of features used both for feature finding and to determine which features are included in the environment model" (arXiv:2208.11173v3, Step 8(d), p. 9). Acorn models the slots of the ranked score blocks of positive Demon-0 weight, the ranking of PAR-12 continued from three entries to k, and each row reads only those slots, so a transition part holds k² weights. The width follows from a memory budget, not from tuning: k is the largest power of two for which the transition parts of all three options together, 3k² weights, fit in one weight vector, which gives k = 64 for 16 384 slots (`rankWidth_budget`, `rankWidth_maximal`, `default_width`). The last position is a constant input (next item), so 63 slots are ranked. Rows over all d slots as input would cost k·d weights per option, 192 weight vectors in all against 57 for every other learner together.
- *Local construction, constant input.* A frame that activates no ranked slot would give every row the empty input and the prediction zero, whatever the option does from there. The last position is therefore reserved: it holds no slot in any ranking, by a field of the ranking's type (`RankedFeatures.bias_vacant`), is active in every input (`RankedFeatures.input_membership`) and is never a predicted outcome (`bias_unoccupied`), so each row has a bias weight. The source's features include no such input; with one-hot features it needs none.
- *Published algorithm applied per row, step sizes.* SwiftTD adapts one step size per weight of one scalar prediction. A transition part is k scalar predictions over one input, and their squared errors add, so each row is an ordinary SwiftTD learner over the ranked positions. Every weight has its own step size and each row keeps its own step-size budget; no matrix-valued adaptation rule is introduced.
- *Local construction, residual per action.* The value function reads every active feature, and a frame activates on the order of a thousand slots, so the value of an outcome read from 63 slots alone would understate it by construction and planning would pull every option's value toward that understatement. For each meta action the remainder is that action's value at the outcome's unranked slots, its unranked share: its value at the terminal frame minus its value at that frame's ranked slots, under the meta-controller as it stands at that frame. The share is learned in two parts whose sum is the share exactly, in real arithmetic and for any shared part (`outcome_decomposition`). The shared residual is the terminal frame's nominal value minus the nominal value of its ranked slots alone; the full-width continuation learner predicts it (`Transition.residual`). The deviation of an action is its share minus the shared residual; one learner per meta action predicts it from the ranked positions and the bias (`Transition.outcome`). Under discounted control, where the nominal value is the maximum, the shared residual is the share of the greedy action whenever the ranked values and the complete values are maximal at the same action, and the deviation of that action is then zero in exact arithmetic. Under differential control the shared residual is a difference of two policy means and no action's deviation need vanish.
- *Local construction, combination before the maximum.* The value of the predicted outcome for one meta action is the current value weights applied to the predicted ranked slots, plus the predicted shared residual, plus that action's predicted deviation (`Transition.outcomeValues`); the maximum or mean over actions is taken after. Between two sets of value weights the exact per-action value, the sum of the stored words before rounding, moves by exactly the weight change at each occupied slot times that slot's predicted activity (`outcome_value_propagation`), and it reads no other value weight (`ranked_values_congr`); no new termination of the option is needed. The executed word is within the rounding allowance of `outcome_value_rounding` of that exact value, so a weight change smaller than the binary32 spacing at the word's magnitude can leave the word as it was. With one scalar residual added after the maximum, a change at a ranked slot would be hidden whenever the ranked values and the complete values are maximal at different actions; `deterministic_outcome` shows, in exact arithmetic, that the per-action form has no such case for a deterministic outcome. A predecessor's own action value moves when planning next backs it up, by its step size; a change in an unranked slot's weight reaches the backed-up value only through new terminations ([#48](https://github.com/rbeauchamp/acorn/issues/48)).
- *Declared approximation, deviations.* The differences between actions' unranked shares are predicted from the ranked positions and a bias, 63 slots of about a thousand active ones, and not from the full feature vector. How accurate that is, is UNKNOWN. A full-width learner per meta action would predict each share from every feature at the cost of nine more full-width learners; that alternative is [#47](https://github.com/rbeauchamp/acorn/issues/47). The two designs are not equivalent. With no ranked slot the look-ahead reads no value weight (`ranked_values_vacant`) and each action's value is the shared residual plus a deviation predicted from the bias alone.
- *Declared difference, action values.* Wan et al. show that with a value function linear in the features an expectation model loses nothing against a distribution model, and add that "if we are using an expectation model, then the approximate value function should be linear" (§4, p. 3). Acorn's value function is the nominal value of four linear action values (PAR-2). Each action value of the predicted slots is exactly linear in them (`expectation_linear`); under discounted control the nominal value is their maximum, and the maximum of expected values is at most the expected maximum, with equality when one action is maximal at every outcome (`expectation_maximum_le`, `expectation_maximum_eq`). For an exact expectation model the look-ahead therefore never exceeds the distribution-model backup of the same weights; these are statements in exact arithmetic about the expected features, not about the learned rows. Under differential control the nominal value is the mean under the nominal policy, whose greedy part averages every action within a tie window of the maximum. The tie set depends on the values, so that mean is not convex and no Jensen inequality holds for it. `expected_sandwich` proves, for the executed definition, that it lies between (1 − ε)·max + ε·mean less the tie window and rounding, and that convex combination plus rounding; the executed word can exceed the convex combination by the rounding. `expectation_sandwiched` carries both allowances through the inequality of the discounted case, and `expected_expectation` instantiates it with the executed mean: the mean of expected action values is at most the expected mean plus the tie window and both roundings.
- *Local construction, terminal value.* The shared residual and every share are differences of two readings of one value function at one frame, so both read the same weights and rate: the meta-controller as it stands when the model closes. The policy's own terminal credit takes the policy's terminal word, which under differential control is the value of the next sampled meta action; the model does not.
- *Local construction, change of subset.* Every free decision boundary installs the current ranking in every model (`FreeDispatch.refreshModels`), after the subtask assignments of PAR-12, which the same boundary refreshes when the subtasks are learned. A slot that stays ranked keeps its position, its row and that row's weights from every other retained slot and from the bias (`rerank_retained`, `rerank_weights`, `rerank_bias`); a position whose slot left the ranking takes the next entrant or stays vacant, its row starts fresh and every other row forgets its weight from that position through SwiftTD's retirement entry (`rerank_ranked`, `forget_row`, `forget_column`). Retiring a ranked unit vacates every position that holds its slot in the same way, so a replacement unit at that slot inherits nothing (`retire_vacated`). A model holds a slot at one position only: the ranking's type carries it (`RankedFeatures.slot_unique`), installing a ranking requires distinct slots, which the ranking supplies (`rankedSlots_nodup`), and every construction keeps it. A row's terminal target is therefore γ times its own slot's activity at the terminal frame (`indicator_exact`). The deviation learners read the same positions and forget a changed position in the same way (`rerank_deviations`). Only occupied positions and the deviation learners are updated. A slot near the last ranked place can leave and re-enter as the weights move, and its row starts fresh each time; how often that happens is UNKNOWN. The shared residual learner is not reset at a change of subset: until it relearns against the new subset, the backed-up value counts an entrant's share of the value twice as that slot's row learns, and omits a departed slot's share. Each error is at most the slot's value weight times its predicted activity ([#48](https://github.com/rbeauchamp/acorn/issues/48)).
- *Declared limit, linear model.* Wan et al. show that planning with the best linear expectation model does not in general reach the solution learning from real data reaches (§5.1, Proposition 5.1). They conjecture that the condition under which TD(0) planning with a linear model converges "might not hold in practice causing the planning to diverge" (§6) and show divergence on Baird's counterexample (§7.1). The source uses a linear model (eq. (16)) and so does Acorn; neither result is discharged here.
- *Declared limit, age.* The rows read ranked slots, which carry no age. The reward, residual and duration learners keep the age-augmented input under differential control.

**Contract and composition.** Acorn.Models owns the parts and their targets; Acorn.RankedFeatures the subset, its width and its position-stable merge; AcornVerif.CurrentPlanning the contracts.

- *Backup identity.* `ranked_value_rounding` proves, for every value function, ranking and predicted slot vector with at most 64 ranked positions, that each executed action value at the ranked slots is finite and within 2⁻¹⁷ + 2⁻¹¹ per occupied position of the exact dot product Σⱼ w[slot j]·n̂ⱼ of the stored words. The ranked value, the shared residual and the deviation are then accumulated in binary64 and the total is narrowed once to the word the comparator reads (`Transition.outcomeValues`). The rounding therefore follows the magnitude of the total and not of its parts: a shared residual and a deviation that cancel lose nothing of the ranked part. `wide_total` proves the binary64 total within 2⁻¹⁶ of the exact sum for every learner state, because every stored weight lies in its rule's domain (`prediction_bound`). No projection is applied to the shared or deviation predictions. The residual is the difference between a value and a sum of up to 64 signed weights, so it is signed and has no value range; a range wide enough never to clip a legitimate target does not tighten the bound, and a narrower one would be a new authored constant that clips learned predictions. `discounted_backup_rounding` proves, for every model state with at most 64 ranked positions and no other hypothesis, that the executed discounted backed-up value is within the per-position allowance plus 2⁻¹⁶ + 2⁻¹¹ + 2⁻¹⁷ of clamp(r̂ + clamp(V)), where V is the exact maximum over meta actions of the per-action values and clamp is the exact projection onto the prediction range. Inside 2¹³ the one narrowing is rounded at the total's magnitude; beyond it the word stays beyond the range on the same side, where both projections are the same endpoint (`outcome_value_clamped`). The differential continuation is a raw binary32 word with no range, and a binary32 result is no closer to its exact value than half its own spacing, so no absolute bound holds at every magnitude. `differential_backup_rounding` proves, for any k up to 20 with every exact per-action value at most 7000·2ᵏ in magnitude, that the executed differential backed-up value lies between r̂ − gain·d̂ + M less the per-position allowance, 2⁻¹⁵ and (3·2⁻¹² + `meanSlack`)·2ᵏ, and r̂ − gain·d̂ + M plus the same with `meanRadius` for `meanSlack`, where M is (1 − ε) times the exact maximum plus ε times the exact mean of the per-action values; the lower side is wider by the tie window. `differential_backup_relative` states it for every model state: for any m bounding the exact per-action values, the factor 2ᵏ becomes max(1, m/3500). The allowance scales with the values the backup returns, not with the stored shared or deviation predictions. Each binary32 product, sum and difference is the exact result rounded once to nearest-even (PAR-16); the binary64 additions, the evaluation of the mean and the narrowings are bounded at every scale in `AcornVerif.CurrentPolicyMean`; the projections select a stored word.
- *Range.* Every predicted slot value is a finite word in [0, 1] by its storage type (`Transition.expected_legal`). `outcome_value_bound` and `differential_target_bound` keep every per-action value and the differential backed-up value finite for every dimension and state.
- *Storage.* `transition_weights` gives k(k + 4) weights per option: k rows and four deviation learners, each over the k positions. The width follows from the budget 3k² ≤ d for the rows alone (`transition_budget`). The complete parts of the three options hold 3k(k + 4) weights: within one weight vector from 1024 slots up (`complete_budget`), 13 056 weights for 16 384 slots (`default_budget`), and more than one weight vector at 256 slots, where k = 8 gives 288. `transition_storage` bounds the retained words of one option's rows and deviation learners by (k + 4)(13k + 2) for any stream length. Outside that budget each option also holds a lookup table of one word per feature slot and k slot words (`transition_table`), and the agent holds the recent feature vectors of PAR-14.
- *Work.* `ranked_input_work` bounds the positions a frame activates by k and by one more than its active features, and `row_work` and `deviation_work` each learner's eligibility list by k, so one model update costs at most k + 4 learners of O(k) work for each option. Each free boundary also ranks the units, at most k − 1 scans of the positive candidates (`rankedSlots_length`), and merges the result into each model in O(k²). A model whose slots change rebuilds its lookup table, one word per feature slot, and forgets the changed positions: each retained row and each deviation learner retires each changed position, and one retirement searches that learner's eligibility list. That is at most (k + 4)·c·k for c changed positions, c ≤ k (`changed_work`), cubic in k when half the ranking changes at once. A model whose slots do not change does neither (`RankedFeatures.rerank`). This is work per option and per boundary beyond the O(active) work of a learner update.
- *Composition.* The transition rows hold weights at ranked positions, not at feature slots, so they are no reader in the tester's contribution utility (PAR-11). Model learners restart cold on checkpoint restore; a restored or fresh model ranks no slot until the next free boundary.

**Residual uncertainty.** What share of an outcome's value the ranked slots carry, how accurate the rows and the deviation learners become under changing option policies, and whether planning with these models is stable are UNKNOWN: each depends on the experience stream and the learned weights, which no derivation from the definitions settles. A measured share near zero would favour ranking slots by their value-weight magnitude, which minimizes the bound Σ over unranked active slots of |w| on the residual.

**Refutation attempt.** The audit campaigns (`acorn-core audit`, one per arm, fixed seed and 512 × 512 world) refuted a first cadence: with the ranking installed only at a subtask refresh, which then ran only after an achieved attempt or a new curriculum cycle, no option held a ranked slot at the end of any arm or at three earlier sampled points of the declared-rate campaign, so the transition part was never exercised; installed at every free boundary, each option holds 62 or 63 ranked slots at the end of each arm. An independent reviewer with no part in the design attacked the claims against the sources and the code. Two findings changed the design. Rows without a constant input predicted zero from every frame that activates no ranked slot, so the look-ahead vanished exactly where the ranked slots say least; the reserved bias position is the result. The ranking does not by itself keep one slot at one position, so retirement now vacates every position that holds the retired slot (`retire_vacated`, `forget_column`). Four findings changed statements: the source's eq. (17) terminal target is x_j and γ·x_j is Acorn's correction; a changed value weight changes backed-up values at once but a predecessor's value only when it is backed up; the storage claim omitted the lookup table and the recent feature vectors; and Wan et al. conjecture divergence in §6 and demonstrate it in §7.1. A second independent review of the finished change found the bias vacancy and the distinctness of ranked slots stated only in prose, and the dependence on the value weights proved only for a model of the sum; the vacancy is now a field of the ranking, distinctness is `rankedSlots_nodup`, and `ranked_value_propagation` is over the stored words. A further review of the committed change confirmed five defects, each fixed here: one scalar residual added after the maximum hid changes at ranked slots when the ranked and the complete maxima were at different actions (per-action deviations, combined before the maximum); the type admitted one slot at two positions (now a field of the ranking); the differential nominal value was claimed convex and is not (the sandwich contract); forgetting changed positions is cubic in the worst case, not quadratic (the bound above); and the rounding contract covered only the discounted target (`differential_backup_rounding`). A second look at those fixes confirmed five more points, each fixed: the rounding contracts had been made conditional on a magnitude nothing enforces (restated at the scale of the prediction envelopes, which the next finding replaced); the expectation inequality ignored the upper rounding of the executed mean (both allowances are carried, and `expected_expectation` instantiates it); propagation was claimed for the executed word where it is proved for the exact value; the zero-deviation remark holds only under the discounted maximum; and the budget statements covered rows only (`complete_budget`). The envelope-scale contracts then showed a weakness in the executed sum: with two rounded additions per action, a shared residual and a deviation that cancel absorb part of the ranked value (for the words ranked 10, shared 2³⁰ and deviation −2³⁰ + 64 the two additions give 64 where the exact sum is 74; a stored deviation is bounded by its envelope of 2¹⁴ at 64 positions, so on stored states with a total inside the prediction range the loss was at most about 2⁻¹⁰, the first addition being rounded below 2¹⁵). Each per-action total is now accumulated in binary64 and narrowed once, those words evaluate to 74, and the discounted contract holds for every model state with no reasoning about the parts. What survived: the width derivation, the rounding bounds and the inequality under the maximum, which is about exact expected features and says nothing of the learned rows.

### PAR-14 · Background planning

[Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.03466). Execution owner: `Acorn.Planning`.

At each free decision boundary every option's meta action value is moved toward its backed-up value, at the current feature vector and at one stored recent feature vector. No stored experience is replayed.

**Adaptation and rationale.** The source's planning is approximate value iteration: w ← w + α[max_o (r̂(x, o) + v̂(n̂(x, o), w)) − v̂(x, w)]∇v̂(x, w), with the current weights w on both sides (arXiv:2202.03466v4, §5, eq. (19), p. 16).

- *Published rule, backup.* Each look-ahead reads the option's model under the value weights the meta-controller holds at that moment and moves that option's action value toward the backed-up value through the SwiftTD planning entry (`PlanningResult.lookAhead`). Look-aheads run one after another, so each reads what the previous one wrote. Signed correction permits downward as well as upward adjustment; projection preserves stored-weight legality and trace registers are unchanged.
- *Declared difference, maximum.* The source updates one state-value function toward the maximum over options. Acorn updates each option's action value toward its own backed-up value, and the maximum is the meta-controller's greedy choice among them (PAR-2). The primitive meta action has no model and is not backed up.
- *Local construction, search control.* The Alberta Plan treats planning as "asynchronous value iteration with function approximation" in which "the order chosen greatly affects planning efficiency" (arXiv:2208.11173v3, Step 9, p. 9). Acorn keeps the 128 most recent earlier feature vectors, one per step of the longest option, so every frame from which an option stopping now could have started is still stored. A learning frame is recorded when it completes, after selection and planning, so the store never holds the frame being decided. Each boundary backs up every option at the current vector and at one stored vector. The position moves against the write direction, one step per boundary (`RecentFeatures.advance`): it reaches every position within one pass of 128 boundaries (`sweep_position`, `sweep_reaches`) and consumes no random draw. Where every step is a boundary, the write position moves one way and the sweep the other, so each stored frame is selected once before it is overwritten, and the two close by two positions per step, so the ages selected share one parity. A position no frame has filled yet holds the empty feature vector; a look-ahead there writes nothing (`plan_empty`) and is still counted by the work counter. Two feature vectors per boundary, the store size and the fixed sweep are domain-general constants of the learned core, like the option count and the option duration cap; they read no property of the world and are not registered as a departure. Priorities and other strategies are separate work.
- *No replay.* The admission standard forbids replay. The store holds feature vectors only: no action, reward or successor, so no stored transition exists to present to a learner again. A stored feature vector is presented again only as the place of a backup, to the meta-controller's planning entry. Every target is computed from the current model and the current value weights, models and predictions learn only from the one stream, and the store is process-local and bounded by its type. A retired slot is erased from every stored vector (`FeatureRuntime.retire_recent`), so no backup writes a replacement unit's weight at a frame its predecessor was active in.
- *Composition, differential control.* The differential backed-up value is the reward part minus gain times duration, plus the nominal policy mean of the per-action values of the predicted outcome, with γ = 1 (PAR-15). It writes no gain.

**Contract and composition.** Acorn.Planning and AcornVerif.CurrentModels bind the schedule to execution: `planning_work` and `boundary_work` give two look-aheads per option per boundary, `planning_clock_bounds` the work counter, `planning_primitive` the untouched primitive row, and `planning_traces` and `planning_lags` the unchanged trace registers and lags of every row at both vectors. A changing value function, learned models and the maximum over action values preclude an inherited fixed-point or convergence guarantee.

**Residual uncertainty.** Whether these backups improve decisions is UNKNOWN (F2).

**Refutation attempt.** The independent reviewer of PAR-13 found that the first search control did nothing: frames were recorded before planning and the position moved with the write position, so the stored vector selected at a boundary was the current one whenever boundaries came every step. Recording at completion and sweeping against the write direction are the result. The no-replay argument survived: the store's type holds no action, reward or successor, and erasure at retirement covers every replaced unit of a tester step. The reviewer also confirmed that backing up each option's action value, rather than one state value toward the maximum, is declared.

### PAR-15 · Differential control

[Source](http://www.incompleteideas.net/book/the-book-2nd.html). Execution owner: `Acorn.Average`.

Reward minus estimated gain times duration supplies the continuing criterion. Gamma is one; existing rails impose a numerical budget. The reward-residual gain update and changing models are specified below.

**Adaptation and rationale.** The continuing criterion uses reward minus estimated gain times duration with gamma one. The reward-residual gain variant is used with a shared control criterion; existing value rails are a local numerical approximation because general differential values have no discount-derived bound. Model queries carry actual policy/sample and activation-age meaning. Under this criterion an option's backed-up value is its reward part minus gain times its duration part, plus the ε-mean of the meta action values of the predicted ranked slots, plus the residual (PAR-13, PAR-14); the transition rows carry no age input.

**Contract and composition.** Acorn.Average, Acorn.Models, Acorn.Planning, AcornVerif.AverageReward and AcornVerif.CurrentModelArithmetic own gain/return identities, the bound obstruction, model targets and stored arithmetic. The two-state family characterizes the obstruction to a transition-independent differential-value bound; the stored rail is an explicit numerical approximation.

**Refutation attempt.** Independent review traced every gain/value write, post-planning action ownership, repeated activation, raw target units and age-zero queries. Discrepancy identities distinguish known semantic incompatibilities from unmeasured usefulness. The integration remains demoted.

### PAR-16 · FloatLib rounding theory

[Source](https://github.com/lean-dojo/FloatLib): R. J. George, W. Adkisson and A. Anandkumar, *FloatLib* (2026), MIT licence, pinned at commit 1e83f09ed8c41a953cf8f93d26c210778177b94a. Proof owner: `AcornVerif.FloatLibBridge`.

FloatLib proves real-valued statements about Lean core's logical floating-point model: on finite operands with a finite result, the model's addition, multiplication and division each equal the exact real result rounded once to nearest-even (the three theorems named toReal_ofModel_add_finite_eq_roundAt, toReal_ofModel_mul_finite_eq_roundAt and toReal_ofModel_div_finite_eq_roundAt in its file FloatLib/Floats/Formats/BinaryInterchange/Arithmetic/LeanModel.lean), and its rational rounder returns the nearest-even rounding of a fraction (toReal_roundRatScaled_eq_roundAt). Acorn's binary32 and binary64 arithmetic is that same core model applied to raw words, so these statements are about the definitions Acorn executes.

**Adaptation and rationale.** This is a proof dependency, not a mechanism of the agent. No executed definition, native binary or audit pin changes, and no executing library imports it.

- *Proved connection, representation.* The bridge views the same bits as FloatLib's model. Both sides unpack them with Lean core's decoder, so the map agrees with the existing decoded reading by definition, and the two numerical readings and the two finiteness predicates are proved equal on every word.
- *Proved connection, operations.* For both widths, the executing addition, subtraction, multiplication and division on finite operands with a finite result are proved equal to one nearest-even rounding of the exact real result. FloatLib's multiplication and division theorems assume an exponent inequality that it does not discharge; the existing lemma `model_product_exponent_ready` supplies it for canonically unpacked operands and `model_divCore_ready` for every pair of nonzero finite operands.
- *Proved connection, division with no significant quotient bit.* FloatLib's division theorem also assumes a nonzero provisional quotient. Lean core's division core returns a zero provisional quotient with a nonzero remainder when the exact quotient lies below the exponent it selects, for example the least positive subnormal divided by 1.5. `roundWithAccuracy_zero_roundAt` proves the zero case from the remainder's accuracy record, adapting FloatLib's private fraction lemma as `fraction_accuracy` and the scaling tail of its division proof under FloatLib's MIT notice, which the bridge module carries, so `binary64_div_roundAt` and `binary32_div_roundAt` carry no magnitude hypothesis.
- *Why not replace the executing arithmetic.* FloatLib's verified arithmetic is software arithmetic in modules that import Mathlib. Its compiled functions reach the recursors Mathlib compiles for Bool, Option and List, so an executing library that called them could not keep its checked execution claim under the [Regula audit](verification.md#regula-audit), and it would exchange hardware arithmetic for software arithmetic.

**Contract and composition.** AcornVerif.FloatLibBridge is the only importer; the boundary audit refuses a FloatLib import anywhere else, and refuses any import of it by an executing module. `binary64_div_half_unit_error` bounds the executing binary64 division by half the grid spacing under the hypotheses of `binary64_div_finite_error`, one quarter of that theorem's radius. `nearestEven_eq_roundQuotientEven` identifies Acorn's integer nearest-even quotient with FloatLib's. For each decimal constant in Acorn.Constants a theorem such as `band030_nearest` states that the word is the decimal rounded once to nearest-even binary32. Every theorem of the bridge depends only on propext, Quot.sound and Classical.choice. Statements concern finite operands and finite results; exceptional words and the native primitive correspondence stay outside them.

**Residual uncertainty.** The ideal-function error of the local exponential and logarithm remains unproved; the bridge supplies the per-operation statements that proof needs. FloatLib is young and has one committer, so the dependency is pinned to a commit and confined to one module.

**Refutation attempt.** Before adoption the library at the pinned commit was built whole on the pinned toolchain and audited with Regula v0.3.1, claimed at standard-logical with checked execution. The attack looked for holes, project axioms, compiler-trusting proofs, unproved runtime replacements and theorem hypotheses that Acorn's definitions do not meet. All 28,623 declarations use only propext, Quot.sound and Classical.choice, with no hole and no compiler-trusting proof. The audit found that its compiled functions fail checked execution through Mathlib's compiled recursors, which is why no executing module may import it. Reading the three operation theorems against Acorn's wrappers found the undischarged exponent hypothesis and the nonzero-quotient premise; the first is supplied by existing lemmas and the second is removed by the zero-quotient theorem above. This record is the adopting investigation's own; an independent review of this entry is still owed.

### PAR-17 · Off-policy option learning

[Tree backup](http://incompleteideas.net/papers/PSS-00.pdf); [intra-option learning](http://www.incompleteideas.net/papers/SPS-aij.pdf); [reward-respecting subtasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.03466). Execution owner: `Acorn.Temporal`.

On every step of a learning hierarchy, each option that is not executing learns from the action the agent actually took. Its action-value policy takes tree-backup credit on every such step, and its model learns along runs of frames whose action was selected with the option's own distribution. The executing option keeps its on-policy update, and terminal credit keeps its owner.

**Adaptation and rationale.** Sutton, Machado et al. update every subtask's option and model on each nonterminal step, stopping where the option's own stopping function does, with the importance ratio ρ = π(A|S)/μ(A|S) on the trace (arXiv:2202.03466v4, §3, eq. (10), p. 11; §4, eq. (17), p. 14). Acorn keeps the every-step schedule, applies its own stopping decision (PAR-5, PAR-7) and replaces the ratio:

- *Published variant, policy credit.* The option policies are ε-greedy action-value controllers (PAR-2). Each takes tree-backup credit: Precup, Sutton & Singh, *Eligibility Traces for Off-Policy Policy Evaluation*, ICML 2000, §4, Algorithm 2, p. 4. The error is r + γ Σₐ π(a|s′) q(s′, a) − q(s, a), and earlier traces decay by γλ·π(a′|s′) for the action a′ taken at s′; π is the option's own nominal ε-greedy mass at its frozen values. The ratio methods "require that it be known, Markov (purely a function of the current state), and explicitly represented as action probabilities" (§4, p. 4, of the behaviour policy). Acorn reports a nominal mass for each action on every frame, but the behaviour is not Markov, and a served exploratory run (PAR-8) is a point mass fixed by history: it gives zero probability to actions the option would take, so a ratio estimator has no coverage there, and where the behaviour is ε-greedy over nine actions the ratio reaches 9/ε. Tree backup "requires nothing of the behavior policy other than that it be non-starving" (§4, p. 4). An action value is conditioned on its own action, so a trajectory's first action needs no correction. The source's experiments divide each trace by the largest target probability so that traces do not decay too quickly (p. 5); Acorn applies Algorithm 2 as printed, because that division is a state-dependent λ that can exceed one, outside Theorem 3's hypothesis.
- *Published rule, model credit.* The models are state-value learners (PAR-13), so tree backup, which needs a value for each action, does not apply to them, and the published correction is the ratio of eq. (17). Sutton, Precup & Singh call an action consistent with an option when it "was selected with the distribution π(s_t, ·)" and apply the model updates "to every option consistent with every action taken" (AIJ 112 (1999), §5, eqs. (18)–(19), p. 202). Acorn's option policies are stochastic, so the test is on distributions: a frame is consistent with an option when the masses the behaviour reported for that frame equal the option's own nominal masses, word for word. On such a frame the ratio of eq. (17) is one for every action, exploratory ones included, so the model takes its ordinary update and its traces carry through, as eq. (17) prescribes at ρ = 1; on any other frame the model's trajectory is cut. Because the test reads the two distributions and never the action drawn, the cut is not selected by the action. The model's target is therefore the option's ε-greedy policy as executed, the same target its on-policy updates have. A served exploratory step reports a point mass and is consistent with no option that gives two actions positive mass.
- *Why not the ratio elsewhere.* Where the two distributions differ, eq. (17) needs ρ itself. It reaches 9/ε, about 900 at the declared rate, on an action the behaviour explored into and the option prefers. SwiftTD bounds each update by a step-size budget computed without a ratio, and binds its weight projection and re-anchors the step size when an update overshoots; a trace scaled by such a ratio would do that on every active weight. Clipping the ratio to one is not a remedy: the clipped estimator's fixed point is the policy proportional to min(μ, π), which is close to uniform wherever the behaviour and the option prefer different actions. Two narrower tests were considered and rejected: treating every tied greedy candidate as consistent teaches the model the behaviour's choice within the tie, and requiring the taken action to be the unique greedy one targets the greedy policy, not the executed one, and learns nothing while values tie.
- *Local construction, trajectory state.* Each skill stores the trajectory of its learners while it is not executing: its age, the number of actions followed since it began; whether the model has a live trajectory; and the potential observed at the preceding frame. The age advances on every followed frame, so the executing option's stopping decision (goal, 128-step cap, strict interruption) applies to the stored trajectory unchanged, and a model trajectory that starts partway through reads the option's age at that frame.
- *Published variant, stops.* When the stopping decision fires, the policy is credited the stopping value with trace decay zero, the factor γλ(1 − β) of the source's update procedure at β = 1 (§3, eq. (5) and p. 9), and a live model trajectory is closed toward the terminal targets of PAR-13. Every remaining trace is then released structurally, in work proportional to their number, and the option continues from the current frame with the action taken, as eq. (10) continues after β = 1. A stop does not use the full-width transient clear an invocation start uses, because a stop can occur on every frame for every option.
- *Local construction, settling at an invocation start.* An option the meta-controller selects was following until that frame. Before its invocation start clears its trajectory state, the completed transition is credited exactly as on a followed frame: the stopping value where the stopping decision fires, the tree-backup error otherwise, and the model's step along a live trajectory. No transition a followed option has seen is discarded uncredited.
- *Local construction, interruption by a run.* A persistent run that an executing option's draw begins (PAR-8, D3) takes control at its first served step. The run does not stop the option. The option is linked to the trajectory it was executing, with its age and preceding potential and a live model trajectory, because its last action was a sample of its own distribution. That frame's stage then credits the completed transition exactly as on a followed frame, under the option's own stopping decision, and the served steps are followed like any other. A stop invented by the run would add, to the option only while it executes, a stopping condition that fires at the exploration rate, and the same learners would be trained toward two different stopping functions.
- *Missing mechanism, off-policy SwiftTD.* No off-policy SwiftTD or Swift-Sarsa is published. The correction enters only through arguments the learner already accepts as raw words, the error and the first-loop trace decay, so every existing learner contract (legality, eligibility capacity, schedule) applies unchanged. The learner gains one entry, the structural release of its eligible traces, which writes no knowledge.

Consequences. Algorithm 2's convergence result (Theorem 3, p. 5) concerns tabular, offline updating toward a fixed target policy; none of it transfers to linear function approximation, online updating, adaptive step sizes or a target policy that is ε-greedy in its own changing values. A taken action outside the candidates multiplies earlier policy traces by ε/9, and one of k tied candidates by about 1/k, so off-policy policy credit is close to one-step until one action is uniquely greedy and the behaviour takes it. The meaning of SwiftTD's meta-gradient registers under the corrected decay, and with an expected bootstrap beside a sampled lag, is not established. The source's model rule also requires a Markov option (§5, p. 201); Acorn's options stop at an age cap, and under discounted control the model input carries no age. Consistency compares nominal masses: two controllers have equal masses exactly when their rates and tied candidates agree, so a profile whose primitive and option rates differ, such as the annealed comparison, has consistent frames only while another option with the same candidates is executing. The stopping estimate is the nominal value of the frame's meta-controller snapshot in both criteria: the maximum under discounted control and the ε-mean under differential control, where the executing option's terminal credit uses the value of the next sampled meta action; no meta action is drawn for an option that is not executing.

**Contract and composition.** Acorn.Temporal's `Skill.followTemporal` owns one option's frame, Acorn.Policy's `Controller.backupStep` the tree-backup credit and Acorn.Handcrafted.TemporalControl's `followOptions` the stage, which runs after selection and before primitive credit, so every option reads the same pre-observation gain as the executing one and consumes no random draw. `Skill.settleFollowing` runs inside selection, where the meta-controller starts an option.

- *Reduction.* `Controller.valuesStep_eq_creditStep` proves the on-policy update is the shared kernel at its sampled bootstrap, for every state and raw input word. `follow_executing` and `step_executing` prove the executing option's policy and model after the stage are exactly those selection left. `Skill.settleFollowing_unlinked` proves a skill linked to no frame is settled to itself, so the start of an option that was executing, fresh or restored is the existing on-policy start. `serve_interrupts` and `follow_unoccupied` prove an interrupted option is followed at the first served step. Where its own stopping decision continues there, `handoff_model`, `handoff_policy` and `handoff_age` prove its model takes the executing option's continuing credit word for word, its policy takes tree-backup credit with the same shaped cumulant, and its age keeps counting; where it ends, `handoff_stop` proves the option takes the stop of any followed option.
- *Bounded correction.* `PolicySnapshot.mass_bounded` proves the policy's trace correction is a stored probability in [0, 1] for every snapshot, including nonfinite value words. `PolicySnapshot.consistent_mass` proves that on a consistent frame the behaviour's reported mass equals the option's for every action, so the model's ratio is one wherever it is defined. Issue 10 expected a ratio bound of 9/ε; no ratio other than one is used, and a served step is never consistent with a stochastic option, so served exploration needs no separate bound.
- *Work.* Each option that is not executing costs about one executing-option step, so option learning goes from at most one such step per frame to three. `follow_inactive` proves frozen and primitive-only profiles do no option work and `follow_frame` that the stage writes only the skill table. Every learner keeps an eligibility list within the feature capacity by its storage type (`follow_legal`); that is a bound in the capacity, not in the active features. `stop_step_empty` and `stop_trajectory_empty` prove a stop leaves every policy and model learner nothing eligible, for every state and every trace word, finite or not, because its last entry is the structural release (`release_empty`). `release_idle` proves the first loop that follows a release is the identity, and `release_knowledge` that a release writes no weight or step size, so a start credits no earlier transition. A stopping frame therefore makes one first-loop pass over each eligibility list and one release pass over whatever that loop retained, which is nothing when the traces are finite (`first_loop_stop`, from `mul32_zero`); a continuing frame makes one first-loop pass. The count of loops per frame is read from the definition; it is not a theorem.
- *Consistency and age.* `PolicySnapshot.consistent_iff` identifies the test with equality of the two mass vectors, `PolicyDecision.consistent_own` proves a plain draw's decision is consistent with its policy and `CurrentTemporal.persistent_consistent` that a persistent draw's is, the first action of a run included. `follow_age` proves the stored age advances by exactly one on every continuing frame, whatever the action, and `follow_cap` that a trajectory at the option's full duration ends at the next frame, so policy learning cannot run past the cap. `follow_live` proves the model trajectory is live after a frame exactly when that frame was consistent; `follow_live_model` that along a live trajectory the model takes exactly the executing option's continuing credit; `follow_idle_model` that with no live trajectory an inconsistent frame neither credits nor restarts the model; `follow_policy` that the policy takes tree-backup credit with the shaped cumulant the executing option would have read.
- *Settling.* `settle_continuing` and `settle_ending` prove an option that starts executing first takes the credit a followed frame would have given, and `Skill.settleFollowing_owner` that settling keeps the objective and leaves no stored trajectory.
- *Frozen estimate.* `stopping_estimate_frozen` proves that when a meta decision was drawn at the frame, the stopping estimate reads that decision's own snapshot, values and rate, so a rate read after the meta learner's update cannot enter it.
- *Ownership.* The trajectory is a field of the skill. Fresh, released and restored storage carries none (`FreeDispatch.install_unlinked`, `Ensemble.release_unlinked`, `Ensemble.restore_unlinked`), so the first frame under a new objective starts a trajectory and credits no earlier transition; a still-ranked unit keeps its slot and its trajectory, since its objective is unchanged. `TemporalControl.followOptions_aligned` carries learned interests and distinct held units through the stage, and terminal credit of an executing option is assigned as before.
- *Planning.* Planning (PAR-14) reads every option's model at each free boundary. A model that has seen no consistent frame and no execution is still zero, as before. A model that closes off-policy reads the meta-controller as it stands at that frame for its residual target (PAR-13), while the policy's stop keeps the frozen stopping estimate.
- *Checkpoint.* The trajectory is process-local, like every trace, so the format is unchanged and a restored agent starts each option's trajectory at its first frame.

**Residual uncertainty.** How often a frame is consistent with an option that is not executing, how often its stopping decision fires there, and whether option policies and models improve are UNKNOWN: each depends on the experience stream and the learned values, which no derivation from the definitions settles.

**Refutation attempt.** Two fresh-context reviews attacked the entry. The first, instructed only to break it, attacked the update order against Algorithm 2, frame alignment on every selection branch, stale lags, the stopping decision, the model rule, ownership at every skill boundary, each theorem's statement, composition with planning and cost, and checked every citation. The update order, the alignment (no frame is credited twice or to another objective), ownership and the citations survived. It found that the model's test then admitted every tied candidate, so the model learned the behaviour's choice within a tie; that the stop bounds rested on an assumed zero product; that a start read a stored accumulator; and that the ratio rationale, the Markov requirement, the stopping age and the trace cut were overstated or undeclared. The second review found that the unique-greedy test adopted in response rejected actions the executed ε-greedy policy supports and trained the model toward a different policy than its on-policy updates; that an option starting to execute discarded the transition it had followed; that the model's run length drove the policy's duration clock, so policy learning could run past the cap; that the stopping estimate paired frozen meta values with a rate read after the meta update; and that the stop bound assumed finite traces, which admission does not enforce. The test is now equality of the behaviour's and the option's distributions, the settling step credits the followed transition, the trajectory age advances on every frame, the estimate reads the decision's frozen snapshot, and a stop releases its traces structurally; each is stated above with its theorem. Stability of tree backup with linear function approximation and the meta-gradient semantics were not settled by either review.

### PAR-18 · Off-policy questions

[Horde](http://www.incompleteideas.net/papers/horde-aamas-11.pdf); [GTD2-MP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.14364v2); [intra-option learning](http://www.incompleteideas.net/papers/SPS-aij.pdf). Execution owners: `Acorn.OffPolicy`, `Acorn.Handcrafted.TemporalControl`.

Each option asks the eleven D5 questions about its own policy: for each signal, its discounted sum, at its declared horizon, if the agent followed the option's policy as the option executes it. Every frame whose action was selected with an option's own distribution trains that option's questions, with the GTD2-MP gradient-TD method at importance ratio one and trace parameter zero. No decision reads the questions.

**Adaptation and rationale.** Horde learns many predictions about policies other than the one being followed from the "snippets of relevant experience", which "requires off-policy learning" (Sutton, Modayil, Delp, Degris, Pilarski, White & Precup, AAMAS 2011, §4, p. 763), and uses GQ(λ), a gradient-TD method with a second weight vector (p. 764).

- *Target policies.* The three options' policies, the candidates issue 16 names. Each question asks about the option's ε-greedy policy at its own nominal masses, the same target its model's on-policy and off-policy updates have (PAR-17), so an option's model and its questions concern one policy.
- *Published rule, which transitions.* A transition trains an option's questions exactly when the option drew its action, or when the masses the behaviour reported for the frame equal the option's own, word for word: Sutton, Precup & Singh's consistency of an action with an option (AIJ 112 (1999), §5, p. 202), as PAR-17 applies it to models. On such a frame the ratio of the two reported masses is one for every action, so none is formed. The bank reads PAR-17's live-trajectory flag after `followOptions` and does not test again. This is a choice, not forced by the rule: with an ε-greedy target every frame has a finite positive ratio, and using only the frames whose ratio is one gives fewer samples and weights states by how often the behaviour has the option's distribution there. The ratio elsewhere reaches 9/ε, which PAR-17 rejects for the same reasons. A greedy target with every tied candidate admitted, as a first design proposed, was also rejected: it teaches the behaviour's choice within a tie, which PAR-17's review found for the same test on models.
- *Published variant, learner.* GTD2-MP (Liu, Liu, Ghavamzadeh, Mahadevan & Petrik, *Finite-Sample Analysis of Proximal Gradient TD Algorithms*, UAI 2015, arXiv:2006.14364v2, §5, Algorithm 2 and footnote 3, PDF p. 7) at ρ = 1 with binary features. With `p = ⟨u, x⟩`, `δ = c − ⟨x − γx', w⟩`, `τ = h|x|` and `κ = h|x − γx'|²`, Algorithm 2's midpoint has the inner products `p_m = p + τ(δ − p)` and `δ_m = δ − κp`, and its step adds `h(δ_m − p_m)` to `u` on `x` and `h·p_m·(x − γx')` to `w`. The midpoint vectors are never formed. Reason for this member of the family: its step satisfies a per-step energy inequality on every stream (below). Plain GTD2 does not (`Extragradient.gtd2_change`), and TDC "seems not to have an explicit saddle-point representation" (§6.4, p. 8). The inequality answers a known degeneracy: semi-gradient TD at a uniform step size can grow its weights from its own bootstrap off-policy. `Extragradient.semigradient_family` gives a family of `K` equally weighted binary-feature transitions whose expected update scales one direction by `2γ − 1 − 1/K` per unit step size, positive exactly when `γ > (K + 1)/(2K)`, a threshold that every discount of Acorn exceeds for `K ≥ 2`.
- *Local approximation, step size.* `h = min(α, η/(|x| + |x'|))` with the demons' initial step size α and rate budget η (D4): SwiftTD's overshoot bound with its budget η (Javed, Sharifnassab & Sutton, RLC 2024, §5.2, eqs. (6)–(7), and §6), applied to both active sets. It makes `τ` and `κ` at most `1/9` for every classified transition (`step_guard`), which are the energy inequality's hypotheses. At typical sizes the budget binds, so the step is normalized by the number of active slots.
- *Mean equations.* One sample's update is `h((1 − τ)(δ − p) − κp)·x` for `u` and `h(p + τ(δ − p))·(x − γx')` for `w` (the executed scalars' exact counterparts are `exactCorrection` and `exactMidpoint`), so the mean update is stationary where `E[h((1 − τ)(δ − p) − κp)·x] = 0` and `E[h(p + τ(δ − p))·(x − γx')] = 0`. At `τ = κ = 0` these are GTD2's `E[h(δ − p)·x] = 0` and `E[h·p·(x − γx')] = 0` with each transition weighted by its own `h`, so transitions between smaller active sets weigh more. The midpoint is taken on the same sample as the step, which adds the terms in `τ` and `κ`: at the TD fixed point with `u = 0` the mean main-weight update is `E[h·τ·δ·(x − γx')]`, which is not zero in general. The stationary point therefore differs from the `h`-weighted TD fixed point by terms of relative order `τ` and `κ`: at most `1/9` each, and about `η/2` and at most `η` when the budget binds. The published analysis is for a decreasing step size and an averaged iterate; a constant step and the last iterate keep this bias. Its size in value terms is UNKNOWN.
- *Local rule, transition size.* A transition is classified, and so learned from, only when neither active set exceeds 2¹⁶ slots (`passageLimit`, a field of `Passage`, checked where the transition is classified). The rounding analysis below covers exactly these transitions, in feature spaces of every admitted capacity. The default encoder activates about 1 300 slots, so the rule excludes no transition observed in the audit.
- *Not composed, step-size adaptation.* No step size adapts. The energy inequality measures the weights in a norm that a per-weight step size enters; a step size that decreases enlarges that norm, so SwiftTD's meta-gradient adaptation would void the guarantee. No published adaptive gradient-TD step with a per-step guarantee is known here. A proof that monotone non-decreasing adaptation keeps the inequality, together with evidence that learning is too slow without it, would change this choice.
- *Local approximation, trace parameter zero.* With a trace the step is no longer the extragradient step of one transition, and the inequality's argument does not extend. Horde notes that behaviour matches a target policy "rarely for more than a few steps in a row" (§4, p. 763), which limits what traces add for an option that is not executing.
- *Local approximation, last iterate and projection.* Algorithm 2 outputs a step-size-weighted average of the iterates (eq. (34)); a continuing learner uses its last iterate at a capped step, and every write passes the weight projection. No published finite-sample or convergence statement survives online, dependent samples from changing options.
- *Equivalent specialization, state values.* Horde's demons are action-value functions; a question here asks "follow the option's policy from here", with D5's constant discounts in place of a termination function.

**Contract and composition.** `Acorn.OffPolicy` owns the learner, the classified transition (`Passage`, whose class lists and step size carry their definitions as fields), the stored preceding frame with its membership flags (`Preceding`, flags equal to membership by a field), the question (`Question`, a learner with its silence flag) and each option's bank (`OptionQuestions`). Each `Skill` holds its bank, so every construction that installs a fresh skill asks the questions afresh. `TemporalControl.finish` evaluates the eleven signals once, for the demons and the questions, and runs the bank in learning hierarchy profiles only.

- *Energy, exact arithmetic.* `AcornVerif.Extragradient.extragradient_energy` proves, over any finite index set, that one step from any `(u, w)` with `|u|² + |w − w*|² ≤ t²` and `hτ ≤ s²` ends within `(t + |ε|s)²`, where `ε = c − ⟨x − γx', w*⟩`, whenever `τ(τ + 2κ) ≤ 1` and `κ ≤ 2 − τ`; with zero residual the distance never grows (`zero_residual_nonincreasing`).
- *Energy, executed step.* `AcornVerif.CurrentOffPolicy.step_energy` carries it to the executed step, for every learner state, every classified transition, every finite signal word in `[−1, 1]` (the D5 signals are indicators, `Cumulant.indicator`) and every reference inside the weight range, in feature spaces of every admitted capacity: the bound on the two active sets is a field of the transition's type, not a hypothesis on the space. If `N ≤ t²` before the step, `hτ ≤ s²`, `|w*|² ≤ m²` and `(2|x| + |x'|)·2⁻⁴⁸ ≤ R²`, then after it `N ≤ ((1 + 2⁻²¹)·t + (1 + 2⁻²²)·|ε|·s + 2⁻²¹·m + R)²`. Against `w* = 0` the length of `(u, w)` therefore grows per step by at most the factor `1 + 2⁻²¹`, plus the signal's push and `R`, which is `√(3n)·2⁻²⁴` when both sets have `n` slots, about `3.7·10⁻⁶` at `n = 1 300`. The proof bounds each written word against the exact step from the same stored words before the projection (`second_slot`, `main_slot`): each binary32 write and each narrowed increment is one nearest-even rounding, within 2⁻²⁴ relative plus 2⁻¹⁵⁰ (`binary32_add_relative`, `narrow_relative`), and the binary64 scalars are bounded through the bridge (`scalars_approx`, `increments_close`). The projection never moves a word away from a point inside the range (`clampSym_toward`, `project_value`). The factor is a worst case over rounding directions, not an estimate of drift.
- *Silent questions.* Zero weights and a zero signal are a fixed point of Algorithm 2: every sum, and so every increment, is zero. A question that has learned from no signal word other than zero is silent; its type carries that every weight of a silent question is zero, at construction, at each step and at retirement. A silent question at a zero signal word takes no step and reads no weight; any other step is the learner's and ends the silence (`question_step`, `question_silent`). `silent_fixed` proves that wherever no step is taken the exact midpoint and correction read from the stored words are zero, so Algorithm 2 in exact arithmetic adds zero to every weight, and `question_energy` gives every question's executed step the bound of `step_energy`. That the executed learner step would write the same words there is argued, not machine-checked: every sum, product and narrowing of positive zeros by non-negative finite words is positive zero, and adding it stores the same word. The three knowledge checksums were the same with and without the skip (observed).
- *Unit ratio.* No ratio is formed. `askQuestions_get` arms a slot exactly when the option drew the frame's action or its model trajectory is live; `follow_armed_ratio` and `start_armed_ratio` prove that every armed followed frame reports the option's own masses, action by action, and `CurrentTemporal.persistent_consistent` covers the executing option, whose draw is persistent. A served exploratory frame reports a point mass; `served_unarmed` proves it never arms an option whose snapshot gives a nonzero mass word to another action. These statements concern the reported nominal masses; that each draw samples exactly those masses is the idealized-draw assumption of PAR-8 and PAR-17, not a theorem. Every increment is at most 64 in magnitude for every learner state under the same hypotheses (`increments_close`).
- *Classes and writes.* `classes_partition`, `classes_disjoint` and `class_counts` partition `x ∪ x'`; `step_second` and `step_main` write the second weights exactly at `x` and the main weights exactly at `x ∪ x'`, each slot once, with its class's increment; `advance_stores` and `questions_follow` store a frame exactly when it is armed, and a transition is learned exactly when the stored set is not empty and neither set exceeds the transition size.
- *Lifecycle.* `learner_retire`, `question_retire` and `preceding_retire` leave both weights of a question at zero at a retired slot and drop the slot from the stored frame; `install_questions`, `release_questions` and `restore_questions` give a reinstalled, released or restored slot fresh questions, with no stored frame (`questions_initial`), so the first transition after any of them trains nothing; `refresh_questions` keeps the questions of the first slot holding a unit that stays ranked. The checkpoint stores no question and its format is unchanged, so a restored agent's questions start from zero, as its models do.
- *Observer.* `TemporalControl.finish_skill` proves the completion boundary writes each slot's questions and no other field of any slot. The bank is not among `Skill.readers` or `Skill.stored`, so the tester's utility does not read it, and no selection, planning or telemetry definition reads it. That no decision depends on the questions is read from the definitions and observed in the audit, whose three action digests this change leaves unchanged; a non-interference theorem over `TemporalControl.step` is not proved. The knowledge checksum folds every question's weights.
- *Storage and work.* `bank_weights` gives `2·d·k` weights per option for `k` signals over `d` slots: 360 448 per option and 1 081 344 for three options at the default 16 384 slots, held in every profile, including the frozen and primitive-only profiles that never train them, with one membership flag per slot per option. A learner's two vectors are built separately (`GradientLearner.initial`): two equal replicated vectors compile to one array referenced twice, whose first write would copy every slot, so the native route of that definition requires both constructions in the compiled code. Each step a question takes reads the main weights of `x ∪ x'` and the second weights of `x` once and writes each once: the main sum over `x ∩ x'` is continued over `x \ x'` and over `x' \ x` (`classSums_approx`). Every fold of the step is over one of the three class lists or `x`, and `passage_work` counts the slots one learner step visits, reads and writes together, as `2·|x ∪ x'| + 2·|x|`, at most `4·|x| + 2·|x'|`. The classification filters and re-marks flags over `x` and `x'` once per option and frame. `finish_skill` leaves every skill unchanged in frozen and primitive-only profiles. Installing, releasing or restoring a slot builds its bank: `2·d·k` weight words and `d` membership flags, `23·d` words for the eleven signals. Those events are an assignment refresh at a free boundary, the release of a slot whose unit is replaced, and a checkpoint restore; they are not learner updates, and at the same events each of the slot's SwiftTD learners is built with twelve vectors of `d` words (`retained_slots_exact`), nine policy learners and the model's among them. As for the model's ranking change (PAR-13), this is work per option and per such event beyond the `O(active)` work of a learner update. That the folds are the only work is read from the definitions. That each write is in place is a property of the compiled code, which consumes the learner before writing and builds its vectors separately; it was checked by reading the generated code and with a temporary shared-reference trace of the declared-rate arm, and it is not a theorem. That trace first found each learner's vectors referenced twice at its first write after every attempt boundary: the campaign loop kept the preceding run state alive while the attempt ran, so the first write to every array of the agent copied it, in the SwiftTD learners too ([#51](https://github.com/rbeauchamp/acorn/issues/51)). The loop now hands its progress record to `campaignStep`, which takes it apart before the attempt starts and after which no return follows; the compiled step passes the run state to the attempt without retaining it, and the same trace then found no shared vector across the first four attempt boundaries of the declared-rate arm (observed).

**Residual uncertainty.** In the first 6 304 frames of the declared-rate audit arm, 17 808 of 18 912 option-frames learned a transition (observed, one traced run): the masses reported for most frames equalled every option's own, so all three options were armed. How often a frame arms an option that is not executing once the option policies differ, how accurate the questions become under changing options with a step normalized by the active slots, how far a constant step and the last iterate leave the fixed point from the projected Bellman fixed point, and whether any reader would benefit from the questions are UNKNOWN: each depends on the experience stream and the learned values. Usefulness needs a reader and a prospective qualification. PAR-17's models learn along the same armed transitions with SwiftTD and no gradient correction; whether the semi-gradient growth above reaches them under adaptive per-weight step sizes is not established, their on-policy reduction requires SwiftTD on executing frames, and correcting them is outside this entry.

**Refutation attempt.** An earlier design proposal for this entry ([#33](https://github.com/rbeauchamp/acorn/pull/33)) had five reviews, which attacked the scalar reduction of Algorithm 2, the energy algebra, the semi-gradient family and the citations, and from which the learner and its inequality are taken; its greedy target with tied candidates admitted and its marks scratch are replaced here. A fresh-context reviewer, instructed only to break this entry, then attacked the executed code and the claims before the first build. It re-derived the reduction of Algorithm 2 at ρ = 1 line by line against the source, the class coefficients and the single write per slot; traced the frame alignment on every selection branch (continuing, ending then primitive, ending then a new option with settling, exploration start, served continuation, refresh, release, retirement, restore, frozen and primitive-only profiles) and found no transition credited twice, to another objective or over a retired slot; re-derived the zero-residual identity, the guard, the push bound and the clamp argument; and checked the citations it could reach. Those survived. Its findings changed the entry. The executed energy bound first carried an absolute allowance of 2⁻¹⁰ per written slot, which at default sizes let `√N` grow by about 0.06 per step and so ruled out nothing; it is now the relative bound above, proved through FloatLib's standard model of one rounding. The step size's dependence on the successor's size was undeclared; the 2¹⁶-slot hypothesis and the signal range were missing from the prose; "ratio is one" overstated a statement about reported masses and hid that using only such frames is a choice; several attributions were stronger than their sources; and the signal-value type lived in a module named for another role. Each is corrected here. It also asked for a non-interference theorem for the observer claim, which remains unproved and is stated as such. A second fresh-context reviewer then attacked what was added after that review: the silent questions, the continued sums, and the two option boundaries that now take the option out of its table before updating it. It checked that every construction and write of a question establishes the silence invariant and that silence cannot coexist with a nonzero or negative-zero weight, that the continued sums are the right two sums, that both equality theorems state the previous compositions and that every rebuilt state lists every field, and replayed the native routes against the generated code; these survived. It found that no theorem tied the fixed-point statement to the branch that takes no step, that retirement wrote into silent questions, that the option checksums could cancel between options holding the same words, and several statements about storage and work that claimed more than was proved. `silent_fixed` now takes the branch condition, a silent question is kept at retirement (`question_retire`), each option's checksum is seeded with its slot, and the statements are corrected above. A third review of the committed change, by another reviewer, confirmed five further defects, none in the learned words. A fresh learner's two vectors were one array referenced twice, so its first write copied every slot of the space; they are now built separately and the compiled code is gated on both constructions. The energy and increment bounds assumed a space of at most 2¹⁶ slots, which admission does not enforce; the bound is now a field of the classified transition, checked where it is classified, and the hypothesis on the space is gone. The entry described the mean fixed point as a reweighting by `h` alone and omitted the same-sample midpoint's terms in `τ` and `κ`, which a two-state example exhibits; the mean equations are now stated. The semi-gradient family's coefficient was the sum over its `K` transitions, not their mean; `semigradient_family` now states the mean and its threshold. The baseline scorecard attributed SwiftTD's step-size adaptation to every learner; the questions are now named as the exception. A second look at those fixes confirmed three more. The campaign loop kept the preceding run state alive during an attempt, so first writes after an attempt boundary copied every array; the loop now consumes its progress before each attempt (above). Installing a slot builds dense vectors whatever the active count; that cost is now stated beside the existing learners' and the model's ranking change, which Acorn's contracts already account apart from a learner update. A lemma's comment named one of the two conditions under which a transition is learned; it names both.

### PAR-19 · Planning after the action

[Source](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/robotics-and-ai/articles/10.3389/frobt.2018.00079/full): J. B. Travnik, K. W. Mathewson, R. S. Sutton and P. M. Pilarski, *Reactive Reinforcement Learning in Asynchronous Environments*, Frontiers in Robotics and AI 5:79 (2018). Execution owners: `Acorn.Timing` (the order and the citation), `Acorn.Handcrafted.StepParts`, `Acorn.Host.Attempt`, `Acorn.Host.Runner`. Proof owner: `AcornVerif.StepParts`.

A step is two functions, and a declared step order is part of the agent's construction. Under the default order, `learn-then-act`, both parts run before the world receives the action, and the step is the step of every recorded result. Under `plan-after-act` the host releases the action between the parts, and the planning of a free boundary (PAR-14) runs after the action instead of before the meta draw. No learning or planning function changes; the order in which selection, credit and planning compose does.

**Adaptation and rationale.** The source reorders one loop. Its Algorithm 1 (SARSA, §2) takes the action it chose at the start of the next iteration, after the update; its Algorithm 2 (Reactive SARSA, §3) takes the action directly after choosing it, before the update. Both choose the action before the update, and the source has no planning. The purpose of the reordering is a shorter time between an observation and the action taken on it.

- *Where the step is cut.* The step is cut where its decision is known, after selection. `Agent.choose` is the clock, the encoding of the frame and selection; `Chosen.learn` is everything after it: off-policy option learning (PAR-17), the closing of an interrupted meta span, primitive credit (PAR-2), the demons (PAR-3), the questions (PAR-18) and the tester (PAR-11). `Agent.act_parts` proves that under the default order the two compose to `Agent.act`, for every agent state and percept, in every world.
- *Declared difference, the first part learns.* In the source the part before the action is a policy read. In Acorn it is not. Selection contains writes, and four of them precede a draw that reads them: the assignment refresh (PAR-12), planning (PAR-14), a closing option's terminal credit (PAR-7) and the settlement of a newly selected option (PAR-17). A release of the action between the two parts alone changes no value that a draw reads; it only moves the world's transition. So the order moves a write.
- *Declared difference, planning moves.* The dispatcher takes its planning function as a parameter. Under `plan-after-act` selection runs with the no-planning boundary, and the second part starts with the configured planning on the selected state when the decision records a meta decision (`TemporalControl.planAfter`). At a free dispatch the meta action is then drawn from the meta-controller after the assignment refresh and before this frame's planning (`TemporalControl.atBoundary_unplanned`), where the default order draws it after that planning (`TemporalControl.atBoundary_meta`). The decision keeps the frozen snapshot of that meta decision, and every later read of the snapshot reads the meta-controller before this frame's planning as well. The first part has three: the settlement of an option that starts takes its stopping estimate from the snapshot (`TemporalControl.dispatchMeta`, `Skill.settleTemporal`); under the differential criterion the terminal credit of the option that closes takes the snapshot's value of the drawn meta action (`TemporalControl.atBoundary`, `TemporalControl.closeOption`); and the on-policy credit of the meta decision forms its error and stores its lag from that same value (`TemporalControl.learnMeta`, `Controller.policyStep`), with the consequence of the lag below. The second part has one: the off-policy learning of every option that is not executing (PAR-17) compares against a stopping estimate that is read from the snapshot (`TemporalControl.stoppingEstimate`, `TemporalControl.followOptions`, `Skill.followTemporal`), so the stopping decision of such an option's stored trajectory and the stopping value that a stop credits to its policy (`Skill.stopFollowing`) can differ under the two orders at a frame that records a meta decision. The decision also reports the snapshot's values and probabilities to an observer (`Agent.observe`). Two closings in the first part read the meta-controller itself, as the current value function, which under `plan-after-act` holds no planning of this frame: the model of the option that closes under the differential criterion (`Skill.endTemporal`) and the model of a stored trajectory that the settlement stops (`Skill.stopFollowing`). `TemporalControl.takeoverValue` uses the same stopping estimate on a served step only; that step records no meta decision (`TemporalControl.serve_undrawn`), so the estimate is read from the decision's own meta values and the current rate, and the two orders are one step there. No theorem states the difference of one of these reads: each follows from the definitions named, with `TemporalControl.atBoundary_meta` and `TemporalControl.atBoundary_unplanned`. The deferred planning reads the selected state: the meta-controller after that decision's on-policy credit, and the options after the terminal credit, the settlement and the invocation start. Every free dispatch records a meta decision, and on a step whose decision records none the two orders are one step, with the same next agent and decision (`Agent.actOrdered_undrawn`).
- *What does not move.* The assignment refresh precedes the draws in both orders. With learned subtasks it is a function of the Demon-0 weights and the objectives the step started from (`TemporalControl.select_assigns`), so it reads no part of the percept; and a refresh after the draws could reassign the slot of the option whose first action was just drawn, so that the action would come from a policy the slot no longer holds. The terminal credit and the settlement also stay: an option that starts draws its first action from its own policy after both (`Skill.beginTemporal_policy`), so that draw reads weights that this percept's reward has changed. `plan-after-act` is therefore not a complete act-then-learn order. The order in which those two writes follow the action as well is `act-then-learn`, which is PAR-20.
- *Semantic consequence, the draw.* The backups that planning makes at a frame's feature vector no longer inform the meta draw made at that frame; they inform the draws of later free dispatches. With expectation planning the two orders can therefore give different actions and learned values from the first free dispatch. With no planning selected the deferred planning writes no learner and no rate source (`TemporalControl.planFree_none`).
- *Semantic consequence, the lag.* The on-policy credit of a meta decision stores the value of the drawn meta action, read from the decision's snapshot, as the controller's lag (`Controller.valuesStep_lag`). Planning writes weights and keeps the lag (`planning_lags`), and the next credit forms its error from the lag. Under the default order this frame's planning precedes the snapshot the lag is read from, and later planning reaches the lagged vector only where a planned vector shares features with it. Under `plan-after-act` the backup at the decision's own feature vector follows the lag, at every free dispatch that plans. The effect of the stale lag alone has a scalar form. Take one active feature and a linear value, ignore rounding and step-size adaptation, and compare two controllers that have the same weights after a dispatch and the same inputs of the next credit, and that differ only in the stored lag: the value `q` before a planning step `η` toward a target `T`, or the value `q + η(T − q)` after it. With a credit step `α`, the next credit leaves a value that is larger by `α·η·(T − q)` under the lag `q` (argued here, not machine-checked). This is not the complete difference between the two orders. The credit of a dispatch and the planning of the same frame write the same weights and do not commute, so the weights after the dispatch already differ between the orders; that comparison is not derived here. A refresh of the lag after the deferred planning would be a new update rule with its own admission.
- *Draws.* Every draw of an action, of a meta action and of an exploration run is in the first part: `Chosen.learn_rng` proves, for both orders, that the second part returns the action generator the first part left. The tester's replacement draws are in the second part, from the feature generator's own stream. In both orders the first part writes neither the primitive controller nor a prediction demon (`Agent.choose_keeps`).
- *Cost.* One record for each step in both orders. The planning function runs at a free dispatch in both orders; under `plan-after-act` selection also runs the no-planning boundary there, which clears the diagnostic planning errors. Whether the compiled second part or the released loop writes to a learner array that has a second reference, which makes the runtime copy the array, depends on run-time reference counts; no theorem or gate covers it. In the generated C of the two parts no path was seen to keep such a reference (a reading). The number of runtime copies for each step is UNKNOWN: it depends on those reference counts, and it needs a recorded run of each order with its source revision, build, seed and counting method.

**Contract and composition.** The order is a field of `AgentConstruction`. `AgentCallbacks` has the order as an index: a host loop takes the callbacks of its own position of the world's transition, and `runAttempt` selects the loop by that index. `AgentConstruction.State` is the agent of one construction and `AgentConstruction.Image` is a durable image of one construction. This project makes a state by cold initialization, the two parts of the construction's own order (`AgentConstruction.callbacks`, which `AgentConstruction.callbacks_act` relates to the agent's step of that order), the host's bookkeeping, and restoration from an image of the same construction. This project makes an image of a construction in two places: the snapshot of a state of that construction (`Checkpoint.snapshotImage`), and the admission of a decoded payload (`Checkpoint.admitPayload`), which compares the order word of the payload's own header with the construction and takes no order word from its caller. The payload writer takes an image of its own construction and writes that construction's word (`Checkpoint.imagePayload`). A campaign takes the construction and no callbacks: `AgentConstruction.runCampaign` derives the cold initialization and the callbacks. The finite-prefix drivers fold `Agent.act`, the step of the default order, and take a construction of that order only (`DefaultConstruction`, which holds the proof). The prefix takes the agent's events, and its restore event holds a durable image with no construction. The ANSI loop takes callbacks of the default order by type. The functions on the learner state itself (`Agent.act`, `Agent.choose`, `Agent.restore`) keep their signatures. The audit arms, the ANSI view and the viewer's built-in launch construct the default order.

**What the types do not prove.** The learner state and the durable image hold no order, so a state, an image, a chosen value or a callback record is of its order because of the code that made it, not because of a proof inside it. The order is a type index: a state, an image and a chosen value have the type of one construction, and a callback record has its order as an index, so two values of different orders do not meet by accident. No check stops a module of this project from making such a value. A private constructor stops the constructor notation and the constructor name in another module and does not stop a tactic: `by constructor` applies it in any module. The constructors of the image and of the callback record are public. A proof of the order inside each value was examined and is not used: a stored order with a proof that it is the index is new data that a caller sets, with a proof by reflexivity, beside copied functions or copied durable data. The theorems state what a value under the other index gives. A state gives the agent's step of that index's order on the same learner state (`AgentConstruction.callbacks_act`), and a save that writes that index's word (`CurrentCheckpoint.saved_header`). ### PAR-20 · Acting before learning

[Source](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/robotics-and-ai/articles/10.3389/frobt.2018.00079/full): J. B. Travnik, K. W. Mathewson, R. S. Sutton and P. M. Proof owner: `AcornVerif.DrawFirst`.

Under the step order `act-then-learn` the first part of a step makes every draw and takes no reward, the host releases the action, and the second part makes every write that reads the reward of the percept, then the planning of a free boundary (PAR-19), then the updates that follow selection in every order. It completes PAR-19, under which an option that starts still draws its first action after the terminal credit and the settlement that the same percept causes.

**Adaptation and rationale.** The source proposes to "take actions immediately after choosing them given the most recent observation" (§3). Its Algorithm 1 (SARSA, §2) and its Algorithm 2 (Reactive SARSA, §3) both choose `A′` from `S′` with the policy derived from `Q` before the update of `Q(S, A)`; Algorithm 2 also takes `A′` before that update. Selection in Acorn is not in that order. It makes writes that read the reward before it returns the action: the owed meta reward, the terminal credit of an option that closes, the on-policy credit of the meta decision, the settlement and the start of a selected option, and the credit of a continuing option; the primitive draw of a free dispatch follows the meta credit, and under the discounted criterion the terminal credit precedes the meta draw. PAR-19 lists the draws that read such a write. Under `plan-after-act` one is left: the first action of an option that starts, which is drawn after the option is credited and started (the other draws of that order read the primitive controller, a continuing option's policy before its credit, and the meta-controller after the refresh; this is read from selection and is not a theorem). This entry applies the source's order of choice and update to that draw, and moves every write that reads the reward out of the path to the action.

- *The first part takes no reward word.* `TemporalControl.drawFirst` is the dispatch of selection with every draw first, and it has no reward parameter. `Agent.choose_reward` therefore holds by reflexivity: two percepts with one frame and two reward words give the same temporal state, the same decision and the same record of owed writes. So no draw reads a learner write of the reward. The statement does not make the action independent of what the reward is computed from. The first part reads the frame's achievement event (D8): an executing option ends on it (`Skill.goal_ends`), and the dispatch then refreshes, draws a meta action and can start another option. In the grid world the reward word is a function of that event (`StepResult.reward_completion`), so two percepts that world produces with one frame have one reward word. The first part writes what a draw reads and what holds no learned value: the rate schedule, the model caches, the assignment refresh of a free boundary (PAR-12), the trajectory link of an option that a run interrupts, the diagnostic planning errors, occupancy and the action generator. It writes neither the primitive controller nor a prediction demon (`Agent.choose_keeps`).
- *The second part makes the owed writes.* The first part returns a record (`Owed`) that names the dispatch branch and holds its draws. `TemporalControl.settle` makes the writes from it in the order selection makes them: the owed meta reward; on a served step whose run held no option the advance of the deferred meta clock; at a free dispatch the terminal credit of an option that closes, the on-policy credit of the meta decision, and the settlement, the start and the first credit of an option that starts (`TemporalControl.settle_started`); for a continuing option its own credit and the advance of the deferred meta clock. It records no episode, moves no activation and draws nothing (`TemporalControl.settle_framed`, `Chosen.learn_rng`), and it writes neither the primitive controller nor a demon (`TemporalControl.settle_keeps`).
- *The credit of a drawn decision.* The option learner has the credit half of its step as a function of the drawn decision: `Skill.optionCredit` and `Skill.creditTemporal`. `Skill.optionStep_credit` and `Skill.stepTemporal_credit` prove that each existing step is its draw followed by that credit, for every skill, activation, reward word and generator state; the existing functions keep their bodies. The credit reads the action of the decision and nothing else of it (`Skill.optionCredit_action`).
- *Where the step is the step of `plan-after-act`.* `AcornVerif.DrawFirst.selectWithOperations_settle` proves, for every state, frame and reward word, that selection returns the settled state and the same decision when the draw-first decision starts no option and does not both record a meta decision and end an option under the discounted criterion. `AcornVerif.DrawFirst.actThenLearn_planAfterAct` carries it to the whole step: on such a percept the two orders return the same next agent and decision, in every world. The proof moves each owed write past the draws, which read neither the meta span nor an option that the write touches.
- *Declared difference, the first action of an option that starts.* The draw-first dispatch draws it from the frozen policy of the option that the assignment refresh left (`Skill.frozenPolicy`): that option's action values at the start frame under the weights the preceding step left, and the rate its source resolves to (`AcornVerif.DrawFirst.drawBoundary_start`). Both then make the writes of the start with one function, `TemporalControl.startOption` (`TemporalControl.settle_started`), and the drawn decision is the input that this difference changes. Under the per-learner rate policy of PAR-10, which no research profile selects, the first draw reads the option's rate before the invocation reset.
- *Declared difference, terminal credit under the discounted criterion.* Selection credits an option that closes before the assignment refresh under the discounted criterion, and after the refresh and the meta draw under the differential one, where the terminal value is the value of the drawn meta action. The refresh has to precede the draws (PAR-19), and the credit reads the reward, so the draw-first dispatch has the second order under both criteria. What changes is the owner the credit reaches. The refresh retains the original owner of a slot that it replaces (`Closing.oldOwner`), and that owner takes no credit, as under the differential criterion; in selection the credited option is then replaced, so the learned state of that slot is the same. For a slot that is kept, the credit reads the objective with the held bonus that the refresh installed, the model with the ranking that the refresh installed, and the value function after the refresh has reset the rows of replaced slots. Where that option is kept and the meta draw selects it again, the state its start is given differs from the state of selection as well, by this difference.
