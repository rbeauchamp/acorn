# Prior-art admission and research qualification

This is a reference for researchers reviewing the implemented algorithms.
For an introduction, read [the design](design.md#the-learning-loop) first.
**PAR** means prior-art review; each numbered entry connects a mechanism to its
source, Acorn's adaptation, implementation/proof owners and remaining limits.
Some entries describe local constructions inspired by prior art, rather than
an unchanged published algorithm. Equation-level citations live with the code
and proofs.

## Admission standard

The objective is a complete, practical integration of general learning
mechanisms for this repository's continuing setting. A paper's use of a narrow
benchmark does not make its algorithm environment-specific. Reject on an actual
incompatible dependency or assumption, with evidence of where it enters the
mechanism. Reproducing published benchmark scores is an optional diagnostic,
not an admission requirement; reproducing the mechanism and accounting for its
assumptions are required.

**The admission bar.** Five criteria must all pass: **sound** (the derivation
reproduces), **in-setting** (one unbroken stream, batch size one, no replay, no
target network, no resets, bounded work per step), **semantically compatible
and non-degenerate in composition**, **affordable** (`O(active)` work, `O(d)`
memory), and **buildable** (specified precisely enough to implement without
inventing the missing mechanism). A nonzero update is insufficient if it learns
the wrong quantity. A known incompatibility or degeneracy needs a correction
with a formal characterization and closure at the strongest applicable layer
in `AGENTS.md`; disclosure or hoped-for tuning does not clear it.

**Material adaptation contract.** Each entry identifies the source mechanism,
its state, update equations, interfaces and load-bearing assumptions. For each
material change, record:

- **Kind:** equivalent specialization, published variant, proved source
  correction, or local approximation. Name any missing mechanism explicitly;
  a new algorithm is separate scope, not an undocumented integration detail.
- **Reason:** the integration need, why this choice meets it, and why a closer
  published construction is unsuitable or more costly. A change being bounded,
  published somewhere, or already implemented is not a sufficient rationale.
- **Consequences:** the meaning of the implemented quantities and which source
  identities, assumptions and guarantees survive, change or are lost. Identify
  the current indexed type, derivation, Lean theorem or admission gate that owns each
  claimed property; distinguish real-arithmetic identities from float behavior.
- **Composition:** reconcile units, time basis, state ownership, information
  available at the update, learned targets, normalization/reference levels and
  changing policies or models across interfaces. Numerical projections need an
  algorithmic interpretation as well as a state-safety argument. A clipped
  estimate does not inherit the unconstrained algorithm's guarantees.
- **Residual uncertainty:** name what is unknown and why it is empirical or
  outside the supported theorem assumptions. Uncertain usefulness may remain;
  a known contradiction in the claimed mechanism may not. Any measurement
  follows the proof-first and prospective study-plan rules in `AGENTS.md` and the
  [scientific evidence guidance](../CONTRIBUTING.md#scientific-evidence).

Apply this contract proportionately to material choices, in the existing entry;
do not require a new dossier for ordinary integration work. An exact published
variant need not acquire a new whole-system convergence proof to be usable.
An approximation is admissible only when the five criteria still pass, its
target and rationale are coherent, and claims are limited to what its owners
establish. A label alone is not a justification.

**Every verdict carries a recorded refutation attempt** by a fresh-context
reviewer whose only instruction was to break it — what was attacked, what it
found, what survived, and what was changed as a result. An unattacked verdict is
unfinished work.

Each entry has the shape the protocol prescribes: **Claim** · **Derivation**
· **Setting** · **Cost** · **Adaptations** · **Composition** · **Verdict** · **Proof of any
correction** · **Refutation attempt**.

## Default promotion and demotion

Admission permits an explicitly selectable research integration. It does not
promote that integration into an implicit ordinary-runtime selection. An
admission verdict of **adopt** below means the mechanism satisfies its stated
technical contract; current default qualification is recorded separately.
Default selection requires the qualification decisions below.

Before promotion, record all five decisions in the relevant PAR entry:

| Requirement | Evidence required before default use |
|---|---|
| Correct and compatible | Pass admission, close known semantic defects, and name the compiler-backed owner and scope of every claimed invariant. |
| Stated benefit | Define the improvement in goal achievement, adaptation, a necessary capability, or resource efficiency, with stream conditions and horizon. |
| Convincing evidence | Prove derivable claims. Name each irreducibly empirical UNKNOWN and register its estimand, comparator, meaningful margin, uncertainty method and assumptions, population, selection history, and stopping rule before outcomes. |
| Acceptable tradeoffs | Meet declared lifetime memory, latency, stability and performance-loss limits. A benefit on one measure does not excuse a failed constraint on another. |
| Actual composition | Bind the complete source/configuration, initialization, learned state, interfaces and comparison arms. Confirm empirical promotion claims on fresh data after development and selection; identify a reproducible fallback. |

For empirical performance promotion, the appropriate uncertainty bound must
clear a predefined practically meaningful improvement, on the stated estimand.
A positive mean or a small p-value alone is insufficient. Cost or capability
promotion must establish that benefit while satisfying a predefined acceptable
performance-loss margin. Fix the numerical margins for the objective before
observing outcomes; do not invent universal percentages or retrofit margins to
an existing result. Report negative and inconclusive results and account for
multiple comparisons, selection, dependence, missing/failed runs and exclusions.
The [scientific evidence guidance](../CONTRIBUTING.md#scientific-evidence) owns full protocol, registration,
retention and reproduction requirements. Creating a protocol does not authorize
execution; the smallest informative empirical setting remains the last resort.

Uncertainty-aware evaluation is motivated by Agarwal, Schwarzer, Castro,
Courville & Bellemare, *Deep Reinforcement Learning at the Edge of the Statistical
Precipice*, NeurIPS 2021, §4.1, PDF p. 5: “reporting interval estimates”
([paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2021/file/f514cec81cb148559cf475e7426eed5e-Paper.pdf),
opened 2026-09-05). That paper studies multi-task deep-RL evaluation; it does not
validate a particular uncertainty method or sampling model for Acorn. The
promotion margins, fresh confirmation and demotion rules here are repository
policy, not attributed paper results.

| Finding | Default decision |
|---|---|
| Correctness or composition defect | Repair or disable the affected integration regardless of its measured score. |
| Credible underperformance without a previously accepted compensating benefit | Demote it and use the qualified fallback for the affected composition. |
| Missing or inconclusive promotion evidence | Keep it experimental; absence of a demonstrated loss is not promotion evidence. |
| Declared benefit and all tradeoff requirements met | Promote with an explicit decision and evidence record. |

A foundational mechanism needed for the learning discipline may carry an
explicitly accepted cost. The owner must accept the necessity, bounded cost,
evidence obligations and reconsideration condition before outcome access.
“It might help eventually” is not an exception, and an after-the-fact mission
rationale cannot convert a negative result into promotion evidence.

Qualification attaches to an integration in a composition, not to an algorithm
for all settings. Material changes to learning equations, interfaces, features,
reward/control criteria or selection rules require reconsideration. Prove
equivalence when available; otherwise disclose what evidence no longer
transfers and keep the changed mechanism experimental pending qualification.
Review the decision with a fresh-context refutation attempt. A deterministic
audit pins the chosen behavior.

### PAR-1 · SwiftTD

[Source](https://rlj.cs.umass.edu/2024/papers/RLJ_RLC_2024_111.pdf). Execution owner: `Acorn.SwiftTd`.

Adaptive sparse TD with per-feature step sizes. Projection, trace pruning and meta-register re-anchoring are declared adaptations. The contracts below describe their finite-word state and update semantics.

**Adaptation and rationale.** Binary unique ActiveSet inputs specialize the paper’s feature factors to one on the active set; sparse eligible-set iteration avoids full sweeps. The scaled trace increment is bounded by the overshoot budget, not the sum of raw step sizes. Projection admits stored weights; pruning and re-anchoring control finite-state storage but alter the unconstrained recurrence.

**Contract and composition.** AcornVerif.MetaGradient states the hand-transcribed recurrence and discrepancy identities; external transcription fidelity remains assumed. AcornVerif.CurrentLearner and AcornVerif.CurrentLearnerArithmetic link state/update contracts to the executed owner.

**Refutation attempt.** The recorded independent review challenged overshoot, sparse cost, register timing, transcription and bounds. It established that the budget concerns scaled trace increments, that the paper also uses sparse loops, and that re-anchoring withdraws the weight increment. It rejected attributing gamma-derived value bounds to log step sizes. These distinctions limit the claim here.

### PAR-2 · Swift-Sarsa

[Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.19539). Execution owner: `Acorn.Sarsa`.

On-policy per-action learners with semi-Markov credit. The declared Sarsa substitution does not implement every control variant; corrected meta-gradient and duration credit must retain their executed owners.

**Adaptation and rationale.** One persistent value-difference register and per-action learners specialize the step-size machinery to on-policy control. Semi-Markov duration catch-up is needed at meta decisions; primitive credit uses executed actions each learning step. The eligibility set, not only the active feature set, owns update cost.

**Contract and composition.** AcornVerif.CurrentControl and AcornVerif.CurrentTemporal connect to execution. AcornVerif.CurrentPower.pow_one_word supplies the one-step power reduction. Tie handling treats values within the admitted numerical band as tied. The corrected meta-register recurrence is a stated departure from the printed listing.

**Refutation attempt.** Independent review checked the chosen-action update, shared register and one-step reductions, and found the undeclared register deviation, eligible-set cost unit and approximate tie band. The corrected register discipline is the recorded source adaptation.

### PAR-3 · Horde

[Source](http://www.incompleteideas.net/papers/horde-aamas-11.pdf). Execution owner: `Acorn.Demon`.

Parallel GVF prediction enters representation. Cumulants and horizons are declared D5; recursive changing features and policies limit transfer of fixed-policy analysis.

**Adaptation and rationale.** Parallel bounded GVFs specialize the broader Horde architecture; predictions feed other features to support an online representation. Fixed horizons/targets are D5 and on-policy updates replace the full general off-policy construction. Changing predictions, policies and adaptive steps violate fixed-feature/stationary assumptions.

**Contract and composition.** AcornVerif.CurrentPrediction and Acorn.Handcrafted.PredictionControl own bounded predictions and their use. Bucket admission constrains the output range; reachability is a separate property. The policy, representation and step sizes evolve during the run.

**Refutation attempt.** Independent review challenged attribution, convergence hypotheses, target specializations and reachability. It rejected applying fixed-policy analysis with adapted steps and rejected inferring reachability from a range proof. The terms predictions as knowledge describe this implementation rather than a quoted paper theorem.

### PAR-4 · Generate and test

[Source](http://www.incompleteideas.net/papers/MS-AAAIws-2013.pdf). Execution owner: `Acorn.Features`.

Random projection generation over a hand-authored channel layout. Generation and the tester (PAR-11) are distinct mechanisms; only imprint units are replaced.

**Adaptation and rationale.** The fixed-size bank generates random projections to keep storage bounded and construction reproducible. Each unit samples 32 inputs with replacement from the 11 × 11 tile-kind patch followed by the ten task-word positions (D1), hash-binarizes them and thresholds the signed sum at zero, so one unit can conjoin task and layout, which the linear learners cannot otherwise express. Context positions carry the task words in channel order, so a position's meaning depends on the task type in the first positions; a unit separates task types when it also samples those. A replacement draws the generator's next 32 samples (Mahmood & Sutton, p. 3). This is the generator half; PAR-11 owns the tester. Candidate features still have learned output weights, so their fixed input projections do not make utility estimation impossible.

**Contract and composition.** Acorn.Features, Acorn.FeatureHistory and Acorn.FeatureLifecycle admit dimensions, unique active indices and replacement identity. Every unit's projection is determined by its recorded generator origin (`Bank.build_replace`), so the bank is rebuilt from `O(units)` stored words for any number of replacements. `taskWords_fit` proves every task relation fits the generator's context, so no task word is dropped.

**Refutation attempt.** Independent review checked generator/reset mechanics and rejected the argument that fixed random units cannot be tested because they have no learned parameters: their output weights are learned. The admitted mechanism is bounded generation; representation quality is the subject of F3.

### PAR-5 · Reward-respecting subtasks

[Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.03466). Execution owner: `Acorn.Options`.

Skills receive host reward with held stopping bonuses. Ranked hashed slots select candidate subtasks from positive learned Demon-0 weights.

**Adaptation and rationale.** Host reward remains in each skill’s cumulant. Where ranking finds a candidate, an attained feature adds its held bonus to the stopping value: the unit’s positive Demon-0 weight, held from selection and raised at each refresh to the current weight when larger, stands in for the source’s bonus weight, “one of its largest values” (section 2, equation (4), p. 8). The source replaces the feature’s current weight inside the main value estimate; Acorn adds the held weight to the meta-controller estimate. This supports reward-respecting subtasks without a manually selected subgoal list. The hashed-slot weight is a proxy, and the neutral fallback differs from the paper’s nonzero prescribed stopping value.

**Contract and composition.** Acorn.Options and Acorn.FeatureRanking own the stopping/selection definitions; AcornVerif.Options states the return identities. AcornVerif.CurrentTemporal proves that the executed attained stopping value is at least its estimate for every finite estimate within the prediction cap. Bonus and host-value coordinates must match the policy/sample owner.

**Refutation attempt.** Source review checked strict comparison, stopping equations and neutral fallback. It classified the stopping function as an adaptation rather than an exact instance of the paper at zero bonus; subsequent composition review traced the policy/sample ownership instead of inferring learned-value accuracy from return algebra.

### PAR-6 · Potential-based shaping

[Source](https://ai.stanford.edu/~ang/papers/shaping-icml99.pdf). Execution owner: `Acorn.Options`.

Finite-return coordinate identities preserve the stated terminal and bootstrap terms. Shared-stopping identities do not establish host-policy invariance with changing critics or an arbitrary stopping objective.

**Adaptation and rationale.** Potential shaping uses the source’s discount-coordinate relation. Finite activations must include actual terminal/bootstrap terms; subtracting an initial potential is insufficient to establish policy invariance when the stopping objective changes. Inverse coordinates are needed before comparing option and host estimates.

**Contract and composition.** AcornVerif.Options and the executed Acorn.Options definitions own the terminal telescoping and shared-stopping identities. These compare the stated returns under their hypotheses, not arbitrary objectives or moving critics.

**Refutation attempt.** Independent review rejected a finite-telescoping argument used as a policy-invariance proof, corrected the activation-total interval and identified the missing inverse shaping coordinate at interruption. The retained identities concern the actual terminal and common-coordinate quantities.

### PAR-7 · Options and interruption

[Source](http://www.incompleteideas.net/papers/SPS-aij.pdf). Execution owner: `Acorn.Temporal`.

Typed option lifecycle and semi-Markov updates retain actual accumulated reward and duration. Shared units and strict advantage govern interruption.

**Adaptation and rationale.** Option-selecting updates accumulate actual reward and duration at decision boundaries. Trace catch-up is a local extension that decays registers without intermediate increments; it is not attributed wholesale to the options theorem. Primitive control uses PAR-9 credit rather than a second catch-up update.

**Contract and composition.** Acorn.Temporal, Acorn.Handcrafted.AgentEpisodes and AcornVerif.CurrentTemporal own lifecycle/time-base contracts. Acorn.Features.CreditGap supplies register decay identities. Strict interruption compares compatible quantities; inaccurate critics do not gain a policy-improvement guarantee.

**Refutation attempt.** Independent review attacked k-step trace decay and found that the stated no-increment decay composes mathematically. It identified the Dutch-register time-base mismatch and interruption quantity substitution, and separated the local trace extension from the source’s semi-Markov backup.

### PAR-8 · Temporally extended exploration

[Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.01782). Execution owner: `Acorn.Exploration`.

Persistent exploration has a bounded duration. The local capped duration law is D3 and the rate ε is D6's declared 0.01, a value the source uses; support and useful goal discovery are separate obligations from probability admission.

**Adaptation and rationale.** Persistent action runs follow a capped floor-inverse-uniform duration law. It is a tail-equivalent surrogate for the published zeta law, chosen as the explicit local D3 construction; it is not exact source sampling. Serving a run is deterministic, so a universal positive support floor is unavailable at those steps.

**Contract and composition.** Acorn.Exploration and AcornVerif.Exploration own the actual duration/range definitions and stated mathematical distribution scope. The duration and normalization theorems state their action-set and distribution assumptions. Every executed branch compares the first source word's 53-bit fraction with the rate as exact rational order; at D6's rate exactly its binary32 value's share of first words explores (AcornVerif.TemporalSupport). Under a uniform duration draw the mean run length is H₁₂₈ and the expected exploratory share of primitive-boundary cycles is below 6% (AcornVerif.Exploration). Both statements assume uniform, independent draws that the deterministic generator does not supply; the duration model omits the executed reciprocal's binary64 rounding.

**Refutation attempt.** Independent review checked the inverse-uniform off-by-one and normalization argument, and rejected exact-zeta, implicit-cap and overbroad action-domain wording.

### PAR-9 · Intra-option value learning

[Source](http://www.incompleteideas.net/papers/SPS-aij.pdf). Execution owner: `Acorn.Handcrafted.TemporalControl`.

Primitive action learners receive the executed stream, including option steps. This is an on-policy Sarsa specialization with actual terminal/span semantics; it is not the full off-policy intra-option algorithm.

**Adaptation and rationale.** On-policy Sarsa credit updates the primitive learner from each executed learning-step action, including option execution. This uses otherwise discarded experience without replay; it is a local specialization rather than the source’s full off-policy option-augmented Q-learning algorithm. Frozen control excludes updates explicitly.

**Contract and composition.** Acorn.Handcrafted.TemporalControl and AcornVerif.CurrentControl bind credit to the actual action/terminal semantics. AcornVerif.CurrentPower.pow_one_word closes the one-step duration reduction. Greedification with respect to an estimate is not policy improvement with respect to the true value function.

**Refutation attempt.** Independent review traced all credit branches for dropped rewards, wrong actions and duplicate updates. It rejected a source dangling equation reference, a theorem-shaped improvement claim and omission of the option-augmented maximization domain. The one-step power reduction has a universal implementation-linked owner.

### PAR-10 · Derived exploration rate

[Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.01782). Execution owner: `Acorn.Policy`.

The rate is derived from active optimizer state; duration remains D3. No research profile uses it: they use D6's declared rate or the annealed comparison. The native `own` and `shared` rate words still select it. Empty eligibility, optimizer coupling, support and resource limits are explicit.

**Limit.** The rate is the eligible mean of ν(β) = (β − ln η_min)/(ln η − ln η_min). At the initial step sizes it is about 0.63 for primitive and meta learners and 0.64 for options, and every reinstalled option returns to it. The cited paper gives no rate formula; it uses declared constants (D6).

**Adaptation and rationale.** This is a local construction, not a rate formula taken from the cited duration paper. It derives a receiving controller’s probability from active optimizer state to remove a clock schedule without adding a separate intrinsic-reward channel or replay memory. It remains coupled to optimizer parameters; no universal exploration floor or independent tuning claim follows.

**Contract and composition.** Acorn.Policy's `Controller.exploreRate` aggregates the eligible mean of ν over each learner's configuration-indexed rails, and `SwiftTd.ExploreRate.project` admits the rounded probability; Acorn.Exploration only consumes the admitted rate. Empty eligible sets use the declared fallback after transient clearing. Meta rate reads do not occur on every world step, so their cost cannot be amortized as if they did; under off-policy option learning (PAR-17) each option that is not executing reads its rate on every step, and the start that follows one of its stops reads the empty-eligibility fallback. Duration remains D3.

**Refutation attempt.** Independent review rejected comparison against a nonexistent universal support guarantee: deterministic served exploration already lacks it, and removing an annealed floor broadens the limitation. It located the range obligation in the receiving configuration and final rounded mean, not merely individual log step sizes.

### PAR-11 · Generate-and-test tester

[Source](http://www.incompleteideas.net/papers/MS-AAAIws-2013.pdf); [continual backpropagation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13812v3). Execution owner: `Acorn.FeatureLifecycle`.

Every imprint unit carries a contribution utility. Each step one credit accrues per eligible unit, and every 1/ρ credits replace the least useful eligible unit, so turnover follows from the rate by construction. A replaced unit enters with zero outgoing weight in every reader. The rate, maturity threshold and decay are D7.

**Adaptation and rationale.** Mahmood & Sutton's tester "replaces a small fraction ρ of the features that are least useful" and selects only among features whose age has reached the maturity threshold (p. 3). The utility is the contribution utility of Dohare, Hernandez-Garcia, Rahman, Mahmood & Sutton, *Maintaining Plasticity in Deep Continual Learning*, arXiv:2306.13812v3 (2024), eq. (2), §6, p. 19 (published in revised form as *Loss of plasticity in deep continual learning*, Nature 632 (2024)): a running average, with decay η, of |h|·Σₖ|wₖ|, where h ∈ {0, 1} is the unit's own output on the frame and the sum runs over every physically stored learner's weight at the unit's slot (57 learners under discounted control, 60 under differential control; the discounted models' aliased duration position adds no connection). A unit is eligible when its age exceeds m (Algorithm 1, p. 23); Mahmood & Sutton's `ai ≥ µ` admits a unit one step earlier. Ties go to the lower bank index. A unit held as an option objective is eligible only at a free boundary, so no live option loses its objective. The new unit's projection is the generator's next draw, its log step size returns to the initial rail and its per-slot traces clear through SwiftTD's retirement entry. As in the source, which resets only the replaced unit (Algorithm 1, p. 23), every learner keeps its shared previous prediction and weight-change aggregate. Material adaptations:

- *Published variant, rate accrual.* Algorithms 1 and 3 (pp. 23, 44) reinitialize n_l·ρ eligible units per step, with n_l the layer size, and Mahmood & Sutton select ρn of all n features. Acorn follows the authors' released implementation (github.com/shibhansh/loss-of-plasticity, `lop/algos/gnt.py`, function test_features, commit a6b7958), which accrues ρ per *eligible* unit and spends whole accrued units. No credit accrues while nothing is eligible, so the first maturity period ends without a burst of replacements. Turnover by construction therefore holds over eligible units: `run_turnover` gives a replacement within ⌈period/k⌉ tests whenever at least k units are eligible at each. Issue 9's wording over mature units does not hold: away from a free boundary a held unit is mature but not eligible and accrues no credit, so from zero credit, 500 mature units of which 3 are held accrue 9 940 < 10⁴ credits over the ⌈10⁴/500⌉ = 20 tests that wording allows during a live option. Each of the three slots holds at most one unit (`heldCount_le`), so `run_mature_turnover` gives a replacement within ⌈period/(k − 3)⌉ tests whenever at least k > 3 units are mature at each. The two rules agree when every unit is eligible; at the declared settings, once m + 1 steps have passed, at most 52 of 512 units are immature (`run_immature`), so the accrued rate is at least about 0.9 of Algorithm 1's. The credit is an exact integer with period 1/ρ = 10⁴.
- *Equivalent specialization, adaptation utility.* Eqs. (6)–(7) divide by the sum of input-weight magnitudes. Each sample's hash includes its sample index, so every unit has exactly 32 inputs of weight ±1; the divisor is the constant 32 and the utility order is unchanged, so it is omitted. Summing rather than averaging over consumers is also order-preserving.
- *Declared variant, utility form.* Acorn uses eq. (2) rather than the mean-corrected eqs. (3)–(5). The mean correction assumes a consumer bias that absorbs the unit's mean contribution, which the source also transfers into that bias on removal (p. 20); Acorn's linear readers have no bias input, and adding one would change learner semantics. The source's Appendix D reports plain contribution utility as a weaker ablation than the full measure, and the D7 settings come from Algorithm 3, which uses eq. (5). The sum over learners mixes value scales (horizons 10 to 100).
- *Bounded omission, bias correction.* The source divides its running utility by 1 − η^age (eq. (8), p. 21). For every eligible unit that factor lies within 5·10⁻⁵ of one (`maturity_bias`), so the omission can change only which of two utilities within that relative distance is smaller.
- *Composition, hashed slots.* A unit's outgoing weights are the readers' weights at its hashed slot, which a tile-word feature or another unit may share. Zeroing the slot also erases their contribution, and the utility credits the unit with the whole slot weight even when an alias is active in the same frame.
- *Composition, transition rows.* The rows of an option's expectation model (PAR-13) hold weights at ranked positions, not at feature slots. They are not counted in the utility. Replacing a ranked unit vacates its position: its row starts fresh and every other row forgets its weight from that position, and the slot is erased from planning's stored recent feature vectors (PAR-14).

Storage is every unit's generator origin, birth clock and utility, so it is `O(units)` for any number of replacements.

**Contract and composition.** Acorn.FeatureLifecycle owns the tester. `Lifecycle.test_accrual`/`test_balance` prove each test replaces exactly ⌊(c + n)/period⌋ units, where c is the stored credit and n the eligible count, and keeps the remainder; `candidate_least` proves each replaced unit has the least stored utility among eligible units; `test_unheld` proves no held unit is replaced away from a free boundary. AcornVerif.Retirement composes `Lifecycle.advance`, any learner update and `Lifecycle.test` as the agent does: `run_replaced` gives exactly ⌊(c₀ + Σₜ nₜ)/period⌋ replacements over any run, `run_turnover` a replacement within ⌈period/k⌉ tests whenever k units are eligible, `run_mature_turnover` one within ⌈period/(k − 3)⌉ tests whenever k > 3 units are mature, and `run_young`/`run_immature` at most ⌊(period − 1 + L·N)/period⌋ units younger than L steps. AcornVerif.CurrentRetirement proves the local safety of `Lifecycle.replace`: every reader's weight at the replaced slot is positive zero (`replace_zero`), every reader's prediction on an input without the slot is unchanged bit for bit (`replace_predictions`), and adding the new unit's term preserves the numerical value of every finite partial sum under the pinned float model (`replace_neutral`). Every reader keeps its shared previous prediction and weight-change aggregate (`replace_aggregates`, `controller_retire_values`), so each learner's next TD error after a replacement is its unreplaced error formula with only the retired slot's weight replaced by positive zero (`retire_step_error`), and is unchanged bit for bit on inputs without the slot (`retire_step_error_absent`); the controllers' shared error `r + γ·q(a) − vOld` differs only through the same weight. These establish nothing about inputs that contain the slot, where the old unit's and any alias's contribution is removed, nor about cached temporal predictions, which are retained without recomputation. The linkage to `Agent.act` is structural: learners write only the consumer ensemble. Per-step work is `O(N)` utility decay, `O(active units × stored learners)` outgoing sums and an `O(N)` scan when a replacement is due; the encoder already evaluates every unit each step.

**Residual uncertainty.** Whether turnover at the declared rate improves the agent is UNKNOWN: it depends on the stream and on how quickly useful conjunctions are found, which no derivation from the tester's own state settles (F3).

**Refutation attempt.** A fresh-context review attacked citation fidelity, the adaptation kinds, the rate law, utility aggregation, local safety, the held-unit rule and the generator input. Page numbers, quotes and parameter values, the constant adaptation divisor, the bias-correction arithmetic, the learner count and the held-unit rule survived. It found the arXiv locators attributed to the Nature title, the eligible-count accrual attributed to the paper rather than the released code, and undeclared utility-form, aliasing and maturity-boundary differences; each is now cited or declared above. ### PAR-12 · Ranked learned subtasks

[Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.03466). Execution owner: `Acorn.FeatureRanking`.

Streaming ranking over GVF weight slots drives coalesced refresh at a free decision boundary. Unit identity decides which knowledge is invalidated; a unit that stays ranked keeps its slot and learned state, and its held bonus never decreases. Hash collisions and proxy meaning limit interpretation; spatial comparison remains D2.

**Adaptation and rationale.** Only units whose signed Demon-0 weight is positive are candidates; the positive weight word is both the ranking score and the attainment bonus, so a feature predicting less reward is never pursued as an attainment target. Ranking chooses distinct tied blocks. The bonus is held from selection, as the source sets a bonus weight “so that an option can be learned in preparation for the times at which it is high” (section 2, p. 8). While the unit stays ranked, each refresh raises the held bonus to the current weight when that is larger and never lowers it; this running maximum of the weights seen at refreshes is the streaming form of the source’s “one of its largest values” (p. 8). Objective identity is the unit alone. A refresh keeps the policy, model, cached prediction and meta-controller row of the first slot holding each still-ranked unit; every other slot takes the next entrant in slot order or the neutral objective and is reinstalled when its unit changes, so a repeated unit keeps one slot. A held unit retires only at a free boundary, where every slot holding it is released to the neutral objective with fresh policy, model and meta-controller row and a refresh becomes pending, so the next dispatch installs an entrant; a live option keeps its objective, and its terminal credit is never written into a replacement. No two slots hold the same unit; checkpoint admission refuses an image whose slots repeat one. Coalescing requests until a free boundary preserves an ending activation’s objective. The rationale is bounded streaming discovery without an authored subgoal list. Feature variability is not tested; the source constructs subtasks only for features whose weight is sometimes high and sometimes low. The same ranking, continued from three score blocks to one fewer than the ranked width, decides which feature slots each option’s expectation model reads and predicts (PAR-13); every free boundary installs it in every model.

**Contract and composition.** Acorn.FeatureRanking and Acorn.FeatureRefresh own selection/refresh and identity; Acorn.FeatureReferences owns release at replacement. `FreeDispatch.refresh_retains` proves, for every state and Demon-0 weight array, that the assignment refresh leaves the first slot holding a still-ranked unit its policy, model, cached prediction and meta-controller row unchanged and that its held bonus never decreases; `FreeDispatch.refreshModels_retains` and `FreeDispatch.refreshModels_model` prove the same of the complete refresh, whose only write to that slot's model is the current feature ranking, which keeps the reward and continuation learners and the weights of every slot that stays ranked; `FreeDispatch.refresh_distinct` proves that after a refresh no two slots hold the same unit, for every held input, and the full agent’s `TemporalControl.Aligned` carries distinct held units through initialization, every step, retirement and restoration, so `FeatureImage` stores that witness and every typed image round-trips; `FeatureRuntime.retire_releases` proves that after retirement no held assignment names the replaced unit, and `FeatureRuntime.retire_occupied` that away from a free boundary retirement releases nothing; `Bonus` makes every selected bonus positive and finite at every construction and admission boundary. The checkpoint stores the held bonus word; format 16 refuses earlier generations. Nonpositive scores yield neutral skills rather than arbitrary noise.

**Refutation attempt.** Independent review challenged tied candidates, bonus-only changes, early-stream zeros and stopping invariance. It retained block exclusivity and neutral fallback and limited shaping invariance to trajectories with the same stopping value. Invalidating on a bonus-only change would reset a retained unit’s option policy, model and meta-controller row at nearly every refresh, because the Demon-0 weight moves whenever the unit’s trace is active; identity is therefore the unit, and the bonus is held. A bonus held only at its selection-time weight would stay at an early, typically small value; raising it at refresh changes no learned state. Generate-and-test replaces a retired unit’s projection at the same index, so retirement releases its slots rather than letting the replacement inherit learned state.

### PAR-13 · Option expectation models

[Reward-respecting subtasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.03466); [planning with expectation models](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.01191); [planning with expectation models for control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08543); [average-reward options](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.13855). Execution owners: `Acorn.Models`, `Acorn.RankedFeatures`.

Each option has an expectation model. Its reward part predicts the reward accumulated while the option runs. Its transition part predicts, for each feature slot of a small ranked subset, the discounted probability that the slot is active when the option stops. For the share of the outcome's value that the ranked slots do not carry, a shared residual part and one small deviation part per meta action predict each action's share. Differential control keeps a duration part.

**Adaptation and rationale.** Sutton, Machado, Holland, Szepesvari, Timbers, Tanner & White define the reward part r̂(x, o) and the transition part n̂(x, o) ≈ E[γᴷ x(S_K)] of an approximate option model, take both linear, and learn each row of the transition part by TD with cumulant zero and the terminal frame's feature x_j as stopping value (arXiv:2202.03466v4, §4, eqs. (14)–(17), pp. 13–14). Wan, Abbas, White, White & Sutton call such a transition part an expectation model (*Planning with Expectation Models*, IJCAI 2019, arXiv:1904.01191v4).

- *Published rule with a corrected terminal target, rows.* Every row is a SwiftTD prediction learner with cumulant zero whose terminal target is γ times its own slot's indicator at the terminal frame (`Transition.step`, `Transition.terminal`). The source's eq. (17) passes the unscaled x_j to the TD error of eq. (5), which makes a K-step prediction γᴷ⁻¹x_j; eq. (15) defines the transition part with γᴷ. Acorn targets eq. (15), and `option_model_terminal_discount_correction` gives the difference from eq. (17) for every input: (γ − 1)·β·x_j, zero when γ = 1 or the transition is not terminal.
- *Local approximation, ranked subset.* The source's transition part is a d × d matrix; with 16 384 slots that is about 2.7 × 10⁸ weights per option. The Alberta Plan asks for "a ranking of features used both for feature finding and to determine which features are included in the environment model" (arXiv:2208.11173v3, Step 8(d), p. 9). Acorn models the slots of the ranked score blocks of positive Demon-0 weight, the ranking of PAR-12 continued from three entries to k, and each row reads only those slots, so a transition part holds k² weights. The width follows from a memory budget, not from tuning: k is the largest power of two for which the transition parts of all three options together, 3k² weights, fit in one weight vector, which gives k = 64 for 16 384 slots (`rankWidth_budget`, `rankWidth_maximal`, `default_width`). The last position is a constant input (next item), so 63 slots are ranked. Rows over all d slots as input would cost k·d weights per option, 192 weight vectors in all against 57 for every other learner together.
- *Local construction, constant input.* A frame that activates no ranked slot would give every row the empty input and the prediction zero, whatever the option does from there. The last position is therefore reserved: it holds no slot in any ranking, by a field of the ranking's type (`RankedFeatures.bias_vacant`), is active in every input (`RankedFeatures.input_membership`) and is never a predicted outcome (`bias_unoccupied`), so each row has a bias weight. The source's features include no such input; with one-hot features it needs none.
- *Published algorithm applied per row, step sizes.* SwiftTD adapts one step size per weight of one scalar prediction. A transition part is k scalar predictions over one input, and their squared errors add, so each row is an ordinary SwiftTD learner over the ranked positions. Every weight has its own step size and each row keeps its own step-size budget; no matrix-valued adaptation rule is introduced.
- *Local construction, residual per action.* The value function reads every active feature, and a frame activates on the order of a thousand slots, so the value of an outcome read from 63 slots alone would understate it by construction and planning would pull every option's value toward that understatement. For each meta action the remainder is that action's value at the outcome's unranked slots, its unranked share: its value at the terminal frame minus its value at that frame's ranked slots, under the meta-controller as it stands at that frame. The share is learned in two parts whose sum is the share exactly, in real arithmetic and for any shared part (`outcome_decomposition`). The shared residual is the terminal frame's nominal value minus the nominal value of its ranked slots alone; the full-width continuation learner predicts it (`Transition.residual`). The deviation of an action is its share minus the shared residual; one learner per meta action predicts it from the ranked positions and the bias (`Transition.outcome`). Under discounted control, where the nominal value is the maximum, the shared residual is the share of the greedy action whenever the ranked values and the complete values are maximal at the same action, and the deviation of that action is then zero in exact arithmetic. Under differential control the shared residual is a difference of two policy means and no action's deviation need vanish.
- *Local construction, combination before the maximum.* The value of the predicted outcome for one meta action is the current value weights applied to the predicted ranked slots, plus the predicted shared residual, plus that action's predicted deviation (`Transition.outcomeValues`); the maximum or mean over actions is taken after. Between two sets of value weights the exact per-action value, the sum of the stored words before rounding, moves by exactly the weight change at each occupied slot times that slot's predicted activity (`outcome_value_propagation`), and it reads no other value weight (`ranked_values_congr`); no new termination of the option is needed. The executed word is within the rounding allowance of `outcome_value_rounding` of that exact value, so a weight change smaller than the binary32 spacing at the word's magnitude can leave the word as it was. With one scalar residual added after the maximum, a change at a ranked slot would be hidden whenever the ranked values and the complete values are maximal at different actions; `deterministic_outcome` shows, in exact arithmetic, that the per-action form has no such case for a deterministic outcome. A predecessor's own action value moves when planning next backs it up, by its step size; a change in an unranked slot's weight reaches the backed-up value only through new terminations.
- *Declared approximation, deviations.* The differences between actions' unranked shares are predicted from the ranked positions and a bias, 63 slots of about a thousand active ones, and not from the full feature vector. How accurate that is, is UNKNOWN. A full-width learner per meta action would predict each share from every feature at the cost of nine more full-width learners; that alternative is [#47](https://github.com/rbeauchamp/acorn/issues/47). The two designs are not equivalent. With no ranked slot the look-ahead reads no value weight (`ranked_values_vacant`) and each action's value is the shared residual plus a deviation predicted from the bias alone.
- *Declared difference, action values.* Wan et al. show that with a value function linear in the features an expectation model loses nothing against a distribution model, and add that "if we are using an expectation model, then the approximate value function should be linear" (§4, p. 3). Acorn's value function is the nominal value of four linear action values (PAR-2). Each action value of the predicted slots is exactly linear in them (`expectation_linear`); under discounted control the nominal value is their maximum, and the maximum of expected values is at most the expected maximum, with equality when one action is maximal at every outcome (`expectation_maximum_le`, `expectation_maximum_eq`). For an exact expectation model the look-ahead therefore never exceeds the distribution-model backup of the same weights; these are statements in exact arithmetic about the expected features, not about the learned rows. Under differential control the nominal value is the mean under the nominal policy, whose greedy part averages every action within a tie window of the maximum. The tie set depends on the values, so that mean is not convex and no Jensen inequality holds for it. `expected_sandwich` proves, for the executed definition, that it lies between (1 − ε)·max + ε·mean less the tie window and rounding, and that convex combination plus rounding; the executed word can exceed the convex combination by the rounding. `expectation_sandwiched` carries both allowances through the inequality of the discounted case, and `expected_expectation` instantiates it with the executed mean: the mean of expected action values is at most the expected mean plus the tie window and both roundings.
- *Local construction, terminal value.* The shared residual and every share are differences of two readings of one value function at one frame, so both read the same weights and rate: the meta-controller as it stands when the model closes. The policy's own terminal credit takes the policy's terminal word, which under differential control is the value of the next sampled meta action; the model does not.
- *Local construction, change of subset.* Every free decision boundary installs the current ranking in every model (`FreeDispatch.refreshModels`). The subtask assignments of PAR-12 are refreshed only after an achieved attempt or a new curriculum cycle, and the first such refreshes precede every reward, so a model ranking installed only then would hold no slot until a later achieved attempt. A slot that stays ranked keeps its position, its row and that row's weights from every other retained slot and from the bias (`rerank_retained`, `rerank_weights`, `rerank_bias`); a position whose slot left the ranking takes the next entrant or stays vacant, its row starts fresh and every other row forgets its weight from that position through SwiftTD's retirement entry (`rerank_ranked`, `forget_row`, `forget_column`). Retiring a ranked unit vacates every position that holds its slot in the same way, so a replacement unit at that slot inherits nothing (`retire_vacated`). A model holds a slot at one position only: the ranking's type carries it (`RankedFeatures.slot_unique`), installing a ranking requires distinct slots, which the ranking supplies (`rankedSlots_nodup`), and every construction keeps it. A row's terminal target is therefore γ times its own slot's activity at the terminal frame (`indicator_exact`). The deviation learners read the same positions and forget a changed position in the same way (`rerank_deviations`). Only occupied positions and the deviation learners are updated. A slot near the last ranked place can leave and re-enter as the weights move, and its row starts fresh each time; how often that happens is UNKNOWN. The shared residual learner is not reset at a change of subset: until it relearns against the new subset, the backed-up value counts an entrant's share of the value twice as that slot's row learns, and omits a departed slot's share. Each error is at most the slot's value weight times its predicted activity.
- *Declared limit, linear model.* Wan et al. show that planning with the best linear expectation model does not in general reach the solution learning from real data reaches (§5.1, Proposition 5.1). They conjecture that the condition under which TD(0) planning with a linear model converges "might not hold in practice causing the planning to diverge" (§6) and show divergence on Baird's counterexample (§7.1). The source uses a linear model (eq. (16)) and so does Acorn; neither result is discharged here.
- *Declared limit, age.* The rows read ranked slots, which carry no age. The reward, residual and duration learners keep the age-augmented input under differential control.

**Contract and composition.** Acorn.Models owns the parts and their targets; Acorn.RankedFeatures the subset, its width and its position-stable merge; AcornVerif.CurrentPlanning the contracts.

- *Backup identity.* `ranked_value_rounding` proves, for every value function, ranking and predicted slot vector with at most 64 ranked positions, that each executed action value at the ranked slots is finite and within 2⁻¹⁷ + 2⁻¹¹ per occupied position of the exact dot product Σⱼ w[slot j]·n̂ⱼ of the stored words. The ranked value, the shared residual and the deviation are then accumulated in binary64 and the total is narrowed once to the word the comparator reads (`Transition.outcomeValues`). The rounding therefore follows the magnitude of the total and not of its parts: a shared residual and a deviation that cancel lose nothing of the ranked part. `wide_total` proves the binary64 total within 2⁻¹⁶ of the exact sum for every learner state, because every stored weight lies in its rule's domain (`prediction_bound`). No projection is applied to the shared or deviation predictions. The residual is the difference between a value and a sum of up to 64 signed weights, so it is signed and has no value range; a range wide enough never to clip a legitimate target does not tighten the bound, and a narrower one would be a new authored constant that clips learned predictions. `discounted_backup_rounding` proves, for every model state with at most 64 ranked positions and no other hypothesis, that the executed discounted backed-up value is within the per-position allowance plus 2⁻¹⁶ + 2⁻¹¹ + 2⁻¹⁷ of clamp(r̂ + clamp(V)), where V is the exact maximum over meta actions of the per-action values and clamp is the exact projection onto the prediction range. Inside 2¹³ the one narrowing is rounded at the total's magnitude; beyond it the word stays beyond the range on the same side, where both projections are the same endpoint (`outcome_value_clamped`). The differential continuation is a raw binary32 word with no range, and a binary32 result is no closer to its exact value than half its own spacing, so no absolute bound holds at every magnitude. `differential_backup_rounding` proves, for any k up to 20 with every exact per-action value at most 7000·2ᵏ in magnitude, that the executed differential backed-up value lies between r̂ − gain·d̂ + M less the per-position allowance, 2⁻¹⁵ and (3·2⁻¹² + `meanSlack`)·2ᵏ, and r̂ − gain·d̂ + M plus the same with `meanRadius` for `meanSlack`, where M is (1 − ε) times the exact maximum plus ε times the exact mean of the per-action values; the lower side is wider by the tie window. `differential_backup_relative` states it for every model state: for any m bounding the exact per-action values, the factor 2ᵏ becomes max(1, m/3500). The allowance scales with the values the backup returns, not with the stored shared or deviation predictions. Each binary32 product, sum and difference is the exact result rounded once to nearest-even (PAR-16); the binary64 additions, the evaluation of the mean and the narrowings are bounded at every scale in `AcornVerif.CurrentPolicyMean`; the projections select a stored word.
- *Range.* Every predicted slot value is a finite word in [0, 1] by its storage type (`Transition.expected_legal`). `outcome_value_bound` and `differential_target_bound` keep every per-action value and the differential backed-up value finite for every dimension and state.
- *Storage.* `transition_weights` gives k(k + 4) weights per option: k rows and four deviation learners, each over the k positions. The width follows from the budget 3k² ≤ d for the rows alone (`transition_budget`). The complete parts of the three options hold 3k(k + 4) weights: within one weight vector from 1024 slots up (`complete_budget`), 13 056 weights for 16 384 slots (`default_budget`), and more than one weight vector at 256 slots, where k = 8 gives 288. `transition_storage` bounds the retained words of one option's rows and deviation learners by (k + 4)(13k + 2) for any stream length. Outside that budget each option also holds a lookup table of one word per feature slot and k slot words (`transition_table`), and the agent holds the recent feature vectors of PAR-14.
- *Work.* `ranked_input_work` bounds the positions a frame activates by k and by one more than its active features, and `row_work` and `deviation_work` each learner's eligibility list by k, so one model update costs at most k + 4 learners of O(k) work for each option. Each free boundary also ranks the units, at most k − 1 scans of the positive candidates (`rankedSlots_length`), and merges the result into each model in O(k²). A model whose slots change rebuilds its lookup table, one word per feature slot, and forgets the changed positions: each retained row and each deviation learner retires each changed position, and one retirement searches that learner's eligibility list. That is at most (k + 4)·c·k for c changed positions, c ≤ k (`changed_work`), cubic in k when half the ranking changes at once. A model whose slots do not change does neither (`RankedFeatures.rerank`). This is work per option and per boundary beyond the O(active) work of a learner update.
- *Composition.* The transition rows hold weights at ranked positions, not at feature slots, so they are no reader in the tester's contribution utility (PAR-11). Model learners restart cold on checkpoint restore; a restored or fresh model ranks no slot until the next free boundary.

**Residual uncertainty.** What share of an outcome's value the ranked slots carry, how accurate the rows and the deviation learners become under changing option policies, and whether planning with these models is stable are UNKNOWN: each depends on the experience stream and the learned weights, which no derivation from the definitions settles. A measured share near zero would favour ranking slots by their value-weight magnitude, which minimizes the bound Σ over unranked active slots of |w| on the residual.

**Refutation attempt.** The audit campaigns (`acorn-core audit`, one per arm, fixed seed and 512 × 512 world) refuted a first cadence: with the ranking installed only at a subtask refresh, no option held a ranked slot at the end of any arm or at three earlier sampled points of the declared-rate campaign, so the transition part was never exercised; installed at every free boundary, each option holds 62 or 63 ranked slots at the end of each arm. An independent reviewer with no part in the design attacked the claims against the sources and the code. Two findings changed the design. Rows without a constant input predicted zero from every frame that activates no ranked slot, so the look-ahead vanished exactly where the ranked slots say least; the reserved bias position is the result. The ranking does not by itself keep one slot at one position, so retirement now vacates every position that holds the retired slot (`retire_vacated`, `forget_column`). Four findings changed statements: the source's eq. (17) terminal target is x_j and γ·x_j is Acorn's correction; a changed value weight changes backed-up values at once but a predecessor's value only when it is backed up; the storage claim omitted the lookup table and the recent feature vectors; and Wan et al. conjecture divergence in §6 and demonstrate it in §7.1. A second independent review of the finished change found the bias vacancy and the distinctness of ranked slots stated only in prose, and the dependence on the value weights proved only for a model of the sum; the vacancy is now a field of the ranking, distinctness is `rankedSlots_nodup`, and `ranked_value_propagation` is over the stored words. A further review of the committed change confirmed five defects, each fixed here: one scalar residual added after the maximum hid changes at ranked slots when the ranked and the complete maxima were at different actions (per-action deviations, combined before the maximum); the type admitted one slot at two positions (now a field of the ranking); the differential nominal value was claimed convex and is not (the sandwich contract); forgetting changed positions is cubic in the worst case, not quadratic (the bound above); and the rounding contract covered only the discounted target (`differential_backup_rounding`). A second look at those fixes confirmed five more points, each fixed: the rounding contracts had been made conditional on a magnitude nothing enforces (restated at the scale of the prediction envelopes, which the next finding replaced); the expectation inequality ignored the upper rounding of the executed mean (both allowances are carried, and `expected_expectation` instantiates it); propagation was claimed for the executed word where it is proved for the exact value; the zero-deviation remark holds only under the discounted maximum; and the budget statements covered rows only (`complete_budget`). The envelope-scale contracts then showed a weakness in the executed sum: with two rounded additions per action, a shared residual and a deviation that cancel absorb part of the ranked value (for the words ranked 10, shared 2³⁰ and deviation −2³⁰ + 64 the two additions give 64 where the exact sum is 74; a stored deviation is bounded by its envelope of 2¹⁴ at 64 positions, so on stored states with a total inside the prediction range the loss was at most about 2⁻¹⁰, the first addition being rounded below 2¹⁵). Each per-action total is now accumulated in binary64 and narrowed once, those words evaluate to 74, and the discounted contract holds for every model state with no reasoning about the parts. What survived: the width derivation, the rounding bounds and the inequality under the maximum, which is about exact expected features and says nothing of the learned rows.

### PAR-14 · Background planning

[Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.03466). Execution owner: `Acorn.Planning`.

At each free decision boundary every option's meta action value is moved toward its backed-up value, at the current feature vector and at one stored recent feature vector. No stored experience is replayed.

**Adaptation and rationale.** The source's planning is approximate value iteration: w ← w + α[max_o (r̂(x, o) + v̂(n̂(x, o), w)) − v̂(x, w)]∇v̂(x, w), with the current weights w on both sides (arXiv:2202.03466v4, §5, eq. (19), p. 16).

- *Published rule, backup.* Each look-ahead reads the option's model under the value weights the meta-controller holds at that moment and moves that option's action value toward the backed-up value through the SwiftTD planning entry (`PlanningResult.lookAhead`). Look-aheads run one after another, so each reads what the previous one wrote. Signed correction permits downward as well as upward adjustment; projection preserves stored-weight legality and trace registers are unchanged.
- *Declared difference, maximum.* The source updates one state-value function toward the maximum over options. Acorn updates each option's action value toward its own backed-up value, and the maximum is the meta-controller's greedy choice among them (PAR-2). The primitive meta action has no model and is not backed up.
- *Local construction, search control.* The Alberta Plan treats planning as "asynchronous value iteration with function approximation" in which "the order chosen greatly affects planning efficiency" (arXiv:2208.11173v3, Step 9, p. 9). Acorn keeps the 128 most recent earlier feature vectors, one per step of the longest option, so every frame from which an option stopping now could have started is still stored. A learning frame is recorded when it completes, after selection and planning, so the store never holds the frame being decided. Each boundary backs up every option at the current vector and at one stored vector. The position moves against the write direction, one step per boundary (`RecentFeatures.advance`): it reaches every position within one pass of 128 boundaries (`sweep_position`, `sweep_reaches`) and consumes no random draw. Where every step is a boundary, the write position moves one way and the sweep the other, so each stored frame is selected once before it is overwritten, and the two close by two positions per step, so the ages selected share one parity. A position no frame has filled yet holds the empty feature vector; a look-ahead there writes nothing (`plan_empty`) and is still counted by the work counter. Two feature vectors per boundary, the store size and the fixed sweep are domain-general constants of the learned core, like the option count and the option duration cap; they read no property of the world and are not registered as a departure. Priorities and other strategies are separate work.
- *No replay.* The admission standard forbids replay. The store holds feature vectors only: no action, reward or successor, so no stored transition exists to present to a learner again. A stored feature vector is presented again only as the place of a backup, to the meta-controller's planning entry. Every target is computed from the current model and the current value weights, models and predictions learn only from the one stream, and the store is process-local and bounded by its type. A retired slot is erased from every stored vector (`FeatureRuntime.retire_recent`), so no backup writes a replacement unit's weight at a frame its predecessor was active in.
- *Composition, differential control.* The differential backed-up value is the reward part minus gain times duration, plus the nominal policy mean of the per-action values of the predicted outcome, with γ = 1 (PAR-15). It writes no gain.

**Contract and composition.** Acorn.Planning and AcornVerif.CurrentModels bind the schedule to execution: `planning_work` and `boundary_work` give two look-aheads per option per boundary, `planning_clock_bounds` the work counter, `planning_primitive` the untouched primitive row, and `planning_traces` and `planning_lags` the unchanged trace registers and lags of every row at both vectors. A changing value function, learned models and the maximum over action values preclude an inherited fixed-point or convergence guarantee.

**Residual uncertainty.** Whether these backups improve decisions is UNKNOWN (F2).

**Refutation attempt.** The independent reviewer of PAR-13 found that the first search control did nothing: frames were recorded before planning and the position moved with the write position, so the stored vector selected at a boundary was the current one whenever boundaries came every step. Recording at completion and sweeping against the write direction are the result. The no-replay argument survived: the store's type holds no action, reward or successor, and erasure at retirement covers every replaced unit of a tester step. The reviewer also confirmed that backing up each option's action value, rather than one state value toward the maximum, is declared.

### PAR-15 · Differential control

[Source](http://www.incompleteideas.net/book/the-book-2nd.html). Execution owner: `Acorn.Average`.

Reward minus estimated gain times duration supplies the continuing criterion. Gamma is one; existing rails impose a numerical budget. The reward-residual gain update and changing models are specified below.

**Adaptation and rationale.** The continuing criterion uses reward minus estimated gain times duration with gamma one. The reward-residual gain variant is used with a shared control criterion; existing value rails are a local numerical approximation because general differential values have no discount-derived bound. Model queries carry actual policy/sample and activation-age meaning. Under this criterion an option's backed-up value is its reward part minus gain times its duration part, plus the ε-mean of the meta action values of the predicted ranked slots, plus the residual (PAR-13, PAR-14); the transition rows carry no age input.

**Contract and composition.** Acorn.Average, Acorn.Models, Acorn.Planning, AcornVerif.AverageReward and AcornVerif.CurrentModelArithmetic own gain/return identities, the bound obstruction, model targets and stored arithmetic. The two-state family characterizes the obstruction to a transition-independent differential-value bound; the stored rail is an explicit numerical approximation.

**Refutation attempt.** Independent review traced every gain/value write, post-planning action ownership, repeated activation, raw target units and age-zero queries. Discrepancy identities distinguish known semantic incompatibilities from unmeasured usefulness. The integration remains demoted.

### PAR-16 · FloatLib rounding theory

[Source](https://github.com/lean-dojo/FloatLib): R. J. George, W. Adkisson and A. Anandkumar, *FloatLib* (2026), MIT licence, pinned at commit 1e83f09ed8c41a953cf8f93d26c210778177b94a. Proof owner: `AcornVerif.FloatLibBridge`.

FloatLib proves real-valued statements about Lean core's logical floating-point model: on finite operands with a finite result, the model's addition, multiplication and division each equal the exact real result rounded once to nearest-even (the three theorems named toReal_ofModel_add_finite_eq_roundAt, toReal_ofModel_mul_finite_eq_roundAt and toReal_ofModel_div_finite_eq_roundAt in its file FloatLib/Floats/Formats/BinaryInterchange/Arithmetic/LeanModel.lean), and its rational rounder returns the nearest-even rounding of a fraction (toReal_roundRatScaled_eq_roundAt). Acorn's binary32 and binary64 arithmetic is that same core model applied to raw words, so these statements are about the definitions Acorn executes.

**Adaptation and rationale.** This is a proof dependency, not a mechanism of the agent. No executed definition, native binary or audit pin changes, and no executing library imports it.

- *Proved connection, representation.* The bridge views the same bits as FloatLib's model. Both sides unpack them with Lean core's decoder, so the map agrees with the existing decoded reading by definition, and the two numerical readings and the two finiteness predicates are proved equal on every word.
- *Proved connection, operations.* For both widths, the executing addition, subtraction, multiplication and division on finite operands with a finite result are proved equal to one nearest-even rounding of the exact real result. FloatLib's multiplication and division theorems assume an exponent inequality that it does not discharge; the existing lemma `model_product_exponent_ready` supplies it for canonically unpacked operands and `model_divCore_ready` for every pair of nonzero finite operands.
- *Proved connection, division with no significant quotient bit.* FloatLib's division theorem also assumes a nonzero provisional quotient. Lean core's division core returns a zero provisional quotient with a nonzero remainder when the exact quotient lies below the exponent it selects, for example the least positive subnormal divided by 1.5. `roundWithAccuracy_zero_roundAt` proves the zero case from the remainder's accuracy record, adapting FloatLib's private fraction lemma as `fraction_accuracy` and the scaling tail of its division proof under FloatLib's MIT notice, which the bridge module carries, so `binary64_div_roundAt` and `binary32_div_roundAt` carry no magnitude hypothesis.
- *Why not replace the executing arithmetic.* FloatLib's verified arithmetic is software arithmetic in modules that import Mathlib. Its compiled functions reach the recursors Mathlib compiles for Bool, Option and List, so an executing library that called them could not keep its checked execution claim under the [Regula audit](verification.md#regula-audit), and it would exchange hardware arithmetic for software arithmetic.

**Contract and composition.** AcornVerif.FloatLibBridge is the only importer; the boundary audit refuses a FloatLib import anywhere else, and refuses any import of it by an executing module. `binary64_div_half_unit_error` bounds the executing binary64 division by half the grid spacing under the hypotheses of `binary64_div_finite_error`, one quarter of that theorem's radius. `nearestEven_eq_roundQuotientEven` identifies Acorn's integer nearest-even quotient with FloatLib's. For each decimal constant in Acorn.Constants a theorem such as `band030_nearest` states that the word is the decimal rounded once to nearest-even binary32. Every theorem of the bridge depends only on propext, Quot.sound and Classical.choice. Statements concern finite operands and finite results; exceptional words and the native primitive correspondence stay outside them.

**Residual uncertainty.** The ideal-function error of the local exponential and logarithm remains unproved; the bridge supplies the per-operation statements that proof needs. FloatLib is young and has one committer, so the dependency is pinned to a commit and confined to one module.

**Refutation attempt.** Before adoption the library at the pinned commit was built whole on the pinned toolchain and audited with Regula v0.3.1, claimed at standard-logical with checked execution. The attack looked for holes, project axioms, compiler-trusting proofs, unproved runtime replacements and theorem hypotheses that Acorn's definitions do not meet. All 28,623 declarations use only propext, Quot.sound and Classical.choice, with no hole and no compiler-trusting proof. The audit found that its compiled functions fail checked execution through Mathlib's compiled recursors, which is why no executing module may import it. Reading the three operation theorems against Acorn's wrappers found the undischarged exponent hypothesis and the nonzero-quotient premise; the first is supplied by existing lemmas and the second is removed by the zero-quotient theorem above. This record is the adopting investigation's own; an independent review of this entry is still owed.

### PAR-17 · Off-policy option learning

[Tree backup](http://incompleteideas.net/papers/PSS-00.pdf); [intra-option learning](http://www.incompleteideas.net/papers/SPS-aij.pdf); [reward-respecting subtasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.03466). Execution owner: `Acorn.Temporal`.

On every step of a learning hierarchy, each option that is not executing learns from the action the agent actually took. Its action-value policy takes tree-backup credit on every such step, and its model learns along runs of frames whose action was selected with the option's own distribution. The executing option keeps its on-policy update, and terminal credit keeps its owner.

**Adaptation and rationale.** Sutton, Machado et al. update every subtask's option and model on each nonterminal step, stopping where the option's own stopping function does, with the importance ratio ρ = π(A|S)/μ(A|S) on the trace (arXiv:2202.03466v4, §3, eq. (10), p. 11; §4, eq. (17), p. 14). Acorn keeps the every-step schedule, applies its own stopping decision (PAR-5, PAR-7) and replaces the ratio:

- *Published variant, policy credit.* The option policies are ε-greedy action-value controllers (PAR-2). Each takes tree-backup credit: Precup, Sutton & Singh, *Eligibility Traces for Off-Policy Policy Evaluation*, ICML 2000, §4, Algorithm 2, p. 4. The error is r + γ Σₐ π(a|s′) q(s′, a) − q(s, a), and earlier traces decay by γλ·π(a′|s′) for the action a′ taken at s′; π is the option's own nominal ε-greedy mass at its frozen values. The ratio methods "require that it be known, Markov (purely a function of the current state), and explicitly represented as action probabilities" (§4, p. 4, of the behaviour policy). Acorn reports a nominal mass for each action on every frame, but the behaviour is not Markov, and a served exploratory run (PAR-8) is a point mass fixed by history: it gives zero probability to actions the option would take, so a ratio estimator has no coverage there, and where the behaviour is ε-greedy over nine actions the ratio reaches 9/ε. Tree backup "requires nothing of the behavior policy other than that it be non-starving" (§4, p. 4). An action value is conditioned on its own action, so a trajectory's first action needs no correction. The source's experiments divide each trace by the largest target probability so that traces do not decay too quickly (p. 5); Acorn applies Algorithm 2 as printed, because that division is a state-dependent λ that can exceed one, outside Theorem 3's hypothesis.
- *Published rule, model credit.* The models are state-value learners (PAR-13), so tree backup, which needs a value for each action, does not apply to them, and the published correction is the ratio of eq. (17). Sutton, Precup & Singh call an action consistent with an option when it "was selected with the distribution π(s_t, ·)" and apply the model updates "to every option consistent with every action taken" (AIJ 112 (1999), §5, eqs. (18)–(19), p. 202). Acorn's option policies are stochastic, so the test is on distributions: a frame is consistent with an option when the masses the behaviour reported for that frame equal the option's own nominal masses, word for word. On such a frame the ratio of eq. (17) is one for every action, exploratory ones included, so the model takes its ordinary update and its traces carry through, as eq. (17) prescribes at ρ = 1; on any other frame the model's trajectory is cut. Because the test reads the two distributions and never the action drawn, the cut is not selected by the action. The model's target is therefore the option's ε-greedy policy as executed, the same target its on-policy updates have. A served exploratory step reports a point mass and is consistent with no option that gives two actions positive mass.
- *Why not the ratio elsewhere.* Where the two distributions differ, eq. (17) needs ρ itself. It reaches 9/ε, about 900 at the declared rate, on an action the behaviour explored into and the option prefers. SwiftTD bounds each update by a step-size budget computed without a ratio, and binds its weight projection and re-anchors the step size when an update overshoots; a trace scaled by such a ratio would do that on every active weight. Clipping the ratio to one is not a remedy: the clipped estimator's fixed point is the policy proportional to min(μ, π), which is close to uniform wherever the behaviour and the option prefer different actions. Two narrower tests were considered and rejected: treating every tied greedy candidate as consistent teaches the model the behaviour's choice within the tie, and requiring the taken action to be the unique greedy one targets the greedy policy, not the executed one, and learns nothing while values tie.
- *Local construction, trajectory state.* Each skill stores the trajectory of its learners while it is not executing: its age, the number of actions followed since it began; whether the model has a live trajectory; and the potential observed at the preceding frame. The age advances on every followed frame, so the executing option's stopping decision (goal, 128-step cap, strict interruption) applies to the stored trajectory unchanged, and a model trajectory that starts partway through reads the option's age at that frame.
- *Published variant, stops.* When the stopping decision fires, the policy is credited the stopping value with trace decay zero, the factor γλ(1 − β) of the source's update procedure at β = 1 (§3, eq. (5) and p. 9), and a live model trajectory is closed toward the terminal targets of PAR-13. Every remaining trace is then released structurally, in work proportional to their number, and the option continues from the current frame with the action taken, as eq. (10) continues after β = 1. A stop does not use the full-width transient clear an invocation start uses, because a stop can occur on every frame for every option.
- *Local construction, settling at an invocation start.* An option the meta-controller selects was following until that frame. Before its invocation start clears its trajectory state, the completed transition is credited exactly as on a followed frame: the stopping value where the stopping decision fires, the tree-backup error otherwise, and the model's step along a live trajectory. No transition a followed option has seen is discarded uncredited.
- *Missing mechanism, off-policy SwiftTD.* No off-policy SwiftTD or Swift-Sarsa is published. The correction enters only through arguments the learner already accepts as raw words, the error and the first-loop trace decay, so every existing learner contract (legality, eligibility capacity, schedule) applies unchanged. The learner gains one entry, the structural release of its eligible traces, which writes no knowledge.

Consequences. Algorithm 2's convergence result (Theorem 3, p. 5) concerns tabular, offline updating toward a fixed target policy; none of it transfers to linear function approximation, online updating, adaptive step sizes or a target policy that is ε-greedy in its own changing values. A taken action outside the candidates multiplies earlier policy traces by ε/9, and one of k tied candidates by about 1/k, so off-policy policy credit is close to one-step until one action is uniquely greedy and the behaviour takes it. The meaning of SwiftTD's meta-gradient registers under the corrected decay, and with an expected bootstrap beside a sampled lag, is not established. The source's model rule also requires a Markov option (§5, p. 201); Acorn's options stop at an age cap, and under discounted control the model input carries no age. Consistency compares nominal masses: two controllers have equal masses exactly when their rates and tied candidates agree, so a profile whose primitive and option rates differ, such as the annealed comparison, has consistent frames only while another option with the same candidates is executing. The stopping estimate is the nominal value of the frame's meta-controller snapshot in both criteria: the maximum under discounted control and the ε-mean under differential control, where the executing option's terminal credit uses the value of the next sampled meta action; no meta action is drawn for an option that is not executing.

**Contract and composition.** Acorn.Temporal's `Skill.followTemporal` owns one option's frame, Acorn.Policy's `Controller.backupStep` the tree-backup credit and Acorn.Handcrafted.TemporalControl's `followOptions` the stage, which runs after selection and before primitive credit, so every option reads the same pre-observation gain as the executing one and consumes no random draw. `Skill.settleFollowing` runs inside selection, where the meta-controller starts an option.

- *Reduction.* `Controller.valuesStep_eq_creditStep` proves the on-policy update is the shared kernel at its sampled bootstrap, for every state and raw input word. `follow_executing` and `step_executing` prove the executing option's policy and model after the stage are exactly those selection left. `Skill.settleFollowing_unlinked` proves a skill linked to no frame is settled to itself, so the start of an option that was executing, fresh or restored is the existing on-policy start.
- *Bounded correction.* `PolicySnapshot.mass_bounded` proves the policy's trace correction is a stored probability in [0, 1] for every snapshot, including nonfinite value words. `PolicySnapshot.consistent_mass` proves that on a consistent frame the behaviour's reported mass equals the option's for every action, so the model's ratio is one wherever it is defined. Issue 10 expected a ratio bound of 9/ε; no ratio other than one is used, and a served step is never consistent with a stochastic option, so served exploration needs no separate bound.
- *Work.* Each option that is not executing costs about one executing-option step, so option learning goes from at most one such step per frame to three. `follow_inactive` proves frozen and primitive-only profiles do no option work and `follow_frame` that the stage writes only the skill table. Every learner keeps an eligibility list within the feature capacity by its storage type (`follow_legal`); that is a bound in the capacity, not in the active features. `stop_step_empty` and `stop_trajectory_empty` prove a stop leaves every policy and model learner nothing eligible, for every state and every trace word, finite or not, because its last entry is the structural release (`release_empty`). `release_idle` proves the first loop that follows a release is the identity, and `release_knowledge` that a release writes no weight or step size, so a start credits no earlier transition. A stopping frame therefore makes one first-loop pass over each eligibility list and one release pass over whatever that loop retained, which is nothing when the traces are finite (`first_loop_stop`, from `mul32_zero`); a continuing frame makes one first-loop pass. The count of loops per frame is read from the definition; it is not a theorem.
- *Consistency and age.* `PolicySnapshot.consistent_iff` identifies the test with equality of the two mass vectors and `PolicyDecision.consistent_own` proves a policy's own decision is consistent with it. `follow_age` proves the stored age advances by exactly one on every continuing frame, whatever the action, and `follow_cap` that a trajectory at the option's full duration ends at the next frame, so policy learning cannot run past the cap. `follow_live` proves the model trajectory is live after a frame exactly when that frame was consistent; `follow_live_model` that along a live trajectory the model takes exactly the executing option's continuing credit; `follow_idle_model` that with no live trajectory an inconsistent frame neither credits nor restarts the model; `follow_policy` that the policy takes tree-backup credit with the shaped cumulant the executing option would have read.
- *Settling.* `settle_continuing` and `settle_ending` prove an option that starts executing first takes the credit a followed frame would have given, and `Skill.settleFollowing_owner` that settling keeps the objective and leaves no stored trajectory.
- *Frozen estimate.* `stopping_estimate_frozen` proves that when a meta decision was drawn at the frame, the stopping estimate reads that decision's own snapshot, values and rate, so a rate read after the meta learner's update cannot enter it.
- *Ownership.* The trajectory is a field of the skill. Fresh, released and restored storage carries none (`FreeDispatch.install_unlinked`, `Ensemble.release_unlinked`, `Ensemble.restore_unlinked`), so the first frame under a new objective starts a trajectory and credits no earlier transition; a still-ranked unit keeps its slot and its trajectory, since its objective is unchanged. `TemporalControl.followOptions_aligned` carries learned interests and distinct held units through the stage, and terminal credit of an executing option is assigned as before.
- *Planning.* Planning (PAR-14) reads every option's model at each free boundary. A model that has seen no consistent frame and no execution is still zero, as before. A model that closes off-policy reads the meta-controller as it stands at that frame for its residual target (PAR-13), while the policy's stop keeps the frozen stopping estimate.
- *Checkpoint.* The trajectory is process-local, like every trace, so the format is unchanged and a restored agent starts each option's trajectory at its first frame.

**Residual uncertainty.** How often a frame is consistent with an option that is not executing, how often its stopping decision fires there, and whether option policies and models improve are UNKNOWN: each depends on the experience stream and the learned values, which no derivation from the definitions settles.

**Refutation attempt.** Two fresh-context reviews attacked the entry. The first, instructed only to break it, attacked the update order against Algorithm 2, frame alignment on every selection branch, stale lags, the stopping decision, the model rule, ownership at every skill boundary, each theorem's statement, composition with planning and cost, and checked every citation. The update order, the alignment (no frame is credited twice or to another objective), ownership and the citations survived. It found that the model's test then admitted every tied candidate, so the model learned the behaviour's choice within a tie; that the stop bounds rested on an assumed zero product; that a start read a stored accumulator; and that the ratio rationale, the Markov requirement, the stopping age and the trace cut were overstated or undeclared. The second review found that the unique-greedy test adopted in response rejected actions the executed ε-greedy policy supports and trained the model toward a different policy than its on-policy updates; that an option starting to execute discarded the transition it had followed; that the model's run length drove the policy's duration clock, so policy learning could run past the cap; that the stopping estimate paired frozen meta values with a rate read after the meta update; and that the stop bound assumed finite traces, which admission does not enforce. The test is now equality of the behaviour's and the option's distributions, the settling step credits the followed transition, the trajectory age advances on every frame, the estimate reads the decision's frozen snapshot, and a stop releases its traces structurally; each is stated above with its theorem. Stability of tree backup with linear function approximation and the meta-gradient semantics were not settled by either review.
