# Roadmap

This page says what Acorn works on now, in what order, and why.
GitHub's issues are the record of each piece of work; this page is the order between them.
It was last set on 2026-11-18, and a change to the order changes this page in the same pull request.

## Terms

- The *mission theorem* is the theorem the [mission contract](mission-contract.md) asks for: that Acorn's agent, as running Lean code, achieves goals over a formally stated class of big worlds. It is one theorem for each class.
- A *rung* is one such class of worlds, with its theorem. The classes form a ladder, from the first and smallest toward "any big world". Each rung has one tracking issue, labelled rung.
- An *obligation* is a numbered item of the mission contract's [open obligations](mission-contract.md#open-obligations): a statement not yet precise, a theorem not yet proved or a choice not yet made.
- A *lane* is a kind of work. On a rung there are three: *state* (write a contract, a theorem or a premise exactly, in Lean), *discover* (find the algorithm for which the rung's theorem is true) and *prove* (prove a stated obligation of the executing code). Four more group the rest: *world*, *theory*, *community* and *upkeep*.
- A *parked* issue is not on the active rung. It stays open, and its last roadmap comment says when it re-enters.
- A *mission*, as a unit of work, is an issue with a frozen contract that outside contributors and their agents can land ([missions](missions.md)).

## Where the mission stands

Two theorems say what Acorn's executed agent does on the visit after its first rewarded one, and they disagree. Both are from the agent's cold state, at places that are worlds of its interface and not the host's world, for percepts that meet the conditions the theorems state, and both end at the first decision after the first reward. At a place one decision long the agent takes again the action its first reward followed and is paid again (`rung_door`). At a place of two steps whose second percept carries every word of the first, it begins its second visit with the second of the two rewarded actions and is not paid (`rung_lock`). No theorem says that the executed agent acts better for what it has learned on a class of the ladder, at a state that has been rewarded before, or on a visit later than that one.
On the first rung, whose world, visit length and agent are fixed, `rung_mission` proves the mission theorem's statement from eight premises about the executed agent. Two are proved: the one on memory, for the agent with its whole state as its memory (`rung_memory_bounded`), and the one on finding, that the agent solves more members than the covering bound, each on one of its first 72 visits (`rung_finds`). Finding is a result about how the agent explores before any reward, not about what it learns from one. No theorem proves the other six: that the agent solves again what it has solved on every later visit on which it explores at no decision, two counts of those visits, and three of contract 3's four premises. On 2026-11-18 the owner decided which memory the statement reads: what the agent's decisions read, so that contract 3 is read, and is to be proved, of an agent whose memory is the executed agent's learned state, without its evaluator's session ([#249](https://github.com/rbeauchamp/acorn/issues/249)). That agent is not yet constructed, and no theorem says that it takes the executed agent's actions; both are the fifth step of [#215](https://github.com/rbeauchamp/acorn/issues/215). What remains is the six premises, that construction and proof, a proof that the agent's reports to contract 3 describe its step, a statement for an agent that explores with random draws, and the link from the rung's run to the native entry.

- **Proved.** The kernel's model of worlds and agents. That no agent achieves goals in every world, and that on a class which conceals its goals every agent solves at most a covering bound of members on a first visit. One class of worlds that meets the mission contract's conditions (a) to (c), which make it a concealed class and not a big one (`witness_concealed`). That every agent which keeps nothing of its experience solves at most a covering bound of members on every visit (`visits_need`). That a reference agent which replays a route it has found separates from those agents and retains what it solved, under its own hypotheses (`witness_separation`, `replay_keeps`). That the host's loop of visits is the kernel's (`executed_visits_closed_loop`). Exact resumption from a checkpoint, and that no host write reaches learning.
- **Proved of the mission theorem's statement.** The statement is one proposition over the four intent records (`Mission`), and on a class of worlds in visits it follows from named premises about the class and the agent (`visits_mission`). On the first rung's class every premise about the class holds, the reward lemma included (`find_mission`, `classReward_eq`). A reference agent of fixed memory meets the whole statement there, with every premise discharged (`witness_mission`). The statement reads its promises before a visit the class names, its *horizon*. The advantage comes on a visit that leaves more later visits before the horizon than the *lapses* the class allows, and the agent solves again what it has solved on every later visit before the horizon but for at most that many of each member's visits (`SeparatesBy`, `RetainsBut`). The reference agent meets it with no lapse, at every horizon of at least four visits.
- **Proved of the first rung's constants.** The rung's world is the standard configuration at seed 1 and side 64 with no deer and no food (`witness_standard`), started at the box's center (`witness_start`); a visit is seven steps; the agent is the cold agent the host constructs for the primitive research profile (`rungConstruction`). The class is concealed there (`witness_concealed`), with at least 147 members (`witness_mission`) and at most 392 (`rung_members`) against a covering bound of 98. Every frame of the rung's agent holds at most 1712 slots, so the credit horizon holds of it with no hypothesis on frames (`rung_frame`, `rung_weight_rests`), and on the controller's cold run a slot laid at most seven steps before a first reward has a weight the reward moves (`rung_reach`).
- **Proved of the executed agent's exploration.** Take a profile with no hierarchy (primitive actions only, with no options and no meta-controller) under the declared exploration rate (the fixed rate of [departure D6](learned-only-binding.md#d6--exploration-rate--step-9)), which the primitive research profile is. At the default step order, from cold initialization and up to the first reward word that is not zero, its agent takes the actions of an *explorer*: a machine whose whole state is the exploratory run it has committed to and the action generator, and which reads no percept (`cold_actions`, `Explorer.step_eq`). On a class that conceals its goals behind a *reference* (one world whose percepts every member shows until its goal is first met), an agent solves a member on some visit exactly when the actions of some visit of its run in the reference, taken in that member, meet the member's goal (`Conceals.found_iff`). In the first rung's reference no reward is ever paid (`reference_unpaid`). So whether the rung's agent finds is a property of one action sequence (`rung_finds_iff`), and Lean's kernel evaluates enough of it: the agent solves each of 102 members on one of its first 72 visits, against the covering bound of 98 (`rung_finds`).
- **Proved of the executed agent's controller.** Its control weights stay zero until its first reward (`pre_reward_weights_zero`). What a bias on a few slots then does to the greedy choice (`candidates_of_gap`, `candidates_of_resting`), and how far back a first reward reaches (`creditReach`). On the first rung, at a member's first success within the rung's agent's first 1000 decisions, the reward's credit is written at the start of that visit: right after the step that learns the reward, every slot of the frame the agent laid at the visit's first decision holds, in the row of the action taken there, a weight above the tie window, the margin within which the greedy choice treats two values as tied (`rung_credit`). That step turns into weights the *traces* the agent's earlier steps left, a trace being what a step lays on the slots of its frame for the action it took and what every later step decays (`rung_weights`). The agent's next action is then decided as before the reward, with the totals of those traces on the frame it reads in the place of values that were all zero (`rung_choice`). Where the agent's actions before the reward were pairwise different, each total is the sum of one word, taken once for each slot the frame the decision reads holds of the frame the action was taken on (`loop_totals`). The last action taken is then the only action whose total is within the tie window of the largest, where the decision's frame holds a slot of its frame and at least as many as of each earlier one (`last_candidate`). On the rung that covers a first success on the first visit, which is at its second or third step (`rung_first_visit`). The decision's frame holds a slot of every earlier frame there (`rung_frames_share`); that it holds at least as many of the last action's frame as of each earlier one is a hypothesis of `rung_early_repeat`. It is proved for a first success at the second step, where the two frames laid before the reward are one frame (`rung_frame_kept`). Exactly seven members have such a success, and after each the agent's next action is the step south that met the target, which is not its explorer's action at that time, to eat (`second_step_repeats`, `second_step_codes`). For every other first success no theorem says which action it is.
- **Observed.** Two registered studies on the standard curriculum, each refuted at its horizon ([baseline assessment](baseline-assessment.md#conformance-sequence), U6 and U7). They describe the code before a later change to the hashes, and neither ran on the first rung's class.
- **Open.** The mission theorem, on every rung.

On 2026-11-05 the owner directed that all effort goes to Acorn's mission, with supporting work only for bugs, problems of velocity and issues already planned.
Under the mission contract, Acorn's results are theorems about running code; a run of the agent is a smoke test and never evidence.
The order below follows both: one rung is active, and work that no active rung needs is parked.

On 2026-11-17 the owner reviewed the work against [Oak Lab's mission](https://web.archive.org/web/20260909143403/https://oaklab.ai/mission) (the page as archived; Oak Lab has since withdrawn it), whose goal is "to discover and implement algorithms that allow agents to achieve goals in big worlds", and decided five things.

- **The discovery step goes to the algorithm.** The theorems about the unchanged agent end one decision after a first reward, on the rung for seven members and outside it at a door of one step and a lock of two (`rung_door`, `rung_lock`), and retention is decided at the next visit, for every member the agent has solved. So no further theorem is sought about what the unchanged agent does decision by decision after a reward. Questions 3 and 4 of [#232](https://github.com/rbeauchamp/acorn/issues/232) close by a change with its theorem, which that issue's completion allows.

The owner declined a budget for diagnostic runs.
- **Retention is asked visit by visit**, in two parts ([#248](https://github.com/rbeauchamp/acorn/issues/248)). On a later visit in which the agent takes no exploratory action it solves the member again; that part is a statement about every run. The other part counts the visits in which exploration fires. The postcondition's record and the rung's theorem state it so (`keepsSolving`, `rung_mission`): the agent solves again what it has solved on every later visit before the class's horizon, but for at most the class's number of lapses of each member. The owner also decided that the advantage is read before the horizon, on a visit that leaves more later visits than the lapses (`earnsMoreInTime`), so that an agent which succeeds once and never again does not meet the statement (`rulesOutAdvantageOnce`).
- **Open statements live in the governed tree**, in one directory that `.github/CODEOWNERS` names ([#238](https://github.com/rbeauchamp/acorn/issues/238)).
- **Work beside the retention work is handed over one piece at a time**, each to a parallel session that works from a written brief. The first was the next increment of the work bound ([#90](https://github.com/rbeauchamp/acorn/issues/90)), which has landed (`followOptionsCosted_within`). The second is the mechanism for open statements ([#238](https://github.com/rbeauchamp/acorn/issues/238)). The statement of once-only learning ([#233](https://github.com/rbeauchamp/acorn/issues/233)) stays with the retention work, because it still has to be written. Neither [#90](https://github.com/rbeauchamp/acorn/issues/90) nor [#233](https://github.com/rbeauchamp/acorn/issues/233) has a statement ready to seed: the rung's premises on work and on learning once are about what the agent reports of its steps, and what the two issues owe is the link from those reports to the executing code. No announcement is made before at least 20 open statements are seeded, most of them on the path ([#237](https://github.com/rbeauchamp/acorn/issues/237)).

## The active rung: concealed targets in visits

The first rung is the class of concealed targets of one grid world, run in visits ([#230](https://github.com/rbeauchamp/acorn/issues/230)).
A *concealed target* is a goal whose location the agent's observation does not show until the agent reaches it.
A *visit* is one attempt of a fixed length, after which the world returns to its start and the agent keeps its memory.
The rung's theorem says that the executed agent solves more members of the class on one common visit than any agent that keeps nothing of its experience can on any visit, that it solves again what it has solved on every later visit before a stated visit, the class's horizon, but for a stated number of each member's visits, with the common visit early enough before the horizon to leave more later visits than that number, and that it does both within fixed work and memory while learning from each experience once.

The replaying reference agent already separates and retains on this class, under its own hypotheses (`witness_separation`, `replay_keeps`), and a reference agent of fixed memory that remembers which of its exploring walks met the goal meets the rung's whole statement (`witness_mission`).
So the statement alone does not tell a continual learner from an agent built to memorise a route in this world.
The rung's value is that Acorn's general agent, with nothing specific to this world, meets it within the contract's invariants.
The first rung on which no agent within the stated bound can keep every solution is capacity, condition (d) of the mission contract, which since 2026-11-18 has five parts, (d1) to (d5), and is the first rung that may be called big.
Being big does not say that a class needs perceiving, which is the contract's separate condition (e); a rung on which the mission theorem is claimed as a result about learning from what the agent perceives is asked to meet (e) as well.

### What the rung's agent finds

The owner decided on 2026-11-15 that the rung's premise on finding is certified by Lean's kernel, at the cost recorded here (`rung_finds`).

- **What is proved.** The rung's agent solves each of 102 listed members on one of its first 72 visits: each lies within three tiles of the body's walk on visit 45, 47, 53 or 71, counted from zero (`rungWalks`, `rungFound`). It is proved of the agent's one run, with no assumption on the generator's words. Up to a first reward the agent's actions are the explorer's (`cold_actions`), no reward is paid in the reference (`reference_unpaid`), and a member is solved by a visit exactly when the run in the reference has met it by that visit (`Conceals.found_within`, `cold_found_within`).
- **What was observed first.** Lean's interpreter evaluated the explorer of seed 1 over the grid's nine actions, and the executed world step from the rung's world along its walks. The walks of the first 54 visits met 99 members, the first count above 98, and those of the first 400 visits met 155 (observed on 2026-11-15 in the cloud container of the session that proved the reduction, with Lean 4.35.0; about four seconds; not proved). No agent ran and no learner was updated: the evaluation read the generator, the draw of a policy whose values are all zero, and the world.
- **Why these four visits.** They are not the first at which the count passes the bound. A search over that evaluation chose them for their few moves, 18 in their 28 steps: taking every move to succeed, no choice of at most five of the first 72 visits that meets 99 members had fewer (observed, by a script outside the tree; no theorem says the choice is the cheapest). The kernel checks the walks themselves.
- **What the proof costs.** Lean's kernel took 5.2 seconds to evaluate the explorer's first 576 decisions (`rung_actions`) and 18.5 seconds to evaluate the world step along the four walks (`walks_traced`); the module took 28 seconds to build (observed on 2026-11-15 in the same container, as the profiler's type-checking time and the build's report). The kernel pays that time each time the module is rebuilt. The module's tables are data: a change to the generator, to the persistent draw, to the world step or to the terrain makes the evaluation fail, and the tables are then computed again from the definitions. Exploring without reward ([#170](https://github.com/rbeauchamp/acorn/issues/170)) would be such a change.
- **What it leaves open.** What the agent does after a first reward, which questions 2 to 4 of [#232](https://github.com/rbeauchamp/acorn/issues/232) ask; the next two sections answer the second for an early first success and say what the first decision after it reads. For another seed or another world the reduction to the explorer's walks holds (`cold_finds_iff`) and no walk is evaluated; for a profile with a hierarchy nothing is proved.

### Whether a first reward's credit reaches the start

This is question 2 of [#232](https://github.com/rbeauchamp/acorn/issues/232): after a first success in a member, does credit reach the start of the next visit. Its test is predicate i′ of [a comment on #140](https://github.com/rbeauchamp/acorn/issues/140#issuecomment-6537838112), which reads the credit where the reward writes it and nothing of the next visit. The *own credit* of the successful visit's start frame is what that visit's first decision laid on the frame's slots, as the reward finds it: with |φ₀| slots, step size α, trace total t₀ before the laying and the reward L steps later, α·|φ₀|·(1 − t₀)·decay^(L−1). At or below the *tie window*, the margin within which the greedy choice treats two values as tied, credit has not reached the start.

- **What is proved.** In the visits of a member, let the rung's agent meet the member's target for the first time at a step of a visit, within its first 1000 decisions. Right after the agent's step on the percept that carries that reward, every slot of the frame laid at the visit's first decision holds, in the row of the action taken there, a weight of at least a named word (`lowChain`): a lower bound on the trace a step lays on a frame of at most 1900 slots (the rung's frames hold at most 1712), decayed once for each later step before the reward. That word is above the tie window (`rung_credit`). Every member the agent solves on one of its first 125 visits has such a first success (`rung_first_credit`), and so has each of the 102 members of `rung_finds`, within the first 72 visits (`rung_found_credit`).
- **How that answers the test.** The word bounds from below what the first decision's own laying puts on each slot, and the weight is at least that. So each slot's share of the frame's own credit is above the tie window by itself (*argued*: the bound on the laid increment is a step of `laid_lower`'s proof and no theorem states it alone. The own credit is stated as an executed word where the visit's first action is taken at no other time from 119 steps before that decision up to the reward: `loop_fresh` and `fresh_total`, read at the start frame, then give the action's whole total there as one word summed over the frame's slots. That is so on the rung's first visit (`rung_early_distinct`, `rung_first_visit`)). Of the frame, this is proved: it holds a slot (`rung_frame_slot`), and right after that step the value of the action taken at the visit's first decision, at the frame laid there, is above the tie window (`rung_start_value`). That value is the row's whole total on the frame, of which the own credit is a part.
- **Why only the first 1000 decisions.** The agent's feature bank holds generated units, each with a slot of its own. Its *tester* replaces the least useful units at a fixed rate, and only units more than 1000 steps old; a replaced unit's slot is *retired*, which clears every learner's weight and trace on it. Through the agent's first 1000 decisions no unit is that old, so no slot is retired between the laying step and the reward (`young_ineligible`, `idle_test`, `young_step`). After that, a slot of the start frame that coincides with a retired one loses its credit; nothing is proved there.
- **What it does not say.**
  - That the weight is still there when the next visit starts, except for a first success on the visit's seventh step: the state the theorem describes is then the one the next visit starts from. More is proved of the agent's next decision, whatever the step. Every two frames of the run share a slot (`rung_frames_share`): a percept carries a word for the phase of the world's day, one of eight, and through a visit's seven steps that phase is the first. So at the next decision the value of the action taken at this visit's first decision, at the frame the next decision reads, is above the tie window (`rung_next_value`). After a seventh step the next decision is the next visit's first; no theorem names a member whose first success is on a seventh step. After an earlier step, the steps that remain in the visit form their errors from the weights this reward wrote and can lower it (*argued*). No question of #232 asks this; the test of question 2 reads the credit where it is written.
  - That the greedy choice at the next visit's start is the action the weight was written for. The rows of later actions can hold more credit on the slots their frames share with the start frame, and the next start frame can differ from this one where the agent's own predictions are fed back (*argued*). Questions 3 and 4 ask what the greedy choice then does.
- **What follows for the scoped changes.** The one-step model of [#171](https://github.com/rbeauchamp/acorn/issues/171) is gated on a no to this question. Nothing proved gives that no: every first success within the agent's first 1000 decisions passes the test on each slot its start frame holds, and the 102 members of `rung_finds` have their first success there. A first success after those decisions is not decided. So the gate is not met by what is proved, and it is not ruled out for a later first success.

### What the first decision after a first reward reads

This is the opening statement of question 3 of [#232](https://github.com/rbeauchamp/acorn/issues/232): after a first success, does the greedy choice away from the route repeat one action. It does not answer the question.

- **What is proved.** Take a member's first success by the rung's agent, within its first 1000 decisions: the agent meets the member's target for the first time at a step of a visit (`FirstSuccess`). A *trace* is what a step lays on the slots of its frame, in the row of weights of the action it took, and what every later step decays; before a first reward the weights are zero and the traces are not. Right after the agent's step on the percept that carries the reward, every weight reads the trace its row held on its slot when that step began (`rung_weights`). So the value of each action at a frame is its row's *trace total* there, the sum of those traces over the frame's slots (`reward_values`). The agent's next action is then decided as before the reward, with those totals in the place of values that were all zero (`rung_choice`, from `first_choice`). If the agent is inside an exploratory run it has committed to, it takes the run's next action. Otherwise it draws once, and unless that draw begins a new exploratory run it takes a *candidate*: an action whose total is within the tie window of the largest (`first_choice_greedy`). Every member the agent solves on one of its first 125 visits has such a first success (`rung_first_choice`), and so has each of the 102 members of `rung_finds` (`rung_found_choice`).
- **What the totals say.** The traces that the first reward turns into weights were laid on steps whose rewards were all zero, by an agent whose actions are the explorer's. So a total is what it would be had the reward not come: it records which actions the agent took before the reward, how long before, and on frames that share how many slots with the frame the decision reads. Two things are proved of it in every world of the agent's interface, from cold initialization, over frames of at most 1999 slots and before a unit of the feature bank matures. Take a frame that shares a slot with a frame of at most 1900 slots laid at most nine steps before the reward. At that frame, an action that the 119 steps before the reward took on no frame holding a slot of it has a zero total and is no candidate (`unlaid_excluded`). And at that frame, where every one of those steps whose frame holds a slot of it took one action, that action is the one candidate (`sole_candidate`). Two frames whose percepts carry a common word share a slot (`shared_slot`).
- **The total of an action taken once.** Under the same conditions, where the agent took an action once, the action's total is one word added up a counted number of times. Two stored numbers enter. The *step size*, about 5·10⁻⁵, is what a step lays on each slot of its frame when the row's traces on that frame sum to zero. The *trace decay*, about 0.89 under the discounted criterion, is the factor by which each later step multiplies every trace. A step that takes an action whose row holds no trace lays the whole step size on each slot of its frame, and each later step that takes another action multiplies those traces by the trace decay, for at least eighty such steps (`fresh_laid`, `fresh_after`, `freshChain_above`). So after those steps every slot of that frame holds one word in that row, the step size decayed once for each of them, and no other slot holds a trace (`freshChain`). In the agent's run this holds of an action taken at one time, at most 81 steps before the reward, and at no other from 119 steps before that time on (`loop_fresh`). The action's total at a frame is then the sum of as many copies of that word as the frame holds slots of the frame the action was taken on, added one copy at a time as the agent adds them (`fresh_total`). In a run whose actions before the reward are pairwise different this holds of every action taken, and an action not taken has a zero total (`loop_totals`): the decision reads the frames only through those counts. One comparison is proved. The last action taken before the reward has the largest word, a step size against step sizes decayed at least once, and the two differ by more than five tie windows. So where the decision's frame holds a slot of that action's frame, and at least as many slots of it as of each earlier frame, that action is the one candidate (`fresh_lead`, `last_candidate`), and when the agent is in no exploratory run and begins none, it takes that action again (`first_choice_last`). The comparison reads the two words as exact numbers and bounds each sum's rounding error (`repeatSum_near`); it evaluates neither sum. A row whose action was taken more than once among those times is outside this: its slots can hold different words, and no theorem gives their sum.
- **What that means on the rung.** Little. Count the agent's decisions from time zero. At each time from 39 to 576 the explorer has taken every one of the nine actions at one of the 119 times before (`rung_taken`), so at a first reward at such a time no action is excluded for not having been taken; no theorem lists the explorer's actions after its first 576. Every two frames of the run share a slot: every percept of a visit carries the same word for the phase of the day, the first of eight, because the rung's world starts at the day's first step and a visit's seven steps stay within the day's first eighth (`rung_frames_share`). So every action taken on those 119 steps can hold a total at every frame, and within the explorer's first 576 decisions `sole_candidate` applies to no first reward: the explorer's first two actions differ, from time 39 it has taken all nine within those steps (`rung_early_distinct`, `rung_taken`), and no member's first reward is at time 1 (`rung_first_visit`); this is *argued* from those theorems. The comparison of the bullet above applies on the rung only on the first visit (*argued*: the explorer's sixth action repeats its fourth). A first success there is at the visit's second or third step: of the explorer's first seven actions only the second and the third name a direction, and after any other the body stands where it stood (`rung_first_visit`). The explorer's first three actions are pairwise different (`rung_early_distinct`), and at the times 3 and 4 it holds no committed run with a step left and begins none (`rung_calm`). So at a first success on the first visit, where the frame the next decision reads holds at least as many slots of the frame laid at the decision whose action met the target as of each earlier frame, the agent's first action after the step that learns the reward is that action again (`rung_early_repeat`). That condition is a hypothesis there. The next bullet says where it is proved: after a first success at the second step. For one at the third step it is a hypothesis. Seven of the 102 targets of `rungFound` lie where the first visit's third step would first meet them if its step east succeeds there, as the same move does on visit 47 (*argued* from `rungWalks`; the first visit's third step is not evaluated). - **After a first success at the first visit's second step.** There the condition is proved and the action is named. A step's percept carries its words and its *symbols*, the codes of the window's tiles and of the task that the feature bank's generated units read. - **At two places outside the rung.** `first_choice_last` holds in every world of the agent's interface, and two small worlds show what it means for the next visit ([`CurrentOnePlace`](../lean/AcornVerif/CurrentOnePlace.lean)). ## How the issues encode this

- **Milestones.** [Rung 1: concealed targets in visits](https://github.com/rbeauchamp/acorn/milestone/1) holds the active rung. [Later rungs](https://github.com/rbeauchamp/acorn/milestone/2) holds what waits for it. [Open the roadmap](https://github.com/rbeauchamp/acorn/milestone/3) holds the work for outside contributors.
- **Labels.** One lane on each issue: `lane:state`, `lane:discover`, `lane:prove`, `lane:world`, `lane:theory`, `lane:community` or `lane:upkeep`. A tracking issue of a rung carries the label rung. An issue off the active rung carries the label parked. `needs-owner-decision` and `needs-statement` say what an issue waits on: the second marks work that needs an exact statement before it can be posted as a mission.
- **Sub-issues** group the parts of one deliverable under its tracking issue.
- **Blocked-by links** give the order between issues.

The issues that can be worked on now are the open issues of the first milestone that no open issue blocks, less what the [order of work](#order-of-work) suspends while the successor of the withdrawn discovery mission ([#283](https://github.com/rbeauchamp/acorn/issues/283)) is designed. Suspended, until the owner has confirmed the successor's plain-word account, are the remaining work of [#232](https://github.com/rbeauchamp/acorn/issues/232) from its design note on, the change with its theorem included, and the algorithms of [#170](https://github.com/rbeauchamp/acorn/issues/170) and [#171](https://github.com/rbeauchamp/acorn/issues/171); the agent supervising the owner's projects decided that on the owner's behalf, and the owner can overrule it. Work that only informs discovery can be worked on, such as the criterion that is the first item of the remaining work of [#232](https://github.com/rbeauchamp/acorn/issues/232).
