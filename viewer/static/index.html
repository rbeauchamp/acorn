Interpretation, precision and limits
For question i, q = ((forecast − finite return) / Eᵢ)², Mᵢ = Σq / nᵢ, Rᵢ = √Mᵢ and agreement = 100(1 − Rᵢ). The aggregate is 100(1 − √(ΣMᵢ / m)); every current question must have settled data. Eᵢ is twice the admitted binary32 prediction horizon. It is fixed for that question; invalid arithmetic is refused, never clipped into a good score.

The finite sum omits a discounted tail bounded by γᴴ/(1 − γ) for unit-bounded signals. The core reports separate conservative normalized tail and rounded-return allowances below. Each is a bound on RMSE displacement; the maximum across questions also bounds aggregate displacement. Agreement endpoints reverse: an RMSE interval [L,U] gives [100(1 − U),100(1 − L)]. These are deterministic truncation and rounding bounds.

Exact integer sums and rational aggregation precede display projection. RMSE projection is downward by less than 0.000001 normalized units (0.0001 agreement points). Text shows hundredths of a point; >99.99 and <0.01 preserve nonzero endpoint distinctions. A literal 100 means zero measured finite-return discrepancy.

Squared loss decomposes into forecast-to-conditional-mean discrepancy plus conditional outcome variance; the conditional mean minimizes it. Unpredictable outcomes can therefore lower agreement even for a correct mean forecast. The displayed score includes both terms.

The stream follows evolving behavior. Comparisons must account for policy changes, distribution changes and censored futures. A stop or world discontinuity discards pending futures and reports censoring. Reward and terrain coverage have their own panels.

Context: Oak Lab mission, learning from experience, and Horde. The formulas above define Acorn's evaluator.
