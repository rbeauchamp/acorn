This file is the confirmation file of the intent record of the postcondition of Acorn's mission contract, what achieving goals promises. It names the record's revision, gives the restatement the owner confirmed with the owner's answers, and gives the status of every consequence of the record, with the reason for each decision made on the owner's behalf. The record, its adequacy proofs and its consequences are Lean declarations, checked by Acorn's merge gate, `./scripts/gate.sh`, and so are the second, blind readings and their comparison with this record: the blind record, in `lean/AcornVerif/Intent/PostconditionBlindReading.lean`, the cases of the cases reading, in `lean/AcornVerif/Intent/PostconditionBlindCases.lean`, and every theorem that compares either with this record, in `lean/AcornVerif/Intent/PostconditionBlindCompare.lean`. This file states nothing a theorem proves except by naming the theorem. A name given without its namespace is in `AcornVerif.Intent.Postcondition`, or, for a theorem of the comparison, in `AcornVerif.Intent.Postcondition.Compare`.

## The restatement the owner confirmed

The owner holds Acorn's intent at the level of the research of Rich Sutton, Khurram Javed and Oak Lab and of the OaK architecture. What the owner confirmed of the postcondition is four answers, given on three days.

**2026-11-03.** In conversation with the agent supervising the owner's projects, the owner was shown a plain restatement of each of Acorn's four contracts, each sentence marked as resting on that research, as the owner's own earlier decision, or as a choice the agents made on the owner's behalf, with three forks and a recommendation for each. Asked whether to confirm the contracts and all three recommendations, the owner answered: "yes, and ensure that each contract has citations and links to their sources". `docs/mission-contract.md`, section "The owner's confirmation of 2026-11-03", records that confirmation and the fork choices, and its section "The postcondition: what achieves goals promises" carries the postcondition's restatement word for word. Without its citations and labels it reads:

1. What the agent pursues is reward, the world's scalar signal.
2. In every class Acorn proves, reaching a member's goal reaches the agent as reward. The class states its whole reward function, how reward is aggregated and how long attempts last, so that a lemma can show, for the agents compared (the learning agent and every agent that keeps nothing of its experience) and on the attempts counted, that one agent earns more reward than another exactly when it solves more members. A promise about reward and a promise about members solved are then the same statement, in both directions.
3. After experience in its one stream, the agent gains more reward per attempt across the class than any agent that keeps nothing of its experience could, which by sentence 2 means it solves more members per attempt.
4. Once it has solved a problem, it keeps solving it while that problem stays the same; beyond its capacity it may give up what it no longer needs, but it keeps the ability to learn it again.
5. Success is measured as reward over the agent's stream. Acorn's first theorems prove that the advantage arrives eventually, and a bound on how long learning takes comes next.
6. Nothing is promised about optimal behaviour or about success on a first encounter.

With that answer the owner decided two forks that shape these sentences. Under fork F-A the promise is stated in reward: success reaches the agent as reward, and each class proves that one agent earns more reward than another exactly when it solves more members, in both directions, while attempt goals stay the countable instrument the proofs use (sentence 2). Under fork F-B the promise is staged: lifetime reward over the agent's stream is the target, the eventual advantage is proved first, and a bound on how long learning takes comes next (sentence 5).

## Decided on the owner's behalf

The restatement leaves these open below the owner's level. Each was decided by the agent that wrote the record, unless another role is named, for the reason given; none is the owner's. Each was checked against the second readings.

- **The room after the attempt of the advantage** (the clause `n + lapses + 1 < horizon` of `earnsMoreInTime`, with `solvesMoreInTime_holds` and `unmet_of_lapses`). The attempt of the advantage leaves more later attempts before the horizon than the lapses. Reason: choice A asks that the advantage come "early enough that the promise of solving again still applies to it", and with no more later attempts than the lapses that promise asks nothing of the members solved on that attempt, since every one of those attempts may be a lapse. With that room each such member is solved again on one of them, where its attempts start from the same state (`advantageSolvedAgain_holds`). Both second readings asked only that the advantage come before the stated attempt (point B); the agent supervising Acorn's research decided on 2026-11-18, and the agent supervising the owner's projects confirmed, that the clause of choice A settles that an advantage after which solving again asks nothing does not meet the promise, and that the exact room stays recorded here as decided on the owner's behalf. Consequences: no agent meets the record in a class whose horizon is at most its lapses and one (`unmet_of_lapses`), and such a class asks no solving again of any agent (`keepsSolving_of_lapses`).
- **Learning again is an eventual solve after a real gap** (the property `learnsAgain`). Decided on the owner's behalf by the agent supervising Acorn's research, on 2026-11-18, and confirmed by the agent supervising the owner's projects, as a correction of the record (point C). A member the agent has solved, whose attempts left the state of the solved attempt and have come back to it for good, is solved again on some later attempt: at least once after the last attempt that started from another state. No attempt is named by which it is, and nothing is asked where the problem never left; once it is solved again, the promise of solving again applies from that attempt. Reason: sentence 4 lets the agent give up what it no longer needs and promises the ability to learn it again, with no time named, and fork F-B stages a bound on how long learning takes for later; the form the record had gave the agent only the lapses and one attempt from the first attempt back, which sentence 4 does not ask. The trial `returning`, whose agent misses the first attempt back and solves from the next one on, has the property as it now stands and failed it as it stood (`returning_learnsAgain`, `returning_not_promptly`, with `returning_leaves` for what the property asks of it); the trial `quitting`, whose agent never solves the member again, fails it (`quitting_fails`). `returning` is a model of this property and `quitting` a refutation of it; each of their agents keeps nothing of its experience (`quits_keepsNothing`, `returns_keepsNothing`), so neither trial meets the whole record. In every class that meets the record of contract 2 the record asks the same before and after the correction (`AcornVerif.Intent.BigClass.achieves_iff_promptly`), and the mission statement holds of a rung exactly when it held of it before (`AcornVerif.Intent.MissionTheorem.mission_iff_before`); this is proved of every class that meets the record of contract 2 and says nothing of a class that does not.
- **What is not asked of the ability to learn again, and stays open.** The record does not ask that the agent could still solve a member that is never put to it again from the solved attempt's state, and it reads nothing from the horizon on where a member's attempts never leave that state: the trial `halted`, whose agent solves no member from the horizon on, meets the record (`mayStopAtHorizon_holds`). The blind record read the ability as kept for the agent's whole life and refuses that trial (`halted_mine_not_theirs`). Whether the ability is promised for the whole life, and its form on campaigns, is obligation 10 of `docs/mission-contract.md`, and is the owner's to decide when a class with campaigns is stated; decided on the owner's behalf by the agent supervising Acorn's research, on 2026-11-18, and confirmed by the agent supervising the owner's projects, to leave it open until then. Reason: for an agent that explores with random draws, a promise with no last attempt cannot be stated as one about all but a fraction of runs, which is why the owner bounded solving again by a stated attempt on 2026-11-17.
- **Who keeps nothing of its experience** (`KeepsNothing`). An agent whose next memory does not depend on what it is given; its action may read what it is given at that step, and its memory may change in a way fixed in advance, as a clock's or a timed script's does. Reason: a clock holds nothing of experience, and a comparison that left timed scripts out would let a script count as learning. The blind record took an agent whose action does not depend on its memory (difference 2). Each such agent earns what an agent that keeps nothing earns, so the record's advantage is one over those agents too (`mine_advantage_theirs`), and an agent that stores what it is given and never reads it fails the record (`actionReadsMemory_holds`).
- **The lemma of sentence 2 is asked across any two attempts, and "the attempts counted" are all of them** (`rewardCountsMembers`). Reason: the advantage compares the agent's attempt with every attempt of an agent that keeps nothing. The blind record asked it on one attempt at a time before the horizon and also on reward summed over the attempts so far (difference 3); agreement of summed reward belongs with lifetime reward, which fork F-B stages for later.
- **The advantage is read on one attempt, in that attempt's reward** (`earnsMoreInTime`). Reason: fork F-B makes lifetime reward the target and the eventual advantage the first step, and sentence 3 speaks of reward per attempt, so the per-attempt reading is what the owner's words settle; that one attempt is asked, and not every attempt from some attempt on, was decided on the owner's behalf, since solving again carries the advantage forward but for the lapses. The cases reading reads it so; the blind record reads it in summed reward held to the horizon (differences 4 and 5).
- **The lapses are counted once for each member** (`keepsSolving`): one list of at most the class's number of attempts, the same whichever earlier attempt solved the member. The blind record allows them anew in each stretch of attempts that start from one state (difference 6). Reason: the owner's sentence gives one number for a member once it is solved. The record does not say why an attempt is a lapse, and reads no report of the agent's about its own exploring.
- **"The problem stays the same" is equality of the member's world's state at the start of the attempts, in the learning agent's run** (`Trial.SameProblem`). Reason: the owner's reading says "while the member's attempts start from the same state". Both second readings read it so. Where the state at an attempt's start depends on what the agent did, an agent's own actions decide whether its problem stays the same; that is left as it is.
- **A goal is a decidable condition on a member's states, and a member is solved on an attempt when its goal is met at some step of the attempt** (`Member.goal`, `Trial.solves`). Reason: sentence 2 speaks of reaching a member's goal, and under fork F-A attempt goals are the countable instrument, so whether an attempt solved a member has to be a yes or a no. The blind record computes the same (`solvedB_eq`).
- **The agents compared each run alone in each member, from their initial memory** (`Member.run`), which is contract 1's faithful run never stopped (`run_reaches`). Reason: an agent cannot tell which member it is in, so the class asks one agent to serve each member from the same start, and the mission contract's design reads separation and retention over such separate runs. Campaigns of several members in one stream are left to obligation 10. The blind record's run is the same (`run_eq`).
- **The comparison counts a finite list of members the class names, while solving again and learning again are asked of every member** (`Trial.counted`). Reason: "more members" needs a count, which a class with infinitely many members does not give, while sentence 4 speaks of any problem the agent has solved. The blind record counts the same list (`classSolved_eq`).
- **An advantage on a first attempt is not excluded.** The record does not ask that an attempt come before the attempt of the advantage. The cases reading left that open (its case 23). Reason: sentence 6 promises nothing about a first encounter and forbids nothing, and in a class that conceals its goals no agent has such an advantage.
- **The shape.** The subject is declared a plain value (`.value`), of which lean-intent's O4 asks nothing (`postcondition_o4`). The blind record declared a description made of functions (`.bundle`); lean-intent's obligation for that shape asks every function to vary with its input, and the record admits a world or an agent that is constant, as the records of contracts 1 and 3 do (difference 1).
- **The subject's type** (`Trial`). A class of worlds with what the class states and the agent that learns in it: the members, each a deterministic world with a rational reward and a goal; the members counted; the attempt length; the aggregation; the horizon and the lapses. One horizon and one number of lapses for the whole class, as natural numbers, is the form the record's writer gave the stated attempt and the stated number. The record session was given this type, so it could not differ from it; the cases session was not.
