## The restatement the owner confirmed

The owner holds Acorn's intent at the level of the research of Rich Sutton, Khurram Javed and Oak Lab and of the OaK architecture. On 2026-11-03, in conversation with the agent supervising the owner's projects, the owner was shown a plain restatement of each of Acorn's four contracts, each sentence marked as resting on that research, as the owner's own earlier decision, or as a choice the agents made on the owner's behalf, with three forks and a recommendation for each. Asked whether to confirm the contracts and all three recommendations, the owner answered: "yes, and ensure that each contract has citations and links to their sources". `docs/mission-contract.md`, section "The owner's confirmation of 2026-11-03", records that confirmation and the fork choices, and its section "Contract 2: what makes a class of worlds big" carries contract 2's restatement word for word. Without its citations and labels it reads:

1. A world is big relative to an agent: the agent is far smaller than the world in the resources its task needs, so it can neither fully perceive the world's state nor represent the value or best action for every state.
2. Bigness belongs to the pair, and a class can be made big by restricting the agent instead of enlarging the world.
3. A big world need not change by itself: a fixed world can appear to change to an agent that cannot represent a good solution everywhere, and when its experience is temporally coherent, continuing to learn can then do better than any fixed solution.
4. Acorn states bigness as conditions that a class of worlds can be proved to meet. (a) Every goal can be reached from where its attempt starts. This does not rule out traps: an action may still lead somewhere the goal can no longer be reached. (b) Until a goal is first reached, nothing the agent perceives tells it which goal it faces. (c) An agent that keeps nothing from its experience solves only a small share of the class on any attempt. (d) The class poses more separate problems than the agent's memory can hold solutions to at once, so the agent must keep learning and give up what it no longer needs.
5. Today's proved class meets (a) to (c); (d) is the target for larger classes.
6. Each class must also let the agent learn: success must show in what the agent perceives, and experience must be coherent enough over time for continued learning to pay.
7. Each class is a step toward "any big world", never a narrowing of it.

## Decided on the owner's behalf

The restatement leaves these open below the owner's level. Each was decided by the agent that wrote the record, unless another role is named, and kept after the comparison for the reason given; none is the owner's.

- **Condition (d) is not a property of this record.** It speaks of how many separate problems the agent's memory can hold solutions to at once, and in this subject each agent runs alone in each member, so it never holds two members' solutions at once; (d) needs a subject that presents several members in one stream. Kept on 2026-11-18 by the agent supervising Acorn's research: this change does not add it, and its form and the rung that first proves it wait on the order of the later rungs (rbeauchamp/acorn#234). By the owner's answer of 2026-11-18 a class is big only with (d), so no class is shown big by meeting this record. Both blind readings require (d); the cases read it as more independently varying answers than the agent's memory can hold, and judge a class with one answer repeated in every context, or an agent whose memory holds the whole table, not big (cases 3 and 2).
- **No property reads the agent.** A trial meets the record with one agent exactly when it meets it with any other (`AcornVerif.Intent.BigClass.reads_class`), one that keeps nothing of its experience included (`agentMayKeepNothing_holds`). Reason: sentence 4 states the conditions of a class of worlds, and of (a) to (c) and sentence 6 only what the class gives is asked; the condition that reads the agent's memory is (d). The cases judge a pair whose agent has no memory as failing the second half of sentence 6 (case 13), reading "let the agent learn" of the supplied agent. The record asks it of the class: sentence 6's subject is "each class", and what follows its colon is what the class must give, a success that shows and an experience coherent enough. That the pair's own agent does learn is the postcondition's promise, which the mission's statement asks beside this record (`AcornVerif.Intent.MissionTheorem.Mission`), so no pair with an agent that cannot learn meets the mission's statement.
- **"Coherent enough over time" is recurrence: every attempt of every agent's run in a member starts from the state the member's world starts in** (the property `attemptsRecur`). With the record's other properties it gives what the sentence asks, that continued learning pays: some agent that keeps what its percept showed solves every counted member from some attempt on, which no agent that keeps nothing does on any attempt (`learningPays_holds`). The reading holds only of classes whose worlds restart by themselves after a fixed number of steps. A class with a member that leaves its start and has no restart, one whose restart waits on an action of the agent, and one whose attempts end early and leave the member elsewhere when the next begins, fail the record. The owner decided on 2026-11-03, as fork F-C, that a class of worlds with no restarts joins the ladder later (`docs/mission-contract.md`, section "The owner's confirmation of 2026-11-03"), so the record states coherence for the classes of today's rung, and a statement for continuing worlds is later work. The cases reading shows what that leaves out: its worlds go on from one attempt to the next, and it finds experience coherent where an answer lasts long enough to be found and used (case 1) and not where a fresh answer comes at every step (case 15); the record refuses both.
- **A goal must be reachable within an attempt** (the property `goalsReachable`): by fewer actions than an attempt has steps, from the state each attempt starts in. Reason: sentence 4(a) speaks of where "its attempt" starts and sentence 4(c) of what is solved "on any attempt"; a goal that can be reached only after its attempt cannot be solved on it. The blind record sets no limit, and the cases leave the question open (cases 16 and 19). Where attempts recur the two readings agree (`AcornVerif.Intent.BigClass.Compare.reachable_of_theirs`); the class `far` separates them (`far_theirs`, `far_not_mine`), so by that theorem its attempts do not recur.
- **An attempt of n steps has n times: its start and the states after all but its last step.** This is the subject's convention, the postcondition's record's, and the last step of an attempt is the one by which a class that meets this record is back at its start. Both blind readings count the state after the last step too: an attempt of two steps reaches a goal two actions away (case 17), and an attempt of no step holds its start (case 18). Where attempts recur the readings agree on what is solved (`AcornVerif.Intent.BigClass.Compare.memorylessSolves_iff`); the record's verdicts on cases 17 to 19 show where they part (`c17`, `c18`, `c19`). An attempt of no step has no time, so in such a class nothing is reachable and nothing is solved (`unreachable_of_zero`, `solves_of_zero`).
- **A goal already met where an attempt starts counts as reached and as solved.** The list of no action reaches it, and the first time of an attempt counts. The cases reading counts an already satisfied goal as success too. Condition (c) limits such members as it limits any member a fixed way solves.
- **Condition (b) is stated with one reference world** that every member looks like, in observation and in reward, until its goal is first met. Reason: it is the form the kernel's proofs use, and it gives the consequence that nobody does well on a first attempt (`firstAttemptSmall_holds`).
- **Condition (c) is a count, and "small" is below the count of the counted members.** Decided on 2026-11-18 by the agent supervising Acorn's research, with the correction below. The counted members are distinct, and a number below their count bounds how many members of the class an agent that keeps nothing solves on any attempt; how small the share is, is the margin between that number and the count. The record states no measure, so a class in which such an agent solves infinitely many members fails (c) whatever share of the class they are, and a class with no member fails it (`AcornVerif.Intent.BigClass.Cases.c20`). Reason: the words give no threshold, as both blind readings found, and sentence 5 says that today's proved class meets (c): that class is proved with a covering bound of 98 and more counted members than that (`AcornVerif.CurrentConcealedTarget.witness_big`, `AcornVerif.CurrentConcealedTarget.witness_separation`), so a threshold that 98 of its members would fail contradicts the owner's sentence.
- **A fixed solution, in sentence 3, is an agent that keeps nothing of its experience**, the agent of condition (c): its memory moves the same way whatever it perceives, though its action may read what it is given at that step and it may count its steps. The other reading the blind record session named, a solution that starts every attempt afresh, is bounded by the same number (`freshStartsSmall_holds`).
- **Success shows in what the agent is given at that step**: one function of an observation with a reward, the same for every member, says at every time whether the goal is met then. A class in which success shows only in a later percept, or only in a history of percepts, fails the property. Reason: the seeker of `learningPays_holds` needs only this, and a world can carry in its state what a later percept would show. Neither blind reading gave a case that separates the two.
- **Sentences 3 and 7 add no property.** Sentence 3 says what continuing to learn "can" do, which the record proves as a consequence. Sentence 7 speaks of how Acorn chooses its classes; the record carries it by leaving a class's observations, actions and members free and by having no domain hypothesis. The blind record states both as requirements under meanings it leaves open; the cases reading reads sentence 7 as the direction of the programme and no requirement of a class.
- **The shape.** The subject is declared a plain value, of which lean-intent's O4 asks nothing (`bigClass_o4`). lean-intent's shape for a description made of functions asks every function to vary with its input, and the record admits members whose observation, reward or dynamics are constant. The blind record declares a bundle.

## The correction of condition (c)

Sentence 4(c) says that an agent that keeps nothing from its experience solves only a small share of the class. As first merged, the record bounded what such an agent solves among the counted members only, and said nothing of the members a class does not count. The class `lopsided`, two-door members named by the natural numbers, all with the first door but one, that counts two of them, met that record, while the agent that always tries the first door solves every member but one (`lopsided_always`). The words settle it, both blind readings take the share of the class, and the kernel's own form of (c) bounds the whole class. So the property was corrected in this change: the counted members are distinct, and a number below their count bounds every list of distinct members of the class that an agent that keeps nothing solves on one attempt. The correction follows from the owner's confirmed sentence; its counting form was decided on the owner's behalf by the agent supervising Acorn's research on 2026-11-18. `lopsided` now fails condition (c) and that condition alone (`lopsided_fails`, `lopsided_others`). A class in visits meets the corrected condition under the hypotheses it met the old one under (`AcornVerif.MissionVisits.visits_frozenShareSmall`), so `AcornVerif.MissionVisits.visits_big` and every theorem stated over the record keep their statements. The corrected condition gives the old bound over the counted members (`counted_bound`).
