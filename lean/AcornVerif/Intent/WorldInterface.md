# Confirmation of the interface record

This file is the confirmation file of the intent record of contract 1 of Acorn's mission contract, the interface every world meets. It names the record's revision, gives the restatement the owner confirmed with the owner's answer, and gives the status of every consequence of the record that its explanation cannot show, with the reason for each decision made on the owner's behalf. The record, its adequacy proofs and its consequences are Lean declarations, checked by Acorn's merge gate, `./scripts/gate.sh`, and so are the second reading's record and cases and their comparison with this record, in `lean/AcornVerif/Intent/WorldInterfaceBlindReading.lean` and `lean/AcornVerif/Intent/WorldInterfaceBlindCases.lean`; this file states nothing a theorem proves except by naming the theorem.

## The restatement the owner confirmed

The owner holds Acorn's intent at the level of the research of Rich Sutton, Khurram Javed and Oak Lab and of the OaK architecture. On 2026-11-03, in conversation with the agent supervising the owner's projects, the owner was shown a plain restatement of each of Acorn's four contracts, each sentence marked as resting on that research, as the owner's own earlier decision, or as a choice the agents made on the owner's behalf, with three forks and a recommendation for each. Asked whether to confirm the contracts and all three recommendations, the owner answered: "yes, and ensure that each contract has citations and links to their sources". `docs/mission-contract.md`, section "The owner's confirmation of 2026-11-03", records that confirmation and the fork choices, and its section "Contract 1: the interface every world meets" carries contract 1's restatement, word for word apart from sentence 7, which carries the rewording of the owner's decision F3 that the owner chose under fork F-C. Without its citations and labels it reads:

1. Each step, the world sends the agent an observation and a scalar reward, and the agent answers with one of the actions the world makes available.
2. The set of actions is fixed in advance for each world.
3. Any further signal a world provides, such as a task signal, is part of the observation.
4. The observation need not reveal the world's state, and the world generates the reward outside the agent's direct control.
5. Everything the agent learns comes from these signals, never from the world's internal variables.
6. No host, viewer or tool writes what the agent learns or decides; the viewer can only stop it.
7. A world may restart, between attempts or by its own rules, as part of its own dynamics, and a restart may be the agent's own action, as ARC-AGI-3's RESET is; the agent never restarts, and its memory is never reset.
8. Every world Acorn runs (the tile world, each ARC-AGI-3 game and the Microduck) meets this one interface.

The agents wrote these words from a research report on that research, which a second model from another provider audited; the owner adopted them by confirming them. The owners' log keeps whose words they are as the origin statement `contract-1-origin`, and the owner's answer as the statement `contract-1-restatement`, both dated 2026-11-03 and holding the owner's words above as their source (`AcornVerif.Intent.Decisions.ownersLog`).

What the owner confirmed is this restatement as a whole and the three fork choices, before the record existed. The owner did not see the record, its rendering or any consequence derived from it, and nothing here presents them as the owner's. The log's statement `contract-1-restatement` attaches the owner's answer to the record's current revision, because lean-intent's confirmation check asks each revision whether its rendering restates the owner's intent and counts only an answer naming that rendering; the agents attached it after checking every consequence below against the restatement. Attaching it was decided on the owner's behalf by the agent supervising the owner's projects, which judged the record's restatement to be word for word the eight sentences above; it is the agents' decision, not the owner's, and no further question was put to the owner. Its date is the day the owner answered, not the day the rendering was made. So lean-intent's rendering of this record prints its restatement as confirmed by the owner and each consequence shown as derived from that: what the owner confirmed is the eight sentences above, and that this revision carries them is the agents' judgment. lean-intent's check decides that the answer is a declared owner's and names this revision; it does not check that the owner made it as its source says, or that a consequence recorded as derived follows from the restatement. Both are trusted, with this file and the history of the change that committed them.

## Decided on the owner's behalf

The restatement leaves these open below the owner's level. Each was decided by the agent that carried out the work, unless another role is named, for the reason given; none is the owner's.

- **The run can take its first step** (the property `canBegin`). Some outside input lets the run take a step from the world's start and the agent's initial memory. Both second readings found that the other properties hold emptily of a system that can take no step; the record reading required this property and the cases reading left it open (its case 23). Reason: every sentence of the restatement speaks of what happens at a step, so a system that can take none says nothing of the interface, and this property asks nothing of how long a run goes on, which hosts decide.
- **The viewer's stop takes effect eventually, not at once** (the property `viewerCanStop`). If the viewer keeps asking the run to stop, it stops at that step or a later one. The record reading asked a stop request to stop the run at once, at every state. Reason: sentence 6 says what the viewer can do, not how soon, and the mission contract's design of this contract has a stop request enter the core at an attempt boundary; a run that defers the stop by one step, `AcornVerif.Intent.WorldInterface.Compare.deferredStop`, meets this record and not the stricter reading (`AcornVerif.Intent.WorldInterface.Compare.deferredStop_meets_mine` and `AcornVerif.Intent.WorldInterface.Compare.deferredStop_fails_theirs`, which build in `./scripts/gate.sh`).
- **The available actions are the same at every state of the world, reached or not** (the property `fixedActions`). The cases reading left open a world whose available actions differ only at a state no run reaches (its case 34). Reason: "fixed in advance for each world" is a property of the world, decided before any run, so it does not depend on which states a run reaches.
- **The agent's own answer may return its memory to its initial value** (the consequence `ownAnswerMayReturnToInitial`, proved by `ownAnswerMayReturnToInitial_holds`). Decided on the owner's behalf by the agent supervising Acorn's research, after the cases reading judged that sentence 7 forbids an agent from clearing its own memory when it resets the world (its case 18). Contract 1's record reads "its memory is never reset" at the interface, where the agent's memory is opaque, as a property of the run: at every step a run takes, nothing a host, a viewer, a tool or a world's restart does, the agent's own RESET action included, leaves the agent a memory other than the one its own answer produced, so each step begins with the memory the agent's own previous answer produced (`keepsOwnMemory`). Two reasons tie this to the owner's intent. No property of what a run does at the steps it takes can tell an agent that clears its memory at a restart from one whose memory merely returns to its starting value (an agent that counts steps modulo two, in a world of two-step attempts, returns to its initial memory at every restart), so a ban stated in contract 1 would also refute agents that never reset. And the confirmed postcondition compares the learning agent with every agent that keeps nothing of its experience, so those agents must meet this interface. That the agent itself keeps what it has learned across every attempt is sentence 4 of contract 3, the same decision F3, which `docs/mission-contract.md` states across contract 1's sentence 7 and contract 3's sentence 4; contract 3's record (rbeauchamp/acorn#215) carries it. This record does not forbid an agent from clearing its own memory.
- **The subject's type.** A subject is one world, one agent and the run that couples them (`AcornVerif.Intent.WorldInterface.System`), each world bringing its own types for its states, observations, actions, the agent's memories and the run's outside inputs, so that one record speaks of every world and names no type of any one. The world is a deterministic state machine, one of the departures the owner's confirmation of 2026-11-03 lists as Acorn's (a stochastic world read as a deterministic one with hidden draws). The reward is a rational number: every reward a world Acorn runs computes is a finite machine number, which is a rational, and a rational is one scalar. The run gives the agent one observation and one reward and calls its answer once at each step, receives one outside input at each step, and is described at each place a host could interpose (what it gives the agent, which action it hands the world, how the world moves, which memory the agent keeps, whether it takes the step), so that a run that interposes at any of these places, at a step it takes, with a result other than the faithful run's, is a subject the record refutes. An intervention written into the world's or the agent's own fields is invisible to the record; that those fields are the world's and the agent's is trusted.
- **The shape.** The subject is declared a plain value (`.value`), of which lean-intent's O4 asks nothing (`worldInterface_o4`). lean-intent's shape for a description made of functions asks every function to vary with its input, and this interface admits a world whose observation or reward is constant and an agent whose answer is constant; the record reading reached the same conclusion. A run that passes no signal where the world sends one fails the properties themselves: a run that, at a step it takes, gives the agent anything but the world's observation and reward fails `seesObservation` or `getsWorldReward`.
- **Sentence 8.** Sentence 8 quantifies over the worlds Acorn runs, so it is no property of one system. The record carries it as its scope: one record, over a subject type that admits a world of any types, which each world Acorn runs is to meet (`EveryWorldMeets` states it for a collection of systems). That the tile world's system meets it is the fifth step of rbeauchamp/acorn#213; that ARC-AGI-3 games and Microduck scenes do is obligation 13 of `docs/mission-contract.md`, to be proved with their classes. The record reading stated sentence 8 the same way, parameterized by the collection.
- **What the agent's initial memory holds.** The record constrains what the run gives the agent from its first step on, not how the agent was built (the cases reading agreed, its case 28). Reason: sentence 5 speaks of what the agent learns, and what Acorn's agent holds before its first step is governed by Acorn's learned-only binding (`docs/learned-only-binding.md`).
